The Ten-Hour Plan

What we charge, why, and the two things we still haven’t decided. Every figure below is measured off our own recordings on Opus 5.5 — not estimated.

Why it splits this way

The pen costs us $48 landed — $40 for the recorder, $8 to post it. On the yearly plan the $144 covers that on day one and leaves $92 in the bank, so we never finance hardware out of pocket. On monthly the customer buys it outright, so we don’t either. Neither plan has us lending anyone $48 and hoping.

Question one

Why ten hours

Not because ten is where the money runs out. We don’t lose money on a $15 subscriber until 34 hours a month. Ten is a choice about what the number says to a realtor, not a limit the cost structure imposes.

Six hours would have been too tight

Question two

Why the pen is free on yearly and $50 on monthly

We looked at four ways to charge for hardware. The one currently live on the site — $45 covering the pen and the first three months — is the weakest of them.

The $45 bundle earns sixty cents more over a year than simply giving the pen away — and for those sixty cents it puts a $45 payment in front of the hardest step in the funnel, and makes it impossible for a customer to tell what the pen costs and what the software costs.

The thing we hadn’t considered

Agents work in offices of thirty to two hundred people. The old plan gave the cold-email recipient a free pen and charged the colleague they referred $45. That conversation happens in the office kitchen. A referral scheme should reward the referrer, not tax the referred — and that structure would suppress exactly the referrals we’re counting on. Free-on-yearly is fair to everyone, because it’s earned the same way by anybody.

The campaign

Don’t post free pens to strangers

A free pen to a cold-email lead is the most expensive customer acquisition available to us and the least qualified lead it buys.

Free pens only pay off if more than 47% of the people who accept one go on to pay for a full year. Nobody converts 47% of people who took free hardware from a stranger. We’d be selecting for people who like free pens, not people who have the problem.

Lead with the software. They already own a recorder.

The 92-minute meeting that produced these numbers was an .m4a off a phone. The pen is a convenience, not a requirement — so the trial costs us nothing to run, and it tests the question that actually matters.

Two weeks free. Record your next showing on your phone, we’ll write it up.

No pen, no card, no call. Reply and I’ll send you a link.

If it’s useful, the pen’s $50 — or free if you pay for the year.

Hardware cost per lead: zero. We can email ten thousand instead of one thousand. The pen becomes the upsell rather than the bait — someone who has had two weeks of good notes buys a $50 pen without thinking, while someone who has had a free pen for two weeks has no decision left to make, so makes none.

Unresolved

Two things we still haven’t decided

What happens at hour eleven

Breakeven is 34 hours and we’re including 10, so there is no margin to defend here — which is the argument for not metering. Charging to record teaches an agent to record less, and recording is the whole habit. Recommendation: soft-cap at 10, ship nothing, watch a quarter of real usage. If a bundle is needed later, make it a purchase rather than a meter — +5 hours for $9, per hour, never per minute. A 10-minute recording costs us more per minute than an hour-long one, so per-minute pricing undercharges the expensive shape.

The referral reward

Raised on the call and never settled: a free year for five referrals was floated, then the question “what do we charge the person being referred?” went unanswered. Now that the pen is free on yearly, the obvious shape is a free month per paying referral — but nobody has agreed it, and it should not go on the page until somebody does.

Where these numbers come from. Cost is measured, not estimated: 303 dialogue tokens per minute and 3,540 output tokens, counted on the real 92-minute recording from 22 September. Transcription is AssemblyAI at $0.21/hr plus $0.02/hr diarization. Notes run on Claude Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 per million tokens — 30% cheaper and 40% faster than Opus 5, swapped the same day after testing all four models on that transcript.

Stripe is 2.9% + $0.30 per charge. The pen is $40 retail plus $8 postage. “Typical use” is 4 hours a month; the realistic spread is 45% at 1.5 hrs, 30% at 4, 15% at 9, 7% at 16, 3% at 30. Year-one figures assume the customer stays twelve months and exclude shared infrastructure, which is about $80/month in total and falls below a dollar per user past a hundred customers.
