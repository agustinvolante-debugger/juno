Taiwan interdiction

What breaks, in what order, if the island holding most of the world's advanced-process capacity stops shipping — and what can this corpus honestly say about the cost?

04 Fab & Tooling→05 Compute Silicon→07 Systems & Integration→08 Cloud & Neoclouds
Trigger
A blockade, quarantine or shipping interdiction halting Taiwan's export flow. This page argues the transmission mechanism only; the corpus holds nothing that would support a probability.
Tripwire
TSMC moving a Taiwan shipment interruption from a risk factor to a current-period disclosure in a 20-F or 6-K. Absent that, the only published proxy is Taiwan's advanced-capacity share failing to fall on its projected 2027 path.
Standing
Low — argued here, not predicted

Every other scenario on this site is argued from disclosures. This one cannot be, and the useful thing it can do is say exactly where the evidence stops.

1.The exposure, which is measurable

Taiwan held 66% of global advanced-process foundry capacity — 16/14nm and below — in 2024, against Korea at 11%, the United States at 10% and China at 9% [s]. That is a 2024 baseline on a 2026 page. The same analysis projects the Taiwanese share falling toward the mid-fifties by 2027 as US share roughly doubles, but that projection sits in the corpus's annotations rather than in a citable fact, so this page treats it as directional and does not print it. Rhodium separately put Taiwan's share of sub-10nm logic at 92% [e], an estimate dated 2022.

The tighter chokepoint is above the wafer. C.C. Wei said on TSMC's Q2 2026 call that the company's advanced packaging capacity was limiting its customers' growth [s] — a named CEO stating that his own packaging line caps demand. CoWoS lead times ran 52 to 78 weeks with capacity booked through end-2026 [e], and NVIDIA's 2026 allocation is estimated at about 595,000 wafers, roughly 60% of the total [e].

Two structural facts make substitution slow. ASML plans to ship around 65 Low-NA EUV systems in 2026, with about 30% more capacity planned for 2027 [f] — an annual flow, not an installed base, and the hard ceiling on how fast anyone builds a replacement. And Taiwan itself is fragile before the fabs are: it imports about 97% of its energy and holds roughly two weeks of emergency supply [s]. The grid fails before the cleanrooms do.

2.What this corpus cannot support

There is no AI-era model of this event in the research corpus, and this page will not manufacture one.

What exists is two estimates that measure different things. Rhodium Group estimated that a Chinese blockade halting all Taiwan trade would put well over $2 trillion of global economic activity at risk [f] — activity at risk, which Rhodium itself calls a conservative and partial estimate excluding second-order effects, escalation and sanctions. Bloomberg Economics models something else entirely: a first-year GDP loss. Its figures are paywalled at source, carried in the corpus as a dispute flag, and not printed here. Averaging the two would be a category error, and neither uses AI-era trade weights — both predate the buildout that makes the question urgent. The honest output is therefore a mechanism, not a magnitude. Anyone quoting a dollar cost for an AI-era interdiction is extrapolating, and should say so.

3.The path the shock takes

Fab and tooling to compute silicon. NVIDIA's data-center revenue was $75.2bn in the quarter ended April 2026 [s] at a 74.9% gross margin [s], and essentially all of it passes through Taiwanese wafers and Taiwanese packaging. Below that sit substrates, where four Taiwanese and two Japanese suppliers were seeking upfront commitments for Intel's EMIB capacity [s], and ABF build-up film, whose maker reported a segment business profit margin above 50% with its next plant not operating until 2032 [s]. The deepest layer has the longest replacement time.

Silicon to systems. Rack integration is Taiwanese too. Hon Hai turned NT$8.10tn of 2025 revenue at a 6.15% gross margin [s], with Cloud and Networking Products at 48% of Q1 2026 revenue [s]. Foxconn says it runs more than 40 US facilities across 12 states [s], but discloses no split of AI-rack output by geography — so that footprint cannot be scored as substitution capacity.

Systems to cloud. The buyers have signed for capacity with no alternate supply: $633bn of Microsoft remaining performance obligations [s] against volume GPU lead times of 36 to 52 weeks on an industry backlog of roughly 3.6 million units [e]. In this scenario the depreciation argument inverts — installed hardware becomes more valuable, not less.

4.Who says this matters less than it looks

Diversification is real and partly funded. TSMC's announced US investment reached $265bn [s]. Intel put 18A into high-volume production at the start of 2026 [s] and produced the first commercial logic product on High-NA EUV [s].

The counter to the counter is cost and time. US fab construction runs four to five times the Taiwan baseline [e], and N2 contributed just 3% of TSMC wafer revenue in Q2 2026, its first quarter of contribution [s] — leading-edge share moves slowly even when the money is committed. China's alternative is further back still: SMIC's entire 2025 revenue was $9.33bn at a 21.0% gross margin [s], on advanced-node capacity conservatively estimated at 45,000 wafers a month [e].

No named expert in this corpus argues the probability of this event in either direction. The site's expert prediction ledger failed its attribution audit and is quarantined, so it cannot be cited. That absence is why the standing here is low rather than contested — not because the risk is small, but because nothing licenses a stronger claim.

5.What would falsify this

  • Taiwan's advanced-process capacity share falls faster than the projected 2027 path, in the next annual TrendForce update, with US share ahead of schedule. Concentration is the whole scenario.
  • The CoWoS supply-demand gap closes and lead times fall inside a year. A packaging chokepoint that stops being a chokepoint removes the sharpest link.
  • A non-Taiwan advanced-packaging line qualifies at volume for a top-three accelerator customer. Announced capacity is not qualified capacity; a shipping product is.
  • Intel 14A lands committed external customers. Its own 10-Q still describes 14A external demand as uncommitted; that changing would give the West a second leading-edge source rather than a second fab.
