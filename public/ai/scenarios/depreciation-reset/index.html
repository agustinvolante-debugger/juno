scenario

The depreciation reset

What happens to hyperscaler earnings if the assumed useful life of an AI server compresses back toward four years?

08 Cloud & Neoclouds→16 Capital & Policy→05 Compute Silicon
Trigger
One more large operator follows Amazon in shortening server lives — or an auditor forces the question — turning a private divergence of judgement into a sector-wide reset of the depreciation schedule.
Tripwire
A useful-life change disclosed in the accounting-policy note of a Microsoft, Alphabet or Meta 10-K or 10-Q, with the dollar effect on depreciation quantified in the same note.
Standing
Already happening — in part

This is the cheapest way for the buildout to disappoint. It needs no demand shock, no failed model, no bankruptcy — only an accounting estimate moving in the direction one of the four largest operators has already moved it.

1.The mechanism

Servers are capitalised and written off over an assumed useful life. Lengthen the assumption and reported operating income rises with no change in the business; shorten it and income falls the same way. The sensitivity is roughly $7bn of operating income per year, per year of change [e]. Two qualifiers travel with that figure and this page will not print it without them: it was published as an illustrative example using approximate hyperscaler figures, and it is singular, not a big-four total.

What makes the estimate load-bearing now is the size of the pool it applies to. Combined big-four capital expenditure over the trailing four quarters to March 2026 ran at approximately 2.9 times the depreciation and finance-lease amortization behind it [e]. Strip Amazon's bundled capitalised video content out of that denominator and the ratio goes above three. Every dollar of the gap becomes depreciation later, on a schedule nobody has agreed on.

2.Why this is already happening, in part

January 2025 is the month the consensus broke, and it broke in filings rather than in commentary. Meta extended server and network useful life to 5.5 years, lowering depreciation by about $2.9bn [s]. Amazon went the other way in the same month, shortening a subset of servers and networking equipment from six years to five, taking $889m of additional depreciation and $677m off net income in the nine months to September 2025 [s].

That comparison is the whole argument. Two companies buying similar machines for similar customers, in the same quarter, disagreed about how long the machines last. The reset is therefore not a hypothesis about the future — it is a disclosed accounting change at one operator, waiting to see whether the other three follow.

The direction of travel before that was uniformly the other way. Microsoft went from four years to six effective fiscal 2023 [s]; Alphabet to six, worth about $3.4bn of depreciation [s]; Amazon to six, worth about $3.2bn [s]. Each was defensible alone. Together they lengthened the industry's assumed asset life exactly as the assets began changing fastest. And the extension does not stop the expense: Meta's depreciation on property and equipment still rose from about $11.0bn in 2023 to $18.0bn in 2025 despite the longer life [s].

3.The path the shock takes

Cloud first, because the charge lands on the income statement. A one-year compression is roughly $7bn a year of operating income per operator-year of change, against businesses where cash is already tight at the margin — Amazon's trailing-twelve-month capex equalled 102% of operating cash flow [s].

Then capital and policy, because the leveraged players have no software revenue to absorb it. CoreWeave posts a 56% adjusted EBITDA margin and a $740m GAAP quarterly net loss simultaneously [s]; depreciation and interest consume the middle of the P&L. Its $8.5bn March 2026 facility sits in a bankruptcy-remote subsidiary secured on substantially all of that subsidiary's assets [s] — the GPUs are the lien — with a 1.15x debt-service-coverage covenant and a 2032 maturity [s]. Shorter economic lives revalue that collateral against a six-year tenor. Nebius shows the reflex in the other direction: it extended server life from four to five years effective Q1 2026, in a quarter when depreciation and amortization rose to 40% of operating expenses [s].

Then compute silicon, where the sign flips. Shorter useful lives mean faster replacement cycles. The same event that cuts reported cloud earnings raises near-term accelerator demand. Anyone arguing this scenario as uniformly bearish has not followed it to the bottom of the stack.

4.Who says this will not happen

Michael Burry makes the aggressive version — hyperscalers understating depreciation by about $176bn across 2026 to 2028 [o]. He is a named short-seller with positions, and the counter-evidence is real.

Azure retired V100 GPUs roughly 7.5 years after launch [s], which is the single best datum against "GPUs are worthless in four years." Newer silicon is not following the deflation curve either: B200 rentals ran $4.50–7.00 an hour in Q2 2026 with marketplace rates rising month over month [s], and one-year reserved H100 pricing bottomed at $1.70 in October 2025 before rebounding about 38% to $2.35 by March 2026 [s]. Demand is contracted, not hoped for: Microsoft reported $633bn of remaining performance obligations while stating that demand continues to exceed supply [s], and Alphabet's total RPO reached $519.5bn [s]. Amy Hood's position is the strongest one available here — you do not sign that and then not need the machines.

5.What would falsify this

  • Microsoft, Alphabet or Meta extends again rather than shortens in its next annual accounting-policy note. Three operators lengthening against one shortening makes Amazon the outlier, not the leading indicator.
  • Amazon reverses back to six years on the subset it shortened, and says why in the filing.
  • Reserved pricing on current-generation parts keeps rising through 2027 while Hopper capacity stays on contract past its seventh year. Residual value is the whole question, and the rental market prices it weekly.
  • The capex-to-depreciation ratio falls toward two without a capex cut — meaning depreciation caught up on the existing schedule and there is nothing left to reset.
