scenario

A compute buyer cannot pay

What if one of the small number of counterparties underwriting the buildout cannot fund its commitments, and the sellers have to reprice a backlog built on one name?

16 Capital & Policy→08 Cloud & Neoclouds→07 Systems & Integration→05 Compute Silicon
Trigger
A round, an IPO or a contingent tranche that does not land — forcing a renegotiation, deferral or termination of contracted compute purchases by a counterparty that several sellers' backlogs depend on.
Tripwire
A contract modification, deferral or impairment disclosed against a named large customer by Oracle, Microsoft, CoreWeave or Nebius — or a fall in Oracle's remaining performance obligations not explained by delivery.
Standing
Contested — named people disagree

Start by throwing out the version of this argument you have already heard, because it is wrong in a way that discredits the real one.

1.The bad version, and why it fails

The popular framing sets Sam Altman's $1.4 trillion of data-center commitments against OpenAI's revenue and calls the gap a bubble. Do not. That figure is Altman's own attributed statement, covering roughly 30 GW over about eight years [o]. It is not a sum of disclosed contracts, the balance is not publicly itemised, and setting an eight-year commitment against one year of revenue compares unlike things. This site's own corpus previously recorded it as an aggregation and had to delete the method: the addends did not reach the total, and the version in circulation counted NVIDIA's inbound equity as an outbound purchase.

2.The honest version: concentration, not arithmetic

What is disclosed is enough. OpenAI contracted about $300bn with Oracle over five years, equal to 4.5 GW [s]; an incremental $250bn of Azure services [s]; $38bn with AWS [s]; and up to $11.9bn with CoreWeave [s]. Those four sum to roughly $600bn — and that is the number to argue with, because every addend is a disclosure rather than a paraphrase.

Against it: an annualized run rate above $20bn exiting 2025, on full-year 2025 revenue of roughly $13bn [e], reaching about $25bn annualized by February 2026 on an estimate that excludes Microsoft licensing [e]. Gross margin was about 33% in FY2025, down from 40% the prior year [e], with projected cash burn near $27bn in 2026 and $63bn in 2027 and no cash-flow-positive year expected before 2030 [f].

The fragility is not the ratio. It is the financing terms. OpenAI raised $122bn at an $852bn post-money valuation in March 2026 [s] — and of Amazon's $50bn commitment inside it, $35bn is contingent on an initial public offering or on reaching artificial general intelligence [s]. Roughly a third of the largest private round ever raised is conditional on an event with no agreed definition. NVIDIA's much-quoted $100bn is a letter of intent, staged per gigawatt deployed, and NVIDIA's own filing says there is no assurance a transaction completes [s]. AMD issued a warrant for up to 160 million shares at a cent, with full vesting contingent on 6 GW of deployment [s]. Suppliers are paying equity for demand, which tells you how they price its certainty.

3.The path the shock takes

Capital to cloud. Oracle's remaining performance obligations of $523bn are dominated by one contract [s], and it spent $55.7bn of FY2026 capex against roughly $50bn of guidance [s] to serve it. CoreWeave carries $24.9bn of debt on $4.8bn of equity [s], having been 62% Microsoft as recently as 2024 [s], and its March 2026 facility covenants on a 1.15x debt-service-coverage ratio — cash flow, not asset value [s]. Nebius guided $20–25bn of 2026 capex on $3.0–3.4bn of revenue [f] against contracts of $17.4bn with Microsoft [s] and about $27bn with Meta [s]. Each of these is a levered position on a handful of names.

Cloud to systems. Four customers were 75.1% of Super Micro's accounts receivable at March 2026, the largest at 32.2% [s], on a nine-month gross margin of 8.2% [s] and cash down from $5.2bn to $1.3bn in nine months [s]. Dell booked $24.4bn of AI orders against $16.1bn recognised in a single quarter [s]. Thin margins plus concentrated receivables is how a customer's problem becomes an integrator's problem.

Systems to silicon. NVIDIA's data-center revenue was $75.2bn in the quarter ended April 2026, up 92% [s], and Broadcom guided Q3 FY2026 AI semiconductor revenue to $16bn, up 200% [f]. Those growth rates are the last thing to break and the loudest when they do.

4.Who says this will not happen

The filings, and they are not weak. Amy Hood reports $633bn of Microsoft remaining performance obligations while stating demand continues to exceed supply [s]; Alphabet's RPO reached $519.5bn from $108.2bn a year earlier [s]. The buyer set is also broader than the discourse admits: Anthropic's run rate crossed $47bn in May 2026 [s] and it raised $65bn in Series H funding the same month [s]. A single failure would hurt; it would not empty the order book.

The macro steelman is the Federal Reserve's own: total US private nonfinancial credit stood at $42.2 trillion with the debt-to-GDP ratio at its lowest in two decades [s]. This leverage is being layered onto an under-levered corporate sector. Bridgewater, forecasting about $650bn of 2026 big-four AI infrastructure investment [f], is on the cautious side of that same evidence — which is the point: the disagreement is about pace, not about whether the demand exists.

5.What would falsify this

  • The OpenAI IPO completes and the $35bn contingent tranche converts [s]. That single event removes the strangest term in the corpus and most of this page's force.
  • Oracle's RPO keeps rising while its capex is funded from operating cash flow. Concentration matters only if the seller is financing the buyer's growth.
  • A revenue year that covers a contract year. The commitments run about eight years; a run rate that makes the annualised obligation ordinary settles the argument on arithmetic.
  • Neocloud concentration falls with no customer above about a quarter of revenue, disclosed in a 10-Q rather than asserted on a call.
