Scale AI

The labelling incumbent whose most valuable asset was not being aligned with any lab. In June 2025 it sold 49 percent of that.

09 Data Supply

Scale AI is the only company in this stack whose most valuable asset was a negative: it was not aligned with any single frontier lab. In June 2025 it sold 49 percent of itself to one of them, and the layer got a natural experiment on what neutrality was worth.

1.Role in the stack

A data-supply vendor for the human-generated half of the layer — labels, preference rankings, demonstrations, graded answers. It priced per completed task at a markup and grew by managing the largest low-cost annotation workforce. That model built the autonomous-vehicle era of labelling and the RLHF era after it.

Scale reported $870m of 2024 revenue against a $1.5bn annualized run-rate at end-2024, with an estimated $2bn in 2025 [e]. The 2025 figure is an estimate; the 2024 one is reported. Meta then invested $14.3bn for a 49 percent non-voting stake at a $29bn valuation in June 2025 [s].

Customer exposure sat in exactly the accounts that stake threatened. Google spent about $150m with Scale in 2024 and had planned $200m for 2025 before moving to split after the Meta investment [s]. The corpus holds Scale's per-task price points and gross-margin range in a source note rather than as citable facts, so this page will not print them — which means the most basic question about the business, what it keeps per dollar billed, has no admissible answer here.

3.The bull case

Start by correcting the story, because the widely told version overstates the damage. "OpenAI departed Scale" is false, and it is contradicted by the very CNBC report the claim is sourced to: OpenAI's chief financial officer said the company would keep working with Scale as one of several data vendors [s]. Google's move is reported intent, not a completed exit. Microsoft was reported to be pulling back. Any account listing four clean departures has invented most of them.

What remains is a business with real reported revenue that roughly doubled from 2024 to an estimated 2025 [e], a $29bn arm's-length mark set by a sophisticated buyer with full diligence [s], and a shareholder that is also one of the three or four entities on earth that needs data at this scale. Meta did not buy 49 percent of a vendor it intended to stop using.

4.The bear case

The bear case does not need the departure list, and it is stronger without it. One month after the Meta stake, in July 2025, Scale laid off 200 full-time employees — about 14 percent of staff — and ended roughly 500 contractor relationships [s]. That is a measured consequence rather than a reported intention, and it arrived inside a quarter. A labelling vendor's asset is that it is aligned with nobody; an investor can destroy that asset by buying in, and the payroll shows the damage before the revenue line does.

The second problem is that the growth in this layer moved to a different unit. Mercor reached $2bn of annualized gross revenue in June 2026, up from a $760m run-rate at end-2025 [s], buying credentialled expert hours at average rates above $85 with contractors keeping 60 to 70 percent of the top line [s]. Those splits are Sacra estimates with no independent corroborator, so the netted number is soft. The direction is not: the reinforcement-learning era buys physicians and quantitative analysts by the hour, and Scale was built to buy annotation by the task.

Third, buyer concentration has no floor. Three or four labs need data at this scale, each is large enough to be a majority of a vendor's revenue, and each can build the capability internally. Meta's 49 percent stake is the most expensive in-housing attempt anyone has made, and it was aimed at this company.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether Google's reported plan to split from Scale ever completes. The originating CNBC report describes intent, not an exit; resolution shows up in a disclosed vendor change or in Scale's next revenue figure, not in more reporting.
  2. 02Whether Scale discloses a 2026 revenue figure at all, and whether it grows off the estimated $2bn 2025 base — the only way to tell whether Meta became an anchor customer or a replacement for lost ones.
  3. 03Whether headcount recovers past the pre-July-2025 level. If it does not, the 200-person cut was a reset of the cost base rather than a reaction to one quarter.
  4. 04Whether Meta discloses in-housing of data operations it previously bought. The stake was reportedly about capability, and capability brought in-house is billings removed.
  5. 05Whether any per-task price or gross-margin figure is ever published by Scale or a customer. The corpus holds Scale's unit economics only in a source note, so the layer's oldest business model is currently unauditable.
