Who actually does the work

24 firms, ordered from the bottom of the stack upward. Each page is the same five things: role, exposure, the best bull case, the best bear case, and what would settle it.

01 Primary Energy

  • GE VernovaThe AI buildout's most-quoted scarcity story, told with the most-abused arithmetic. Its own reported ratio is 3.7x, not 7x.

03 Datacenter Physical

  • VertivThe counterpoint to the integrators: power and thermal content earns roughly six times an ODM's margin inside the very same rack.

04 Fab & Tooling

  • ASMLThe whole leading edge runs through about sixty-five machines a year. ASML decides how many exist, and has committed to 2027 but not to 2028.
  • IntelThe manufacturing revival is real; the customer list is not. 18A in volume, first High-NA logic product — and 95% of Foundry revenue is Intel selling itself.
  • TSMCRevenue up 36% on units up 16.6%. TSMC is not selling more wafers to the AI boom — it is selling the same wafers for more, and rationing the packaging.

05 Compute Silicon

  • AMDThe credible second merchant supplier — which in October 2025 paid its largest prospective customer in equity to become one.
  • BroadcomCustom silicon's share gains are real and, while packaging is rationed, additive — Broadcom has taken capacity that did not exist, not sockets from NVIDIA.
  • HuaweiChina's answer to NVIDIA, running at scale on a stockpile it cannot replace — the question is what happens when it runs out.
  • NVIDIAThe stack's price-setter, now also its largest investor — and the one company whose biggest market was closed by the buyer's government, not the seller's.

06 Memory & Interconnect

  • SK hynixA 72% operating margin in a business the industry files under commodity. The level is the finding; the comparison everyone reaches for does not exist.

07 Systems & Integration

  • Hon Hai Precision (Foxconn)The largest AI rack assembler on earth, and eight years of evidence that assembling racks confers no pricing power.
  • Super Micro ComputerThe purest read on AI server integration, because it is only that business — and its record quarter carried its worst margin ever.

08 Cloud & Neoclouds

  • AlphabetIts backlog grew by more in one quarter than the whole figure was a year earlier — and it is not the cloud number people think it is.
  • AmazonThe only hyperscaler that made its own machines worth less on purpose — while spending more on them than its operations produce.
  • CoreWeaveThe neocloud that runs the hyperscaler business model without the hyperscaler balance sheet — and shows what that costs.
  • MicrosoftIts largest AI customer is also its largest AI investment, and the only quantity it will define precisely is the one it must file.
  • NebiusContracted revenue measured in tens of billions, quarterly revenue in hundreds of millions, and capex guided at roughly seven times the revenue it funds.
  • OracleA neocloud with an enterprise software business attached — and a forward book one customer could rewrite.

09 Data Supply

  • Scale AIThe labelling incumbent whose most valuable asset was not being aligned with any lab. In June 2025 it sold 49 percent of that.

10 Foundation Models

  • AnthropicPassed OpenAI on revenue and rented GPUs from a rival's parent in the same month. Both are real, and both travelled wrong.
  • OpenAIThe stack's largest demand-side counterparty, whose most-quoted number is one executive's aggregate rather than a sum of contracts.

13 Applications

  • HarveyLegal AI's capital winner. It publishes lawyer counts and nothing about revenue, so every multiple on it belongs to one research shop.

14 Distribution & GTM

  • AccentureIt booked $5.9bn of generative AI in FY2025 inside a shrinking total book, then stopped disclosing the metric. The silence is the datapoint.

16 Capital & Policy

  • Meta$45.95bn of maximum exposure to loss carried at $1.83bn of equity — the clearest picture in the stack of AI capex moved off a balance sheet.
