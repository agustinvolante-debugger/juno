Anthropic

Passed OpenAI on revenue and rented GPUs from a rival's parent in the same month. Both are real, and both travelled wrong.

10 Foundation Models11 Inference & Serving16 Capital & Policy

Anthropic did two things in May 2026 that look contradictory. It passed OpenAI on revenue, and it rented 220,000 GPUs from a direct competitor's parent company. Both are true. Both are narrower and stranger than the versions that circulated.

1.Role in the stack

A frontier lab that sells tokens rather than a consumer habit. Sacra estimates 70 to 75 percent of revenue comes from pay-per-token API calls, with subscriptions at 10 to 15 percent [e]. That makes Anthropic the most inference-exposed of the large labs — it lives on the per-token price curve, and it is also one of the three or four entities that sets it.

Run-rate revenue crossed $47bn in May 2026 [s], up from the $30bn announced in April [s]. Company-stated, unaudited, and annualized off a seven-week window in which a single large enterprise contract distorts the snapshot. The capital matched it: a $65bn Series H at a $965bn post-money valuation in May 2026 [s].

The overtake is real, and it is roughly 1.4x. Sacra estimates OpenAI at approximately $33bn in that same month [e]. The wider figure everyone repeated — closer to 1.9x — comes from setting Anthropic's May against OpenAI's February number of $25bn [e], which Sacra states excludes Microsoft licensing revenue and large one-time deals. Anthropic's figure carries no such carve-out. Mismatched dates on mismatched definitions produce a lead that is real but smaller than advertised.

Customer exposure is enterprise and top-heavy: more than 500 companies each spending over $1m a year [s], and more than 300,000 business customers accounting for roughly 80 percent of revenue [s]. That 80 percent is the business-customer cut, not the API cut, and conflating the two is how "80 percent from API" got into circulation. On share, Menlo Ventures puts Anthropic at 40 percent of enterprise LLM spend against 27 for OpenAI [e] — Menlo is an Anthropic investor. The independent check points the same way but measures something else: 42.4 percent of AI-spending businesses on Ramp paid Anthropic in June 2026, versus 39.5 percent paying OpenAI [s], which counts payers, not dollars.

3.The bull case

The strongest version is about cost discipline, and it comes from Anthropic's own projections.

forecast · secondary reporting on Anthropic's own projections — single source, directional onlyTraining costs are projected to peak near $30bn through 2028, roughly 4x below OpenAI's projected $121bn of 2028 compute spend [f], with profitability projected for 2028-2029. One secondary source. Treat the ratio as a direction, not a measurement.

Pair that with the one thing nobody else at this layer has demonstrated: raising a list price. Claude Sonnet 5's output price rises from $10 to $15 per million tokens on 1 September 2026, ending introductory pricing [s]. In a layer where per-capability prices fall about an order of magnitude a year, a scheduled 50 percent increase is the most interesting fact on this page.

4.The bear case

The obligations are enormous and mostly conditional. More than $100bn committed to AWS over ten years [s], $30bn of Azure capacity [s], access to up to 1,000,000 Google TPUs [s], and agreements for up to 5 GW of new Amazon capacity plus 5 GW of next-generation TPU capacity [s]. Read the "up to" on all of it: these are announcements about future capacity, not energized load, and the queue that decides delivery sits three layers down.

Then the lease, which is the sharpest version of the bear case. Anthropic contracted with SpaceX — not xAI — for 220,000 GPUs and more than 300 MW across Colossus 1 and 2 [s], reported at $1.25bn a month through 2029, struck after Colossus 1 fell to about 11 percent GPU utilization when xAI moved training onto Colossus 2 [e]. Those terms are press-attributed to SpaceX's IPO filing and typed as an estimate until someone checks the filing itself.

The utilization number is the mechanism, and it cuts both ways. An almost-idle cluster was worth more rented to a direct rival than held empty — which tells you the marginal price of frontier compute is softer than the commitment totals imply. It also tells you Anthropic's marginal capacity is leased from a landlord whose sibling company has a structural interest in that capacity being scarce at renewal.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether the confidential S-1 filed on 1 June 2026, once public, confirms or contradicts the run-rate path — specifically whether audited revenue supports annualizing the $30bn-to-$47bn seven-week window, and what share is pay-per-token API.
  2. 02Whether Claude Sonnet 5's scheduled 1 September 2026 list increase to $15 per million output tokens survives into invoices or is absorbed by enterprise discounting. It is the first testable claim of pricing power at this layer.
  3. 03Whether the SpaceX Colossus lease is renewed, repriced or dropped as Colossus 1 utilization recovers. The reported terms run to 2029, and the lessor's sibling company competes with the tenant.
  4. 04Whether the next AWS or Google disclosure converts 'up to 5 GW' into energized capacity with a date, or restates the same conditional language a year later.
  5. 05Whether any filing discloses the API-versus-subscription revenue split, which would settle Sacra's unsourced 70-75 percent and end the recurring conflation with the 80-percent-from-business-customers figure.
