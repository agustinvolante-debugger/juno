Alphabet

Its backlog grew by more in one quarter than the whole figure was a year earlier — and it is not the cloud number people think it is.

08 Cloud & Neoclouds05 Compute Silicon16 Capital & Policy

Alphabet's remaining performance obligations went from $108.2bn to $519.5bn in a year [s]. The figure is filed, and it is enormous, and almost everyone quoting it is describing the wrong thing.

1.Role in the stack

Alphabet is the only vertically integrated player in the middle of the stack: it designs the accelerator, owns the datacenter, sells the cloud, trains the frontier model, and runs the distribution surface it all lands on. It is also the only company selling a rival frontier lab a genuine alternative to NVIDIA at gigawatt scale.

The RPO number first, with its scope attached. $519.5bn at 30 June 2026, against $108.2bn a year earlier — and it is total-company remaining performance obligations from the filing, not the Google Cloud backlog management cites on calls [s]. It also roughly doubled in a single quarter, which is a stranger fact than the annual comparison and gets less attention.

Capex is guided at $175–185bn for 2026, with the top end later pushed toward $190bn [s]. The funding is the change in character: Alphabet raised approximately $83bn of debt between November 2025 and June 2026 [e] — an aggregation across quarterly proceeds and shelf takedowns, by a company that spent two decades not needing any.

And the accounting is the same optimism as everyone else's. Alphabet extended server and network equipment life to six years effective January 2023, lowering depreciation by roughly $3.4bn [s].

3.The bull case

The TPU is the case, and it is a supply-side one. Ironwood delivers 4,614 TFLOPs per chip [s] with 192 GB of HBM at 7.37 TB/s [s], scaling to 9,216-chip pods at 42.5 exaflops [s]. Anthropic took access to up to 1,000,000 TPUs and more than 1 GW of capacity in October 2025 [s], then signed for multiple gigawatts of next-generation TPU with Google and Broadcom from 2027 [f]. Alphabet is selling its competitor the means of production, at a cost base no merchant-silicon buyer can match.

Efficiency compounds that. Alphabet reports 2025 fleet-wide PUE of 1.09 against a surveyed industry average of 1.54 [s] — company-reported, but a structural cost advantage if it holds. And it is deflating the layers above it deliberately: Gemini File Search charges $0.15 per million tokens to index with nothing for storage [s], and Search grounding fell to $14 per 1,000 queries from $35 in one model generation [s].

4.The bear case

RPO is obligations, not cash, and Alphabet publishes no cancellation or renegotiation terms — so the strongest number on the page cannot be stress-tested from outside. The scope problem makes it worse: a total-company figure cannot tell you how much of the buildout is contracted against.

The dependencies are also less controlled than the vertical-integration story suggests. Alphabet has signed 1 GW of datacenter demand response with five utilities, with its own caveat that flexibility has limits and will only be available at certain locations [s], and its order for up to 500 MW from Kairos small modular reactors does not target a first reactor online until 2030 [s]. On data, a rival's cap table moved Alphabet's supply chain: it spent about $150m with Scale AI in 2024 and planned $200m for 2025, then moved to split after Meta's investment [s].

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether Alphabet begins disclosing Google Cloud backlog separately from total-company remaining performance obligations. Everyone quoting $519.5bn treats it as a cloud metric; it is not. Until the split is filed, none of it can be attributed to AI demand with confidence.
  2. 02Whether the $175–185bn capex range is raised a second time. The top end has already moved toward $190bn once, and a further raise this late in the year would mean the constraint is cost inflation rather than ambition.
  3. 03Whether the six-year server and network life set in January 2023 survives the next 10-K. Alphabet took roughly $3.4bn of annual depreciation relief from that change and has not revisited it while Amazon moved the other way.
  4. 04Whether Alphabet's debt raising keeps compounding past the roughly $83bn taken between November 2025 and June 2026, and whether any of it arrives secured or through a special-purpose vehicle rather than as plain unsecured Alphabet paper. The structure tells you more than the size.
  5. 05Whether the multi-gigawatt next-generation TPU capacity announced with Broadcom starts arriving in 2027 as stated. That date is the test of whether Alphabet can sell accelerators at scale to labs it competes with.
