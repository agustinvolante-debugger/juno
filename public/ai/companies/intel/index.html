Intel

The manufacturing revival is real; the customer list is not. 18A in volume, first High-NA logic product — and 95% of Foundry revenue is Intel selling itself.

04 Fab & Tooling05 Compute Silicon

There are two Intels in the corpus and they contradict each other. One is executing better than it has in fifteen years. The other still cannot name a leading-edge customer.

1.Role in the stack

Intel is the only US-owned company operating a leading-edge logic fab, which makes it the sole candidate for a second source at the top of the stack — and the reason the concentration question in fab-tooling has any answer other than "Taiwan." It is simultaneously a merchant chip designer competing with its own would-be foundry customers.

Q2 2026 revenue was $16.1bn, up 25%, which Intel characterised as its strongest revenue growth in more than fifteen years [s] — the company's own framing, and deliberately vaguer than the "since 2011" that circulated. Intel Foundry revenue was $5.77bn [s] against TSMC's $40.20bn in the same quarter [s], and only $293m of Foundry's $5,765m was external [s]. Roughly 95% is intersegment. Capex guidance was raised to more than $20bn gross in July 2026 [f].

3.The bull case

The manufacturing claims are now filed rather than promised. Intel released its first 18A products in high-volume production at the start of 2026 [s] — the start of the year, per Intel's own 10-Q, not the late-2025 date that reached the trade press. It also produced the first commercial logic product manufactured on High-NA EUV, announced in Q2 2026 [s], a curve TSMC chose not to ride for N2 or A16. Whatever else is true, Intel is ahead on one tool generation.

Demand signals exist below the customer line, too. CEO Lip-Bu Tan said in May 2026 that four Taiwanese and two Japanese substrate suppliers were seeking upfront commitments to secure capacity for Intel's EMIB packaging [s]. Suppliers do not ask for prepayment against capacity nobody expects to use. And NVIDIA put $5bn into Intel in September 2025 for roughly a 4% stake alongside x86 co-development [s].

4.The bear case

The bear case is a single sentence in Intel's own filings. Intel committed in Q2 2026 to a 2028 high-volume ramp for 14A with risk production in the second half of 2027 [f] — and the same quarter's 10-Q still describes external 14A demand as uncommitted. That is three months after Reuters, citing Elon Musk, reported Tesla as the first major external 14A customer for chips destined for Tesla's Austin Terafab complex [e]. That report is sourced to comments rather than to any announcement or filing by either company, and every other outlet carrying it is syndicating the same wire.

So the $293m is not a starting point that grows mechanically [s]. It is the measured size of Intel's merchant foundry business after a decade of trying, funded by capex above $20bn gross [f] whose gross-to-net bridge Intel declined to quantify. A process node with no committed external volume is a cost centre with a roadmap.

Policy took something too: BIS revoked Validated End-User authorisation for Intel Semiconductor (Dalian) alongside two Korean fabs, effective 31 December 2025 [s].

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether Intel's next 10-Q still describes 14A external demand as uncommitted. The Q2 2026 filing did, three months after Reuters reported Tesla as the first external 14A customer on Elon Musk's word. One of those two statements has to move.
  2. 02Whether Intel Foundry external revenue clears $1bn in a quarter. It was $293m of $5,765m in Q2 2026. Until external revenue is a material fraction, 'foundry' describes an org chart, not a market position.
  3. 03Whether any named 14A customer appears in an Intel or customer filing rather than in a wire story citing comments. A filing-grade disclosure is the only version of this that is falsifiable.
  4. 04Whether 14A risk production actually starts in the second half of 2027, the milestone Intel committed to in Q2 2026 ahead of a 2028 high-volume ramp. Slipping risk production moves the ramp by definition.
  5. 05Whether the gross-to-net gap on 2026 capex is quantified. CFO David Zinsner guided gross capex above $20bn and attributed most of the bridge to grants and investment tax credits without sizing it, which leaves the real cash number undisclosed.
