Meta

$45.95bn of maximum exposure to loss carried at $1.83bn of equity — the clearest picture in the stack of AI capex moved off a balance sheet.

16 Capital & Policy08 Cloud & Neoclouds01 Primary Energy

Meta's most important AI number is not its capex. It is a maximum exposure to loss of $45.95bn sitting against $1.83bn of carried equity [s], in a venture that does not consolidate.

1.Role in the stack

Meta is the pure buyer. It has no cloud business, so it cannot sell any of this capacity to anyone — every dollar is a cost recovered only through ads and ranking. That gives it the strongest incentive of the four hyperscalers to move the spend somewhere other than its own balance sheet, and it has built the most elaborate machinery for doing so.

Meta raised 2026 capital expenditure guidance to $125–145bn in April 2026, from $115–135bn, attributing the increase to memory-chip prices and added data centre costs [s]; 2025 actual was $72.22bn [s].

Then the structure. The Blue Owl joint venture developing the Richland Parish campus commits the parties to fund pro rata shares of approximately $27bn in total estimated development costs — that is not the venture's size or its capitalisation [s]. What was actually contributed at formation was $11.3bn: Meta $4.3bn and Blue Owl $7.0bn in cash, with Meta taking a one-time $2.6bn distribution back and holding a 20% interest [s]. The gap between $11.3bn in and $27bn of eventual cost is the structure's entire point. Financing came through a joint-venture vehicle: roughly $27bn of A+ rated bonds plus about $2.5bn of equity, arranged by Morgan Stanley and anchored by PIMCO [s].

The filed exposure is the number to hold onto: maximum exposure to loss of $45.95bn against equity carried at $1.83bn as of 31 December 2025, as an unconsolidated variable interest entity under the equity method, with no residual value guarantee liability recorded [s].

3.The bull case

The market has repeatedly said yes at investment grade. Meta sold $30bn of its own investment-grade bonds in October 2025 against an order book of roughly $125bn [s] — four times the deal — and the joint-venture paper priced at A+ [s]. This is not exotic credit reaching for yield; it is the highest-quality end of the market funding datacentre shells.

Meta is also buying the scarce input early rather than late. A 20-year 1,092 MW virtual PPA for Clinton from June 2027 [s], 2,100 MW from Vistra's Perry and Davis-Besse plants plus 433 MW of uprates [s], and development frameworks for up to 1,200 MW with Oklo [s] and up to 690 MW with TerraPower [s]. And unlike a cloud provider, Meta needs no external customer to justify a GPU — improved ranking pays back inside a business it already owns.

4.The bear case

The useful-life extension is losing. Meta went to 5.5 years effective January 2025, worth about $2.9bn of depreciation relief [s] — and depreciation on property and equipment still climbed from roughly $11.0bn in 2023 to $18.0bn in 2025 [s]. The accounting lever is smaller than the volume it is fighting.

Its capital allocation record on AI assets is also mixed in a specific, documented way. Meta paid $14.3bn for a 49% non-voting stake in Scale AI at a $29bn valuation [s]; neutrality was the asset in data labelling, and Google — about $150m of 2024 spend with $200m planned for 2025 — moved to split afterwards [s]. Meanwhile capacity increasingly comes from leveraged intermediaries: roughly $27bn with Nebius [s] and $21bn with CoreWeave [s].

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether the Blue Owl venture ever consolidates. It is an unconsolidated variable interest entity under the equity method today; a change in the control analysis would move roughly $46bn of disclosed exposure onto Meta's balance sheet in one line, with no change in the underlying economics.
  2. 02Whether a residual value guarantee liability is ever recorded against that venture. None had been as of 31 December 2025. The quarter one appears is the quarter Meta's own accountants price the downside.
  3. 03Whether the 5.5-year server and network life set in January 2025 holds in the next 10-K. Depreciation rose to $18.0bn in 2025 despite the extension, which means the extension is losing to volume — and a reversal would land on top of that.
  4. 04Whether the $125–145bn capex range is raised a second time. The April 2026 raise was attributed to memory-chip prices and added data centre costs, both input costs Meta does not control.
  5. 05Whether Meta's neocloud commitments — roughly $27bn with Nebius and $21bn with CoreWeave — are reaffirmed in filings or quietly restructured. Both counterparties fund delivery with debt secured on the hardware, so Meta's capacity depends on someone else's credit window staying open.
