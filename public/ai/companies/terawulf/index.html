TeraWulf

A bitcoin miner turned AI landlord that had to buy its own creditworthiness from Google — and disclosed what it cost.

03 Datacenter Physical16 Capital & Policy

TeraWulf is where the Google financing machine touches dirt. It is also the only participant that has published, line by line, what standing behind someone else's lease is worth — because TeraWulf is the one who had to pay for it.

1.Role in the stack

A landlord, not a cloud. TeraWulf owns powered land and builds data centre shells on it, then leases critical IT load to operators who bring their own chips. That places it one rung below the neoclouds: it does not buy accelerators, does not carry GPU depreciation, and does not price compute. It converts secured grid interconnects into contracted rent.

It arrived here from bitcoin mining, which is the entire reason it matters. Miners hold the scarce input — energised, permitted power at scale — and Google has been systematically converting them. The Financial Times identified five such projects totalling 1.4 GW that raised $15bn of debt on the strength of a Google backstop [s]. TeraWulf was the first.

The operating business is small and just crossed over. Total revenue was $44.8m in the quarter to 30 June 2026, of which $31.9m — 71% — was HPC lease revenue and $12.8m was digital assets, against a net loss of $940.8m [s]. Lease revenue passed mining revenue for the first time, while total revenue still fell year over year, because bitcoin declined faster than the leases ramped.

The contracts are large and mostly undelivered. Three leases signed with Fluidstack in August 2025 cover 378 MW of critical IT load at Lake Mariner in upstate New York; one had partially commenced by 30 June 2026, with the rest expected in the second half of 2026 and early 2027 [s]. A second lease signed with Anthropic in July 2026 covers approximately 401 MW at the Justified Data Campus in Hawesville, Kentucky, on a 20-year initial term with two five-year extensions at Anthropic's election, first capacity expected in the second half of 2027 and full delivery in early 2028 [s]. Against the 779 MW those two leases total, 102 MW were energised as of July 2026 [s].

Now the part worth reading closely. Fluidstack is the named tenant on the Lake Mariner leases, and Google backstops its obligations: on a payment default or an insolvency, Google may either pay the lease termination fee or pay all rent due and assume the lease as tenant itself [s]. That is what let TeraWulf raise $3,200.0m of 7.75% senior secured notes due 2030 through a subsidiary in October 2025 to fund the buildout [s].

The guarantee was not free. TeraWulf issued Google warrants over 73,580,000 shares at an exercise price of $0.01, expiring in August 2030, and Google pledged those warrants to the noteholders until the backstop became effective [s]. TeraWulf booked the fair value at issuance as a $515.5m asset in deferred charges, amortised over the life of the leases [s] — a company capitalising its own credit rating as an intangible and depreciating it alongside the transformers.

3.The bull case

The asset is the interconnect, and it is genuinely scarce. TeraWulf holds energised, permitted power that takes years and a queue position to replicate, and it monetises that without buying a single accelerator. No GPU depreciation, no obsolescence cycle, no exposure to whether Hopper or Blackwell holds its rental rate — the tenant absorbs all of it. The leases run 20 years with extension options at the tenant's election [s], which is utility duration on infrastructure that cost a fraction of the contracted rent.

The credit enhancement genuinely works, and the market prices it. Google-backed data centre projects borrowed at a median 7.1% against 9.3% for neocloud operators building around NVIDIA chips, a gap Jefferies analysts called a structural cost-of-capital disadvantage [s]. TeraWulf's 7.75% coupon [s] sits inside that spread. For a company with $44.8m of quarterly revenue [s] to raise $3.2bn of secured debt at all is the backstop working exactly as designed.

And the counterparty risk is better than the tenant's own balance sheet suggests, because the guarantor can step into the lease [s]. Google marks the aggregate liability across ten such developments at $815m while acknowledging exposure of as much as $44bn if every lease went bad [s] — an accounting judgment that these leases do not default. TeraWulf's rent sits behind that judgment.

The Abernathy sale is evidence of underwriting, not just liquidity. TeraWulf put roughly $450m into a 168 MW Texas joint venture and agreed in July 2026 to sell its entire 50.1% for approximately $530.0m in cash at a premium to invested capital [s]. A developer that can exit a stake at a premium mid-build has demonstrated the development margin is real.

4.The bear case

The reported loss is dominated by the guarantee, and it gets worse when things go well. TeraWulf recognised a $755.7m loss in the quarter and $972.0m across the half year on the change in fair value of the Google warrants, with the liability rising from $844.7m to $1,816.7m in six months [s]. Those warrants strike at a cent, so their value tracks the share price: the better the equity story, the larger the loss booked on the instrument sold to buy the credit. It is non-cash, but it is not cosmetic — it is 73,580,000 shares of real dilution [s] against 485.7m weighted average shares, and the $515.5m capitalised asset [s] is the floor on what the arrangement cost, not the ceiling.

The Kentucky lease is the unhedged one. The Lake Mariner leases carry a Google backstop; no such support is disclosed for the 401 MW Anthropic lease [s], which is the larger of the two. Its reported $19bn of contracted revenue traces to the company's announcement and the press carrying it rather than to a filing — the 10-Q discloses the megawatts, the term and the options, but not the value [e]. Financing Hawesville on Anthropic's credit alone is a materially different exercise from financing Lake Mariner on Google's, and TeraWulf has not yet shown it can.

Delivery is the actual risk, and the numbers are far apart. 102 MW energised [s] against 779 MW contracted [s] means most of the revenue depends on construction that has not happened, on a grid that has to deliver, to a schedule that runs into 2028. Each backstop only becomes effective at its own lease's commencement date [s] — so during the build, the period of maximum capital at risk, the protection is not yet live.

And the concentration is nearly total. Two tenants, both routed to the same end user: Fluidstack leases capacity that serves Anthropic, and Anthropic leases the rest directly. The Abernathy buyer was a group led by Fluidstack itself [s], so TeraWulf's largest customer is also the acquirer of its assets. Every strand — the rent, the guarantee, the asset sale, the $35bn of TPU hardware financed to fill these buildings [s] — terminates at one lab's ability to pay. TeraWulf is not diversified across the AI trade. It is a levered instrument on a single counterparty, wrapped in a guarantee from that counterparty's largest investor.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether the 401 MW Anthropic lease in Kentucky gets a Google backstop. The Lake Mariner leases have one and the Kentucky lease does not; the financing terms TeraWulf gets for Hawesville are the cleanest test of what the guarantee is actually worth.
  2. 02Energized megawatts, not contracted megawatts. 102 MW were live in July 2026 against 378 MW plus 401 MW signed. This is the only number on the company that cannot be signed into existence, and it is the one to track quarter to quarter.
  3. 03Whether the warrant liability keeps compounding. It went from $844.7m to $1,816.7m in six months and flows straight through the income statement, so a strong equity year produces a larger reported loss.
  4. 04Whether Fluidstack's leases commence on schedule in the second half of 2026 and early 2027. One of three had only partially commenced by 30 June 2026, and the backstop on each lease only becomes effective at that lease's commencement date.
  5. 05What the Abernathy proceeds buy. TeraWulf sold a 50.1% JV stake to its own largest tenant for cash arriving in instalments through 2027 and says it will redeploy into wholly owned capacity — the next campus announcement tells you whether that happened.
