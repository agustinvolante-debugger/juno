Harvey

Legal AI's capital winner. It publishes lawyer counts and nothing about revenue, so every multiple on it belongs to one research shop.

13 Applications

Harvey publishes two kinds of number, and they are not the same kind. Its user counts are company disclosures. Its revenue figures are estimates from a single research shop, restated by aggregators that measure nothing. The $11bn valuation [s] is priced on the second kind.

1.Role in the stack

A vertical application sold to one profession. It buys tokens from the foundation-model layer at prices it does not set, wraps them in legal workflow, and sells seats to law firms and in-house teams on annual commitments. Nothing in the technology is proprietary to Harvey; the position it is defending is distribution inside a regulated profession.

Harvey served more than 100,000 lawyers across 1,300 organizations in more than 60 countries as of March 2026 — its own disclosure [s]. Sacra's higher count, 142,000 lawyers at 1,500 customers, post-dates Harvey's by two months, sits well above it and carries no source, so this page uses the company's figure and treats the other as unsupported.

Revenue is the inverse situation. The last well-sourced figure is the $100m ARR Harvey reached in August 2025, three years after founding [s]. The $300m now attached to May 2026 [e] is a Sacra estimate with no independent corroborator, and the aggregator figures near $190m that look like confirmation correspond to Sacra's own end-2025 estimate, not to $300m. Restatement is not corroboration.

Pricing is seat-based and enterprise-shaped: the base offering starts at about $1,200 per lawyer per month on twelve-month commitments [e]. Sacra's wording is "starts at", so that is a floor, not a point price — and Harvey publishes no pricing at all.

3.The bull case

The strongest version is that Harvey is buying the one thing at this layer that does not commoditize. Models get cheaper and capabilities get copied; a workflow embedded in a regulated profession, with conflict rules, privilege, and partner-level sign-off around it, does not get swapped on a benchmark result. On that reading, 100,000 lawyers at 1,300 organizations [s] is a distribution asset, not a technology one, and it is the largest such asset in legal.

The capital reinforces it deliberately. Harvey raised at an $11bn valuation in March 2026 [s] against Legora's $550m at $5.55bn [s]. Two firms financed at that scale in one profession is a race to exhaust the market before a third entrant establishes itself — a distribution strategy, and one that works if the switching costs are as high as both companies are betting.

4.The bear case

The valuation arithmetic rests on a number nobody outside the company has measured. $11bn against an estimated $300m is roughly 37 times forward revenue [s][e] — arithmetic over the two facts cited in this sentence, with an unverified denominator. Run it on the last independently sourced figure instead, the $100m of August 2025 [s], and the same valuation is more than a hundred times trailing. A site that prints the flattering denominator without saying which one it used is doing marketing, not analysis.

The second problem is the spread. Harvey sells a fixed seat and buys variable tokens, and it controls neither end. Claude Sonnet 5's output list price rises from $10 to $15 per million tokens on 1 September 2026 [s], and the tokenizer change behind recent model generations raises effective per-request cost in ways a dollars-per-million table does not show. Harvey discloses no gross margin, so the effect on unit economics is unobservable from outside.

Third, the model layer can walk into the workflow. Claude Code reached an estimated $2.5bn annualized by February 2026 [e] — a lab taking a vertical professional workflow directly, at a scale an order of magnitude above Harvey's estimated ARR. Legal is more defended than coding by regulation and procurement. It is not defended by anything Harvey owns.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether Harvey publishes an ARR figure of its own. Until it does, every valuation multiple on the company rests on a Sacra estimate with no independent source, and the last well-sourced revenue number is from August 2025.
  2. 02Whether the next user disclosure grows lawyers faster than organizations. Seat depth inside large firms is what an $11bn valuation is priced on; adding small firms at the same seat count is not the same growth.
  3. 03Whether any Harvey round, secondary or tender prices below $11bn. With the denominator unobservable, the numerator is the only side of the multiple that can be resolved from outside.
  4. 04Whether a named Am Law 100 firm discloses replacing Harvey with Legora or with a lab's own product. There has been no measured churn event in legal AI, which is why the switching-cost claim is still an assumption.
  5. 05Whether Harvey ever discloses a gross margin. It buys tokens at prices set one layer down and sells fixed-price seats; the September 2026 Sonnet 5 list increase is the first visible squeeze on that spread.
