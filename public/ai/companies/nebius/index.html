Nebius

Contracted revenue measured in tens of billions, quarterly revenue in hundreds of millions, and capex guided at roughly seven times the revenue it funds.

08 Cloud & Neoclouds16 Capital & Policy

Nebius is CoreWeave's shape one lap earlier, and the interesting number is not the growth rate. It is the distance between what the company has contracted and what it must spend to deliver it.

1.Role in the stack

A neocloud: it buys accelerators, wraps them in power and networking, and rents capacity on multi-year contracts, with no software business alongside to absorb a bad year. That makes it — like CoreWeave — a place where every cost the hyperscalers can bury inside a larger P&L is visible on the income statement.

Revenue was $399.0m in the first quarter of 2026, up 684% year over year, with adjusted EBITDA of $129.5m [s]. Against that, two contracts: $17.4bn with Microsoft through 2031, scalable to $19.4bn, signed in September 2025 [s], and approximately $27bn with Meta over five years, announced alongside the Q1 2026 results [s]. The Meta figure rests on two press reports that agree with each other; the corpus has no company filing confirming it, and this page flags that rather than smoothing it.

The capital structure is the exposure. Nebius guided full-year 2026 revenue of $3.0-3.4bn with capital expenditure of $20.0-25.0bn [f] — the leverage-forward neocloud model in one ratio, roughly seven times capex to revenue. And its supplier is now a shareholder: on 11 March 2026 Nebius sold NVIDIA a pre-funded warrant for 21,065,936 Class A ordinary shares at $0.0001 each, raising approximately $2bn gross, with a six-month transfer lock-up [s].

3.The bull case

The revenue is contracted, and by counterparties that do not fail. Microsoft at $17.4bn [s] and Meta at roughly $27bn [s] against a $3.0-3.4bn revenue year [f] is more than a decade of visibility signed by two of the four largest capex payers on earth. NVIDIA putting $2bn into the equity [s] is a supply-allocation signal as much as a financing one.

The pricing environment supports the unit economics too, and it argues against the simple oversupply story. One-year reserved H100 contracts bottomed at $1.70 per GPU-hour in October 2025 and rebounded about 38% to $2.35 by March 2026 [s], because providers sold out and little Hopper capacity came off contract. Frontier-generation pricing is firmer still: B200 rentals ran $4.50-7.00 an hour in the second quarter of 2026 with marketplace rates rising month over month [s].

4.The bear case

Read the two guides together. Capex of $20.0-25.0bn against revenue of $3.0-3.4bn [f] means the contracts run to 2031 while the spending happens now — and the first quarter's $399m [s] annualises below the low end of the company's own revenue guide. Every dollar of that capex has to be financed before the contracted revenue arrives, and the gap is the business.

CoreWeave shows what the financing eventually looks like at scale: a $740m GAAP net loss alongside a 56% adjusted EBITDA margin in the same quarter [s], bridged by $536m of quarterly net interest [s] on $24.9bn of debt [s], and an $8.5bn facility placed in a bankruptcy-remote subsidiary secured on substantially all of that subsidiary's assets [s]. Nebius is earlier in that sequence, not exempt from it.

The competitive floor is softer than the contracts suggest. One estimate — a single source with unpublished methodology, from Value Add VC, so directional only — counts more than 300 new GPU cloud providers entering the market in 2025 [e]. And the demand-side risk sits above: Nebius is a marginal seller of capacity to firms that are also building their own, so a pause in hyperscaler capex reaches the neoclouds first.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether 2026 revenue tracks toward the $3.0-3.4bn guide. Q1's $399m annualises well below the low end, so the guide requires a steep intra-year ramp that either shows up in Q2 and Q3 or does not.
  2. 02Whether 2026 capex lands inside the $20-25bn guide, and how it is funded. Nebius raised about $2bn from NVIDIA in March 2026 via a pre-funded warrant; the size and structure of the next raise is the clearest read on the gap between contracted revenue and the capital needed to serve it.
  3. 03Whether the roughly $27bn Meta agreement appears in a Nebius filing with disclosed terms. Two press reports agree on the number and no company document in the corpus confirms it — the 6-K or annual report is what would.
  4. 04Whether Nebius reports a positive operating result in any quarter of 2026, not only positive adjusted EBITDA. The adjustment is where depreciation on a fleet being built at $20bn+ a year lives, and that gap widens before it narrows.
  5. 05Whether any Nebius facility is financed against the GPUs themselves. CoreWeave's March 2026 loan put the hardware in the lien inside a bankruptcy-remote subsidiary; if Nebius follows, the collateral question moves from theoretical to priced.
