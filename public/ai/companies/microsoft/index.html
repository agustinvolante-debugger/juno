Microsoft

Its largest AI customer is also its largest AI investment, and the only quantity it will define precisely is the one it must file.

08 Cloud & Neoclouds14 Distribution & GTM16 Capital & Policy

Microsoft is the only company in this stack that is simultaneously its biggest AI customer's largest shareholder, its largest supplier, and its largest rival's landlord. That is not a metaphor. It is three disclosures from the same twelve months.

1.Role in the stack

Three businesses that reinforce each other. Azure sells the capacity. Microsoft Marketplace is the toll road other people's AI software travels to reach enterprise budgets. And Microsoft's balance sheet holds equity in the frontier labs on both sides of the model race — which means it books revenue from customers it partly owns.

Two RPO numbers, two scopes, and they are not interchangeable. The 10-Q reports $633bn of total remaining performance obligations at 31 March 2026, up from $398bn six months earlier, alongside management's plain statement that demand continues to exceed supply [s]. The earnings release from the same quarter reports $627bn of commercial RPO, up 99% [s]. Do not average them. And the widely repeated "$80bn of backlog Microsoft cannot fulfil because of power" appears nowhere in the call — Amy Hood's position is qualitative, not quantified [s].

The AI figure is softer than it looks. Microsoft says its AI business passed a $37bn annual run rate, up 123% [s] — a run rate, not recognised revenue, with no stated composition. Satya Nadella said on the same call that Microsoft 365 Copilot had passed 20 million paid enterprise seats, from 15 million a quarter earlier [s]; the release itself contains no seat count. At list price those seats imply roughly $7.2bn of annualised value — a ceiling, almost certainly above realised contract value, and about 19% of the $37bn [e].

3.The bull case

The strongest argument is the rail, not the run rate. Microsoft charges 3% on Marketplace transactions with no listing fee, on an agency model where it owns billing and collections [s]. The mechanism that matters sits underneath: 100% of an eligible partner purchase made through Marketplace decrements the customer's Azure consumption commitment [s]. A third-party AI purchase becomes a zero-marginal-cash decision for any enterprise already carrying a commitment, while buying direct requires new budget. That is distribution power the take rate does not capture.

On the asset question, Microsoft has the better historical record. It moved server life from four years to six in fiscal 2023 [s], and Azure retired V100 GPUs roughly 7.5 years after launch [s]. Service life has exceeded book life.

4.The bear case

The counterparty is the exposure. Microsoft holds an OpenAI position valued near $135bn, about 27% as-converted [s], and OpenAI contracted an incremental $250bn of Azure — in the same transaction where Microsoft surrendered its right of first refusal as OpenAI's compute provider [s]. Both sides of that relationship now move on one company's ability to pay. Anthropic's $30bn Azure commitment [s] sits against a combined NVIDIA–Microsoft investment of up to $15bn in Anthropic [s] — the same structure again, hedged onto the other lab.

Meanwhile the spend against it is not guided at all. Analysts track Microsoft toward roughly $190bn of calendar-2026 capital expenditure [e] — an estimate, and marked as one, because Microsoft publishes no annual capex figure to check it against. The disclosure asymmetry runs through the whole page: Microsoft Cloud revenue of $51.5bn in the December quarter is filed and precise [s], while the AI business is a run rate it will not define and the Copilot seat count exists only as a sentence on a call.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether commercial RPO growth keeps decelerating. It was +110% at December 2025 and +99% at March 2026 — the level is enormous and the second derivative is already negative. Two more prints below 99% and the 'you do not sign that and then not need the machines' defence weakens.
  2. 02Whether Microsoft ever defines its AI revenue rather than quoting a run rate whose composition it does not disclose. A figure in the 10-K, or continued silence, tells you which kind of number it is.
  3. 03Whether the six-year server and network life set in fiscal 2023 survives the next 10-K. Microsoft has not touched it since; Amazon has already shortened a subset of its fleet back to five years.
  4. 04Whether the Microsoft 365 Copilot seat count moves from the earnings call into the press release or the filing. Today it exists only as a verbal CEO statement, and it is seats sold, not seats used.
  5. 05Whether the carrying value of the OpenAI stake changes in a filing. It is currently the largest single equity position underwriting a customer relationship anywhere in this stack, and Microsoft gave up its right of first refusal on that customer's compute in the same month it booked the commitment.
