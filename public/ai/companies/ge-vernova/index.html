GE Vernova

The AI buildout's most-quoted scarcity story, told with the most-abused arithmetic. Its own reported ratio is 3.7x, not 7x.

01 Primary Energy02 Grid & Interconnect

GE Vernova is the instrument everyone reads the energy shortage on, which makes the arithmetic worth doing carefully. The circulating figure is that it signs seven times what it ships. Its own reported unit metric, in the same presentation, is 3.7 times.

1.Role in the stack

One of three companies on earth that build heavy-duty gas turbines at scale, alongside Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries. It also sells the grid equipment behind the meter through its Electrification segment, which is where data centers show up most directly. Data center load was 20% of the 100 GW of gas capacity under contract as of the first quarter of 2026 [s] — and because the company says that 20% explicitly supports data centers, excluding indirect service, it is a floor rather than a share.

Gas equipment backlog plus slot reservation agreements reached 116 GW at the end of the second quarter of 2026, up from 100 GW a quarter earlier [s]. Total company backlog is $176bn [s], on $24.2bn of second-quarter orders, up 88% organically [s]. Electrification booked over $5bn of data-center-linked orders in the first half of 2026, more than double its full-year 2025 total [s]; gas power data-center orders are not in that number.

Now the ratio, because it is the most abused number in this discourse. GE Vernova signed 20 GW of gas equipment contracts in the quarter while shipping 3 GW [s] — roughly seven times, and the most generous of three available readings. In the same presentation the company reports its own unit metric: 12.1 GW of gas turbine orders against 3.3 GW of gas turbine sales, 3.7 times [s]. The gap is composition. The 20 GW is firm orders plus a much larger block of non-binding slot reservation agreements, which GE Vernova reports separately from both orders and backlog; on new firm orders alone, the quarter's signings came in below its 3.3 GW of shipments [s]. The scarcity is real. The multiple depends entirely on whether a reservation counts as a sale.

The same care applies to the slot count. Approximately 10 GW of combined 2029 and 2030 delivery capacity remained after the first quarter of 2026 [s] — GE Vernova's slots, not the industry's. Siemens Energy separately held 60 GW of firm orders plus 27 GW of reserved capacity [s], and none of that is in the 10 GW.

3.The bull case

Pricing is the evidence, not the backlog. GE Vernova guided first-half 2026 gas equipment orders to 10 to 20 points above the fourth quarter of 2025 on a dollar-per-kW basis, and reported in July 2026 that it landed at the higher end of that range [e]. Sellers do not achieve the top of a guided price increase in a market with spare capacity. Behind it, global gas turbine orders reached 110 GW in 2025 against global manufacturing capacity of 60 to 70 GW a year [e], and industry wait times reached up to 84 months, from 30 to 36 months previously [e].

4.The bear case

The strongest version comes from the company's own chief executive. On the same call the corpus cites for lead times, Scott Strazik said that in many of these projects "the gas turbines are really not the gating item," pointing at EPC buildout, permitting and fuel availability across a three-year project cycle [e]. The CEO whose slot scarcity anchors the shortage narrative does not hold the shortage narrative.

Supply is also already responding. Wood Mackenzie's Ed Crooks noted in February 2026 that all three manufacturers have announced significant gas-equipment capacity expansions since the start of 2025, and that turbine makers are increasingly not the primary bottleneck for large loads seeking power [o]. Wood Mackenzie projects 63 GW of manufacturing capacity additions between 2026 and 2030 [f] while expecting orders to peak in 2026 [e]. If both hold, GE Vernova adds output into a falling order book, and every slot reservation signed at today's pricing is a bet that does not clear.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether the 116 GW under contract shifts toward firm orders rather than slot reservations. The conversion rate from reservation to firm order is the number that decides whether this is a backlog or a waiting list, and GE Vernova reports the two separately in every deck.
  2. 02Whether GE Vernova reports at least 125 GW of gas equipment under contract at end-2026, as Scott Strazik has guided. It is the cleanest falsifiable test on the page and it resolves in one reporting cycle.
  3. 03Whether Wood Mackenzie's forecast that global orders peak in 2026 shows up in the 2027 order intake. If it does while 63 GW of manufacturing capacity is still being added to 2030, this becomes the overcapacity case rather than the shortage case.
  4. 04Whether H2 2026 gas orders hold the dollar-per-kW level already achieved. Pricing landed at the high end of a guided 10 to 20 point increase over Q4 2025; the next print tells you whether that was the ceiling or the new floor.
  5. 05Whether Electrification data-center orders grow off the $5bn booked in H1 2026. GE Vernova disclosed that figure under Q&A rather than in a table, so the disclosure itself may not survive — and its absence would be informative.
