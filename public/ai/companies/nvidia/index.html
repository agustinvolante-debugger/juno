NVIDIA

The stack's price-setter, now also its largest investor — and the one company whose biggest market was closed by the buyer's government, not the seller's.

05 Compute Silicon16 Capital & Policy

NVIDIA sets the price of AI compute, and in the last two years it also started paying for the demand. Both facts belong on the same page, because the company's revenue and its balance sheet now point at the same counterparties.

1.Role in the stack

Merchant accelerator silicon, sold increasingly as a rack rather than a chip — plus the networking that binds racks together, which is now a business in its own right. NVIDIA is also the layer's largest single equity investor, which means its customers' cost of capital and its own order book are not independent variables.

Revenue was $81.6bn in the quarter ended April 2026, up 85% [s], with data center at $75.2bn, up 92% [s], at a 74.9% GAAP gross margin held straight through the Blackwell-to-Rubin transition [s]. Networking alone was $14.8bn, up 199% [s].

China is the exposure that already resolved, and Beijing closed it. Revenue from customers headquartered in China including Hong Kong fell to $4,550m from $9,659m a year earlier [s] — but the filing's own caveat is that geographic revenue is designated by the headquarters of direct customers, so this is not a China-destination measure. The destination facts are separate and starker: zero data center Hopper products shipped to China in the quarter, against $4.6bn a year earlier [s], and zero revenue under the February 2026 H200 licensing programme as of the May 2026 filing [s]. Washington legalised the supply; Beijing had already barred the demand.

3.The bull case

The margin is the argument. A 74.9% GAAP gross margin held through an architecture change [s] is what pricing power looks like when a competitor is supposed to be taking share, and NVIDIA guided the following quarter to $91.0bn plus or minus 2% [f]. Meanwhile the strongest architectural challenger left by licence rather than by benchmark: Groq signed a non-exclusive agreement with NVIDIA in December 2025 worth $17bn in cash [s].

4.The bear case

NVIDIA is financing its own order book, and the list is now long enough to be structural: up to $100bn intended for OpenAI as each gigawatt deploys [s], a $6.3bn agreement to buy CoreWeave's unsold capacity through 2032 [s], $2bn of CoreWeave equity at $87.20 a share [s], a $2bn Nebius pre-funded warrant [s], up to $10bn of a combined $15bn into Anthropic with Microsoft [s], and $5bn into Intel [s]. Vendor equity and vendor backstops make revenue quality harder to read, not easier.

The second bear point is that a single sovereign already demonstrated it can zero a segment. The cost of one reclassification was a $4.5bn charge for excess H20 inventory and purchase obligations [s]. Twelve months after the AI Diffusion rule was rescinded, NVIDIA reported that the replacement's scope and timing remained uncertain [s], while US officials expressed an expectation of 15% or more of licensed China revenue with no regulation codifying it [s].

forecast · NVIDIA, July 2026 NVIDIA says it plans to produce up to $500bn of AI infrastructure in the United States with named partners [f]. No time horizon, no contractual basis, no partner breakdown — a plan, not a booking.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether any subsequent filing reports non-zero revenue under the February 2026 H200 China licensing programme. As of May 2026 it was zero, and NVIDIA said it did not know whether imports would be allowed. This resolves on a revenue line, not on a policy announcement.
  2. 02Whether the Taiwan line in the geographic revenue table keeps growing while the China line falls. Revenue is booked by direct-customer headquarters, so the two moving together would suggest re-routing rather than lost demand.
  3. 03Whether the up-to-$100bn OpenAI letter of intent converts into a definitive agreement with recognised progress payments. NVIDIA's own forward-looking-statements section flags the $100bn as an intent; a signed contract with milestones would move it out of that section.
  4. 04Whether GAAP gross margin holds near 74.9% once CoWoS capacity reaches the 120,000-130,000 wafers-per-month target. The margin is currently supported by a rationed packaging line; the first quarter with slack is the test.
  5. 05Whether the 15% China revenue share ever appears in a published regulation. NVIDIA's 10-Q says the US government expressed the expectation but 'did not publish a regulation codifying such requirement.'
