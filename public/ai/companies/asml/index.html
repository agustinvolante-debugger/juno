ASML

The whole leading edge runs through about sixty-five machines a year. ASML decides how many exist, and has committed to 2027 but not to 2028.

04 Fab & Tooling

ASML is the only company in the stack whose capacity can be stated as an integer. Roughly sixty-five extreme-ultraviolet lithography machines will ship in 2026, and nobody else makes one. Every argument about 2027 and 2028 accelerator supply is, underneath, an argument about that integer.

1.Role in the stack

ASML sells the tool, not the wafer. It has a monopoly on EUV lithography and a strong position in the deep-ultraviolet immersion systems that do the rest of the patterning. Its customers are the four or five firms that can afford a leading-edge fab; its constraint is its own build rate, not demand.

Q2 2026 net sales were €9.3bn at a 54.0% gross margin [s], of which EUV was €3.8bn of €6.6bn in net system sales, including exactly one High-NA machine [s]. Full-year guidance was raised in July to €43-45bn, from €36-40bn after Q1 [f]. Customers in China were 29.1% of 2025 net sales, down from 36.1% in 2024 [s] — still the single largest geographic exposure, and the one governed by export policy rather than by demand.

Unit economics are unusual for a supplier this critical: the High-NA EXE:5200-class system lists at approximately $380m [e], and fewer than a dozen exist anywhere.

3.The bull case

Demand is running ahead of ASML's own planning. Guidance moved from €36-40bn to €43-45bn inside one quarter [f], and the company plans roughly 65 Low-NA EUV shipments in 2026 with EUV net system sales growing above 45% year over year — CFO Roger Dassen's figure, not the CEO's [f]. Growth above 45% on a unit count that cannot expand much means ASML captures price the same way its customers do.

High-NA has also stopped being a science project. The first commercial logic product manufactured on High-NA EUV was announced in Q2 2026, produced by Intel [s] — a milestone TSMC has deliberately not chased, holding Low-NA for N2 and A16. A second technology curve with one customer today is optionality; ASML owns both curves.

4.The bear case

The relief everyone is modelling is not committed.

forecast · ASML (CFO Roger Dassen) ASML plans a 30% capacity increase for both Low-NA EUV and immersion systems in 2027, and is only investigating a further 30% increase for 2028 [f]. Dassen's language separates the years: 2027 is an intention to increase, 2028 is an investigation. And the metric is capacity — what ASML could build — not output shipped.

That distinction is the bear case, because it cuts both ways. If 2028 does not arrive, the supply floor under every accelerator roadmap is lower than modelled. If it does arrive, ASML has added 30% twice into a demand curve funded by a handful of buyers whose own capital is being raised in private credit markets — and lithography is the longest-lead, least-cancellable item in that chain, which is exactly what makes it painful to hold when orders pause.

Policy is the second exposure, and it is bidirectional. ASML's own filings record that China suspended its rare-earth export restrictions for twelve months until 10 November 2026 [s], with existing risk-mitigation measures still in place. ASML does not control either end of that. Nor does it control the demand side of its China business, where BIS revoked Validated End-User authorisations for three foreign-owned fabs inside the country effective 31 December 2025 [s], removing blanket tool-import rights from ASML's largest non-Chinese customers there.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether the 2028 expansion moves from 'investigating' to 'planning' in a subsequent ASML statement. CFO Roger Dassen distinguishes the two years sharply: 2027 is a plan, 2028 is only under investigation. Every 2028 accelerator-supply model implicitly assumes the upgrade happens.
  2. 02Whether China's share of net sales falls further after 10 November 2026, when the rare-earth export suspension ASML records in its own filings expires. The April 2025 licensing regime was never suspended, so the expiry adds a constraint rather than restoring one.
  3. 03Whether High-NA appears in more than one system in a quarter's net system sales. Q2 2026 included exactly one, so unit cadence — not the €380m list price — is the readable signal on adoption.
  4. 04Whether EUV net system sales growth stays above the 45% pace guided for 2026 once the tool count is capped near sixty-five. Revenue growth above unit growth means ASML, like TSMC, is being paid on price.
  5. 05Whether ASML's guidance is raised a third time. It went from EUR 36-40bn after Q1 to EUR 43-45bn in July; a company raising its own forecast twice in two quarters is telling you its order book moved faster than its planning.
