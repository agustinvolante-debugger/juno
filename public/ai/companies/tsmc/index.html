TSMC

Revenue up 36% on units up 16.6%. TSMC is not selling more wafers to the AI boom — it is selling the same wafers for more, and rationing the packaging.

04 Fab & Tooling05 Compute Silicon

Every argument about the AI buildout eventually arrives at one company's price list. TSMC's Q2 2026 says what that price list is doing, and it is not what a volume boom looks like.

1.Role in the stack

TSMC sells two things: a wafer start at a named process node, and a slot on an advanced packaging line. The second is now the scarcer good. Almost every accelerator on the site — NVIDIA's, AMD's, Google's TPU, Amazon's Trainium — is a claim on the same two queues.

Q2 2026 revenue was $40.20bn, up 36.0% [s], on 4.336 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers, up 16.6% [s]. Nineteen points of that growth is price and mix. Gross margin was 67.7% [s] against 59.9% for full-year 2025, 56.1% in 2024 and 54.4% in 2023 [s]; net profit was NT$706.56bn, up 77.4% [s]. HPC reached 66% of revenue from 61% in Q1 [s], with N3 at 30% of wafer revenue [s] and N2 booking its first-ever contribution at 3% [s].

Concentration is the counterweight: the ten largest customers were approximately 78% of 2025 net revenue, the largest at 19% and the second at 17% [s]. TSMC names none of them.

3.The bull case

Pricing power, demonstrated rather than asserted. N2 wafers are expected to run above $30,000 against roughly $20,000 at N3 [e] — an estimate, because TSMC discloses no wafer prices — and the margin trajectory says customers are paying it. The company then raised 2026 capex to $60-64bn from $52-56bn [f], having spent $40.9bn in 2025 [f]. Firms with pricing power and no demand problem behave exactly this way.

The strongest evidence for the scarcity is the CEO's. C.C. Wei said on the Q2 2026 call that TSMC's advanced packaging capacity was limiting its customers' growth [s]. That is a named executive on the record describing his own line as the constraint on everyone above him — and it is a stronger citation than the widely circulated claim that the top three CoWoS customers hold more than 85% of 2026 capacity [e], which traces to an unattributed trade estimate with disputed wafer counts and is used here for nothing.

4.The bear case

The cost side is being rebuilt in the most expensive place available. US fab construction runs four to five times the Taiwan baseline, per CFO Wendell Huang in July 2026 [e], against $265bn of announced US investment after a further $100bn Arizona commitment [s]. Depreciation from that arrives regardless of what pricing does.

The margin is already guided down. TSMC guided Q3 2026 revenue to $44.6-45.8bn [f] and guided gross margin below the quarter just reported, attributing the step down to N2 ramp dilution. N2 is 3% of wafer revenue today [s]; the dilution scales with the ramp, and the pricing offset is an estimate rather than a disclosure [e].

forecast · TrendForce, relaying unnamed institutional investors via Taiwan's Economic Daily News CoWoS capacity is reported to reach 120,000-140,000 wafers per month by end-2026, with the supply-demand gap narrowing from about 20% to about 10% [f]. Third-hand provenance, and TSMC discloses no CoWoS capacity at all. But if the gap narrows, so does the reason customers accept the price.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether Q3 2026 gross margin comes in below the 67.7% just reported. Management already guided it lower on N2 ramp dilution, so the reported number tests whether a new node's start-up cost outruns the price it commands.
  2. 02Whether unit growth converges with revenue growth in the next quarterly report. Today the gap is nineteen points, and pricing carries the story. When wafers shipped and revenue grow at similar rates, the pricing phase of this cycle is over.
  3. 03Whether C.C. Wei repeats on a subsequent call that advanced packaging capacity is limiting customers' growth. He is the highest-authority source in the corpus for packaging-as-bottleneck; the quarter he stops saying it is the quarter the queue clears.
  4. 04Whether top-ten customer concentration in the next 20-F exceeds 78%. It has gone 70%, 76%, 78% across three years while the largest customer's share fell from 22% to 19% — concentration is rising and broadening at the same time.
  5. 05Whether the gross margin absorbs the US fab mix. Construction at four to five times the Taiwan baseline has to show up somewhere; if it does not, customers are paying for it.
