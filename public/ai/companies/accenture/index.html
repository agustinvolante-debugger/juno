Accenture

It booked $5.9bn of generative AI in FY2025 inside a shrinking total book, then stopped disclosing the metric. The silence is the datapoint.

14 Distribution & GTM15 End Customer

The most informative thing Accenture published about AI in fiscal 2026 is a word that does not appear.

1.Role in the stack

The distribution layer's largest channel. Accenture does not build models or run inference; it sells the integration — the transformation programme, the change management, the systems work that moves a model into a Fortune 500 process. Roughly 799,000 employees are the product [s], and bookings are the demand signal the rest of the market reads.

Accenture recorded $5.9bn of generative-AI new bookings in fiscal 2025, including $1.8bn in the fourth quarter [s]. That was approximately 7.3 percent of $80.62bn of total new bookings, in a year when total bookings declined 1 percent [e]. A company-defined metric in an earnings release, so a company claim — but the standard proxy for systems-integration AI demand, which is why it mattered.

Read $5.9bn as a terminal value, not a point in a series. Accenture disclosed no generative-AI bookings or revenue figure in any of its three fiscal-2026 quarterly releases; the terms "generative AI" and "GenAI" appear zero times in each [s], verified by extracting all three releases and searching them. Nothing may present the $5.9bn as current or trending.

What replaced it is a turn in the total book. Third-quarter fiscal-2026 bookings were $19.32bn, down 2 percent in dollars and 3 percent in local currency, on revenue of $18.7bn, with full-year revenue growth guidance cut to 3 to 4 percent in local currency [s].

3.The bull case

The steelman is Accenture's own, and it is not weak. Generative AI stops being separable once AI sits inside most transformation programmes. A metric with no clean boundary is worse than no metric, because every quarter becomes an argument about what counted; the mega-deal disclosures that replaced it may proxy the same demand more honestly.

There is also a bull reading of the fiscal-2025 numbers themselves. $5.9bn of generative-AI bookings arrived inside a year when the total book fell 1 percent [e] — meaning AI was the only part growing, and the traditional book was the drag. And the category does appear to be still compounding at peers who still publish: Capgemini reported more than 8 percent of group bookings in 2025 and more than 10 percent in the fourth quarter, on €24,356m of bookings [s] — stated as a floor, and above Accenture's terminal share. If a direct competitor's AI share is rising, Accenture's silence is more likely an accounting decision than a demand collapse.

4.The bear case

Companies retire growth metrics when the metrics stop flattering them. The sequence is the argument: a disclosed $5.9bn in fiscal 2025 [s], then three quarters of nothing [s], then bookings down 2 percent in the quarter AI consulting was supposedly compounding [s]. Suggestive rather than decisive — but a company confident in the number keeps printing it.

The structural version is worse than the disclosure question. Accenture sells hours applied to work that AI is meant to compress. A successful AI programme delivers fewer billable hours in the next contract, which means the more the technology works, the more the channel's own revenue base erodes. A declining book alongside a withdrawn AI metric is exactly what that would look like from outside, and the corpus cannot distinguish it from a measurement change.

The peer set makes the opacity structural rather than idiosyncratic. Infosys reported fiscal-2026 revenue of $20,158m with $14.9bn of large-deal contract value and no AI-specific figure anywhere in the release, while describing itself as AI-first [s]. Cognizant reported trailing-twelve-month bookings of $29.6bn and headcount up 21,300, also with no AI figure [s]. When the flagship metric is withdrawn and no competitor except one publishes a substitute, AI demand through the services channel becomes unauditable — and the blast radius of that is analytical, landing on everyone who cites integrator bookings as evidence.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether the words 'generative AI' or 'GenAI' reappear with a number attached in any fiscal-2027 release. After three straight quarters of zero mentions, reinstatement is the disclosure event, not continuation.
  2. 02Whether fourth-quarter fiscal-2026 bookings return to growth after the third quarter's 2 percent decline. Two consecutive down quarters would separate a demand problem from a currency effect.
  3. 03Whether full-year fiscal-2026 revenue lands inside the cut 3-4 percent local-currency guidance or below it. The guidance was already reduced, so a miss against the reduced number is the meaningful signal.
  4. 04Whether Capgemini keeps publishing an AI share of bookings. If the last major integrator still disclosing one stops, AI demand through the services channel becomes unauditable from outside entirely.
  5. 05Whether Accenture ever quantifies AI-driven efficiency against its own headcount, approximately 799,000 in the third quarter of fiscal 2026. That number decides whether AI expands the billable base or eats it.
