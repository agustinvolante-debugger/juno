OpenAI

The stack's largest demand-side counterparty, whose most-quoted number is one executive's aggregate rather than a sum of contracts.

10 Foundation Models13 Applications16 Capital & Policy

OpenAI's $1.4 trillion is not a sum of contracts. It is Sam Altman's own attributed figure, covering roughly 30 GW across about eight years [o], and treating it as an audited commitment book is the most common error in the entire discourse.

1.Role in the stack

OpenAI occupies the model layer and the application layer at once — it trains the frontier system and sells the consumer product on top of it — and that makes it the largest single buyer in the capital layer. Oracle, Microsoft, Amazon, AMD, NVIDIA and CoreWeave all have material forward exposure to one company's ability to pay.

Altman put total data centre commitments at about $1.4tn across roughly 30 GW and about eight years in November 2025 [o]. The publicly named contracts are $300bn with Oracle [s], an incremental $250bn with Azure [s] and $38bn with AWS [s] — $588bn . The remainder is not itemised, and one widely circulated reconstruction of the total double-counted NVIDIA's inbound equity as an outbound purchase [o].

Pick the right denominator. Annualized revenue exited 2025 above $20bn; the roughly $13bn also in circulation is full-year 2025 recognised revenue, a different metric [e]. The scale on the demand side is genuine: more than 900 million weekly ChatGPT users [s], over 50 million consumer subscribers [s] and more than 9 million paying business users as of February 2026 [s]. The 1 billion monthly active app users is a third-party estimate, not an OpenAI disclosure [s].

3.The bull case

Nothing else in software has this shape. Run-rate revenue moved from about $20bn at end-2025 to roughly $25bn in February 2026 [e] and about $33bn by May 2026 [e] — estimates that explicitly exclude Microsoft licensing and large one-off deals, so they understate the total. Enterprise passed 40% of revenue and is tracking toward parity with consumer [e].

Capital has not been the binding constraint. OpenAI raised $122bn at an $852bn post-money valuation in March 2026 [s], after $500bn in an October 2025 secondary [s]. And it converts capital into capability faster than the schedule implies: reasoning training compute scaled 10x from o1 to o3 in four months [s].

4.The bear case

The margin is going the wrong way. Gross margin fell to 33% in fiscal 2025 from 40% the prior year, against an internal 46% target [e]. Inference cost was about $8.4bn in 2025 with $14.1bn projected for 2026 [e]. Projected cash burn is roughly $27bn in 2026 and $63bn in 2027, with cash-flow positive not expected until 2030 [f], and internal projections put 2028 compute spend at $121bn against an $85bn loss [f].

The financing is softest where it is largest. NVIDIA's up-to-$100bn is a letter of intent, progressive on each gigawatt deployed, which NVIDIA's own release flags as forward-looking and yet to be finalised [s]. $35bn of Amazon's $50bn is contingent on an IPO or on reaching AGI [s]. AMD issued a warrant for up to 160 million shares at $0.01, vesting in tranches tied to Instinct deployments and to share-price targets escalating to $600 [s], against an agreement to deploy 6 GW of AMD GPUs over five years [s].

And it is not winning the enterprise. Menlo — an Anthropic investor, conflict noted — puts Anthropic at 40% of 2025 enterprise LLM spend against OpenAI's 27% [e]; Ramp's transaction data shows 42.4% of AI-spending businesses paying Anthropic versus 39.5% paying OpenAI [s]. Meanwhile the price floor is set elsewhere: DeepSeek-R1 launched at $2.20 per million output tokens against $60 for o1 [s].

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether OpenAI itemises the portion of the $1.4tn that no public contract accounts for. The named agreements total $588bn. Until the remainder is disclosed, the figure is an attributed aggregate, not a verifiable commitment book — and no page on this site should treat it as one.
  2. 02Whether the NVIDIA arrangement converts from a letter of intent into a definitive agreement. NVIDIA's own filing says there is no assurance it completes, and the investment is structured to arrive progressively as each gigawatt is deployed — so the schedule, not the headline, is what to watch.
  3. 03Whether the $35bn AGI-or-IPO tranche of Amazon's commitment is funded, and against which trigger. No party has published a definition of artificial general intelligence for the purpose of the contract.
  4. 04Whether gross margin recovers above the 33% recorded for fiscal 2025. It fell from 40% the prior year against a 46% internal target, so the trend and the miss both point the same way; inference cost per dollar of revenue is the whole thesis.
  5. 05Whether the Ramp and Menlo readings of enterprise share converge or diverge further. Both currently put Anthropic ahead of OpenAI among enterprise buyers, and one of the two has a disclosed conflict of interest — a third independent measurement would settle it.
