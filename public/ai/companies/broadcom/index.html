Broadcom

Custom silicon's share gains are real and, while packaging is rationed, additive — Broadcom has taken capacity that did not exist, not sockets from NVIDIA.

05 Compute Silicon16 Capital & Policy

Broadcom is the reason "can anyone catch NVIDIA" became the wrong question. Its AI business is now guided at $16.0bn a quarter [f], and NVIDIA accelerated anyway. Both things happened because the constraint is not in either company.

1.Role in the stack

Broadcom does custom silicon design, serialisation and packaging for buyers who own the resulting product — Google's TPU, Meta's and OpenAI's accelerators — plus the Ethernet switching those systems run on. It does not own the roadmap or the customer relationship at the end of it. That is the model's strength on margin and its weakness on durability.

Q2 FY2026 revenue was $22.187bn, up 48% [s], following $19.311bn in Q1 [s]. AI semiconductor revenue inside that was $10.8bn, up 143% [s] — the best single datapoint anywhere on custom-ASIC share gains.

Customer exposure is concentrated in a handful of captive programmes. Anthropic disclosed agreements in May 2026 for up to 5 GW of new Amazon capacity plus 5 GW of next-generation TPU capacity with Google and Broadcom [s], and had announced in April 2026 that the Google-Broadcom partnership brings multiple gigawatts online starting 2027 [f]. Both are "up to" commitments about future capacity, not energised load.

3.The bull case

Run the arithmetic that the additive-versus-substitutive argument turns on. NVIDIA's data center revenue grew 92% to $75.2bn in the April 2026 quarter [s], which is roughly $36bn of quarterly revenue added year over year . Broadcom's entire guided AI line, at $16.0bn [f], is under half of that single increment — and NVIDIA guided the next quarter up again to $91.0bn [f].

If ASICs were displacing merchant GPUs, those two series would move in opposite directions. They do not, because the market is rationed: CoWoS demand is rising toward about 1.0 million wafers in 2026 from roughly 670,000 in 2025 [e], against capacity that went from about 35,000 wafers a month at end-2024 [e] to 72,500-80,000 at end-2025 [e], booked out with 52-78 week lead times [e]. A TPU order does not cancel an NVIDIA order. It consumes capacity that did not exist a year ago. On that reading Broadcom's 143% growth [s] costs NVIDIA nothing and is worth exactly what it prints.

4.The bear case

The strongest counter is that additive cuts against Broadcom too. Its growth is evidence that it won an allocation, not that it won a socket. Nothing in the numbers shows a buyer choosing an ASIC over a GPU on merit — only a buyer taking incremental packaging capacity while none was available at any price. When the queue clears, the comparison becomes real for the first time, and it will be made against a supplier holding a 74.9% gross margin [s] and roughly 60% of 2026 CoWoS allocation on Morgan Stanley's projection [e].

The demand is also being funded rather than earned, and the Google–Anthropic programme shows how far that now goes. Broadcom agreed in April 2026 to buy 3.5 GW of TPU hardware from Google for deployment by Anthropic [s], and its filings disclose $128bn of purchase commitments — $55.2bn of deliveries scheduled for fiscal 2027 and $72.9bn for fiscal 2028 — relating almost entirely to that purchase [s]. Against a company guiding $16.0bn of quarterly AI revenue [f], that is a purchase obligation several years of the AI line in size.

Then read what Broadcom did to make that hardware financeable. It guaranteed the two senior tranches of the vehicle that bought the chips through residual value support covering about $30bn of a $35bn financing, agreeing to cover any shortfall if Anthropic stopped paying and the hardware could not be resold for enough to make senior investors whole [s]. So Broadcom buys the silicon from Google, sells it into a private-credit vehicle, and then stands behind the vehicle's senior debt. Its exposure is not the margin on a sale; it is the residual value of used accelerators in the scenario where the single tenant defaults — the one scenario in which used accelerators are worth least.

forecast · Broadcom, Apollo and Blackstone, June 2026 The platform announced in June 2026 targets more than 20 GW of global AI deployments through 2028 [f], on an initial tranche of $35bn led by Apollo [s]. The gigawatts are an announced plan; the tranche is committed capital. When a chip vendor helps assemble the private credit that buys its chips, the order book and the credit cycle stop being independent.

A note on double counting, because it matters for reading the two paragraphs above together. The Apollo-led $35bn tranche [s] and the $35bn Compute SPV that bought roughly 1 GW of TPU hardware [s] agree on amount, month, sponsor and anchor investors. This site treats them as one vehicle described from two sides rather than two separate $35bn commitments. No filing in the corpus confirms that identity, so it is flagged here rather than asserted — but adding the two figures together would be the easiest way to overstate this programme by half.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue growth and NVIDIA's data center growth decelerate together or in sequence. Simultaneous deceleration says both were supply-limited; Broadcom accelerating as NVIDIA slows is the first evidence of actual substitution.
  2. 02Whether the Apollo-led $35bn initial tranche is drawn, and against what — announced gigawatts and committed capital are different quantities and should never be added. Watch too for the residual value support on that vehicle being quantified in a Broadcom filing rather than reported: it is the largest contingent exposure attributed to the company anywhere in this corpus.
  3. 03Whether the multi-gigawatt Anthropic TPU capacity with Google lands in 2027 as announced. Broadcom's AI line is a claim on a small number of captive programmes; a slipped or resized programme is directly visible in guidance.
  4. 04Whether Broadcom's AI revenue growth survives the quarter after CoWoS lead times fall below a year. The additive read is conditional on scarcity, and the queue clearing tests it in both directions.
  5. 05Whether any captive customer moves design in-house or to a second ASIC vendor. Broadcom does not own the products it builds, so its position resolves on customer disclosures, not its own.
