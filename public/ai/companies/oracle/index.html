Oracle

A neocloud with an enterprise software business attached — and a forward book one customer could rewrite.

08 Cloud & Neoclouds16 Capital & Policy

Oracle changed business model without changing its name. It now buys accelerators, wraps them in power and networking, and rents them on long contracts — the CoreWeave business, run from inside a database company.

1.Role in the stack

Oracle Cloud Infrastructure is a capacity landlord with an unusual advantage and an unusual dependency. The advantage is a database installed base that generates cash regardless of GPU pricing. The dependency is that the forward book is dominated by one counterparty.

Oracle spent $55.7bn of capital expenditure in fiscal 2026, against guidance of approximately $50bn [s] — its own forecast missed high by about 11% in the first full year of the build.

The forward book grew faster than anything else in the layer. RPO was $455bn in Q1 FY2026, up 359% year over year [s], reaching $523bn [s] — with the as-of quarter still unconfirmed from the filings, most likely the quarter ended November 2025. Inside it sits a single September 2025 contract with OpenAI for $300bn of computing power over five years, equal to 4.5 GW, with delivery reportedly starting 2027 [s]. That one agreement is roughly 57% of the entire book .

Funding so far has been plain: $18bn of investment-grade bonds in September 2025, raised largely to serve that contract [s].

3.The bull case

Oracle won the deal, and the deal is contracted. A book that went from $455bn to $523bn [s] is not a pipeline or a letter of intent; it is signed revenue obligation, funded at investment grade rather than through the secured structures the neoclouds use [s].

The delivery model is also lighter than it looks. Oracle leases rather than owns the flagship Stargate site: the $7.1bn JPMorgan-led construction loan for the second phase at Abilene went to the Crusoe–Blue Owl joint venture developing the campus, not to Oracle and not to OpenAI [s]. Someone else's balance sheet carries the shell.

And Oracle has the cover CoreWeave lacks. It runs the same buy-and-rent economics with a second, unrelated software business absorbing a bad quarter — which is precisely the structural difference the cloud layer identifies between hyperscalers and pure plays.

4.The bear case

Concentration is worse than at any hyperscaler. Roughly 57% of the forward book rests on a counterparty whose annualized revenue run rate exited 2025 above $20bn [e], against a $300bn five-year obligation [s]. Oracle does not need OpenAI to fail for this to hurt; it needs only a renegotiation.

The timing is adverse in both directions. Capex is being spent now and revenue begins in 2027 [s] — and the first year of spending already overshot its own guidance by about 11% [s].

The surrounding paper is also thinner than the headlines. Stargate's founding equity split — SoftBank and OpenAI reported at $19bn each for 40%, with Oracle and MGX near $7bn each — was never confirmed by the parties and does not internally reconcile [e]. Oracle's participation in the structure its largest contract depends on is, publicly, an unconfirmed report.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether Oracle discloses the as-of quarter and the customer composition of the $523bn RPO. The corpus cannot presently pin the quarter, and one contract plausibly accounts for more than half of it. A concentration disclosure would settle the single most important question on this page.
  2. 02Whether FY2027 capex guidance is set above the FY2026 actual of $55.7bn or below it. Above is a re-underwriting of the OpenAI build; below is a retreat, and the first credible sign that a hyperscale contract can be delivered more slowly than signed.
  3. 03Whether the contracted OpenAI capacity begins delivering in 2027 as reported. Delivery is the moment RPO starts converting into revenue, and Oracle has been spending against it for two years already.
  4. 04Whether the next funding tranche arrives as unsecured bonds like the $18bn September 2025 issue, or moves to secured and special-purpose structures. CoreWeave's March 2026 facility shows what the market charges when the collateral is named.
  5. 05Whether any part of the $300bn contract is re-scoped, re-priced, or re-phased. It is five years and 4.5 GW against a counterparty whose own commitments run about eight years, so a timing change on either side propagates straight into Oracle's book.
