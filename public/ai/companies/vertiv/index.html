Vertiv

The counterpoint to the integrators: power and thermal content earns roughly six times an ODM's margin inside the very same rack.

03 Datacenter Physical07 Systems & Integration

Vertiv is the answer to the question the systems layer keeps dodging. In the first quarter of 2026 the companies bolting AI racks together earned operating margins of 1.8 to 3.6 percent while the thermal, power and connector suppliers into those same racks earned 17.8 to 25.4 percent [s]. Vertiv is on the profitable side of that line, selling into the identical demand.

1.Role in the stack

Coolant distribution units, sidecars, busway, switchgear and UPS — the power train and thermal loop that a 120 kW liquid-cooled rack cannot function without. Commercially it sits ahead of the data center layer rather than inside it: this equipment is ordered before the shell is finished, which makes Vertiv's order book the earliest hard read on how much capacity is actually being built.

FY2025 net sales were $10.2bn against $8.01bn in 2024, with 58% transacted in the Americas [s]. Operating margin went from 4.9% in 2020 to 17.9% in 2025 on revenue up from $4.37bn to $10.23bn [s] — against the 1.8 to 3.6 percent the rack integrators reported in the first quarter of 2026 [s]. Estimated combined order backlog reached $15.0bn at the end of 2025, from $7.2bn a year earlier [s]: about one and a half times annual revenue, with the 10-K stating that orders are being placed in advance of Vertiv's ability to fulfil them.

Q1 2026 net sales were $2,650m, up 30%, with 44% organic growth in the Americas and a 20.8% adjusted operating margin [s]. The margin expanded inclusive of tariff impacts, which is the single most direct pricing-power evidence in the corpus for this part of the stack — compare Supermicro, where tariffs became an $89.4m line item against $1,019m of quarterly gross profit [s].

3.The bull case

The rack-scale transition moved the profit pool and Vertiv is where part of it landed. A 120 kW rack carrying liquid coolant at two litres a second [s][s] is a purchase order for cooling and power content that the integrator does not make, and the same shift shows up independently at Asia Vital Components, whose operating margin went from 2.47% to 19.73% [s], and Delta, 7.66% to 15.13% [s]. Three companies, three countries, one direction.

4.The bear case

Backlog is not revenue, and Vertiv's own 10-K supplies the caveat: the majority is firm and expected to ship inside 12 to 18 months, and customers can reduce or defer, usually with penalties [s]. It also says orders are arriving ahead of what Vertiv can build — which is a demand signal and a delivery risk in one sentence.

The deeper problem is that nobody can net this backlog against the buildings it serves. Digital Realty had 1,402 MW under construction, 54% pre-leased [s]; Vertiv has $15.0bn of equipment on order [s]. Those may be the same demand counted from two sides, and no disclosure in either filing resolves it.

The named counterargument on the technology is Uptime Institute's Jacqueline Davis, who argued in April 2026 that direct liquid cooling will stay confined to high-density AI and HPC rather than reaching mainstream mission-critical adoption within three years [o], citing redundant CDU and manifold engineering, reduced ride-through time for generator start, and unresolved ownership of DLC components between facilities and IT teams. If she is right, the mainstream retrofit market is later than the order book implies. And the demand itself is borrowed from one layer down: 48% of surveyed data center experts named power availability as the most prominent obstacle to delivering projects on schedule [s]. Vertiv sells the equipment; it does not sell the interconnection date.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether backlog grows again in the FY2026 10-K or flattens near $15.0bn. The 10-K already says customers may reduce or defer orders, usually with penalties, so the first flat print — not a decline — is the signal that ordering ahead has stopped.
  2. 02Whether adjusted operating margin lands inside the guided 22.8-23.8% band. Q1 2026 delivered 20.8% inclusive of tariff impacts; landing in the band would be a second consecutive year of expansion while absorbing trade costs, which is what pricing power looks like in a filing.
  3. 03Whether the 58% Americas revenue share moves. Vertiv's growth is concentrated in one region's build cycle, and a shift would show either diversification or that US permitting friction has started to bite.
  4. 04Whether any disclosure lets Vertiv's $15.0bn backlog be reconciled against Digital Realty's 1,402 MW under construction. The two may describe the same buildings from opposite sides, and nothing in either filing nets them.
  5. 05Whether direct-liquid-cooling orders keep converting at the rate the backlog implies. Uptime's Jacqueline Davis argues DLC stays confined to high-density AI and HPC for three years; if she is right, the retrofit demand behind part of that backlog arrives late.
