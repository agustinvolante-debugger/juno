Huawei

China's answer to NVIDIA, running at scale on a stockpile it cannot replace — the question is what happens when it runs out.

05 Compute Silicon16 Capital & Policy04 Fab & Tooling

Huawei is the test of whether export controls work, and the honest answer from the corpus is: not the way either side claims. The company shipped serious volume in 2025. It shipped it largely by drawing down chips bought before the rules landed. Both halves are true, and most commentary picks one.

1.Role in the stack

Vertically integrated in a way no Western firm is: it designs the Ascend accelerator, builds the SuperPoD systems around it, sells the cloud, and courts the developers. That integration is a genuine advantage under sanctions — there is no supplier to cut off — and it is why Huawei, not SMIC, is the name that matters in China's AI silicon.

Huawei reported 2025 revenue of CNY 880.941 billion, about $126.0 billion, with R&D at 21.8 percent of revenue [s]. On accelerators, SemiAnalysis estimated 805,000 Ascend units shipped in 2025, up from 507,000 in 2024 [e].

Treat the mix inside that total with more caution than the total itself. Independent estimates cluster at 700,000–805,000 for total Ascend shipments, but the 910C share is genuinely unsettled — SemiAnalysis puts it at 653,000, Bernstein at roughly 350,000, and Huawei's own early-2025 plan at 100,000. Nobody should present the 653,000 as settled, and this page does not.

3.The bull case

The inputs were secured before the door closed. SemiAnalysis estimated Huawei had taken delivery of more than 2.9 million Ascend dies from TSMC [e], and that China had procured 13 million HBM stacks, enough for 1.6 million Ascend 910C packages, with Samsung supplying 11.4 million [e]. That is years of headroom bought in advance.

Domestic supply is not zero either. SMIC's monthly capacity passed one million 8-inch-equivalent wafers at end-2025 at 93.5 percent utilisation [s], and SemiAnalysis's conservative estimate put advanced-node capacity at 45,000 wafers per month, of which at most 20,000 was needed for Ascend [e].

And demand is now policy. Chinese guidance issued in November 2025 requires state-funded data centre projects to use only domestically made AI chips [s]. The clearest evidence that this bites is on the other side of the trade: NVIDIA booked $4,550 million from China-headquartered customers in the quarter ended April 2026, against $9,659 million a year earlier [s]. Beijing did more to close that market than Washington did.

4.The bear case

A stockpile is not a supply chain. The same estimate that credits Huawei with 2.9 million dies expected the bank to deplete within about nine months from September 2025 [e] — and CSIS separately puts the figure at around 2 million chiplets for an earlier Ascend generation, a different unit measuring a different thing, so the real range is a bracket rather than a number. Either way, replacement has to come from SMIC at yields nobody publishes.

The roadmap concedes where the constraint is. Huawei announced the Ascend 950PR for Q1 2026 and the 950DT for Q4 2026 with self-developed HBM [f] — memory is the thing it is trying to internalise, because that is what it cannot buy. Announcing it is not shipping it.

The ecosystem numbers are also company-reported with no published methodology: more than 4 million Ascend developers at end-2025 [e]. Read them as a claim, not a measurement.

One gap worth stating plainly: the corpus has Huawei's total revenue but no citable figure for its AI cloud business specifically, so this page cannot test whether silicon volume is translating into compounding cloud revenue. That is the question that would settle the bull case, and it is currently unanswerable here.

◆What to watch

Falsifiable indicators — each one resolves on a disclosure, not on an opinion.

  1. 01Whether Ascend shipments in 2026 exceed 2025's without a new source of leading-edge dies. The 2025 volume ran on a pre-controls TSMC stockpile that SemiAnalysis expected to deplete within about nine months of September 2025; if volume holds through 2026, the stockpile thesis is wrong and domestic capacity is larger than estimated.
  2. 02Whether the Ascend 950DT ships in Q4 2026 with self-developed HBM, as announced. Memory is the binding constraint, so shipping on domestic HBM would matter more than the FLOPS number attached to it.
  3. 03Whether any independent estimate converges with SemiAnalysis's 653,000 910C figure. It currently sits roughly 2x Bernstein's and 6.5x Huawei's own early-2025 plan — the total is far better established than the mix.
  4. 04Whether SMIC's disclosed capacity and utilisation move enough to supply Ascend at volume. SMIC does not break out advanced-node capacity, so this resolves only through inference on total capacity plus yield commentary.
