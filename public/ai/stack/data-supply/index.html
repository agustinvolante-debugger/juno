Data Supply

Licensing, labeling, synthetic data, RL environments

Every other layer in this stack buys something built on purpose. This one buys other people's work: text written for human readers, judgment sitting inside someone's head, and the labour of pointing at what is correct.

Both ends of that supply repriced at once, in about eighteen months. The free end is being fenced off — 45 percent of the C4 web corpus is now restricted once terms of service are counted [s]. The paid end stopped being piecework and became credentialled experts billing above $85 an hour [s].

1.What this layer is

Three inputs, one destination. Scraped corpora — the web, crawled. Human-generated data — labels, preference rankings, demonstrations, graded answers, and the reinforcement-learning environments that produce them. Synthetic data — tokens a model writes for another model to learn from.

The physical anchor is two objects that have nothing in common. One is a plain text file on a web server that tells a crawler no. The other is a physician at a kitchen table, paid by the hour, marking which of two model answers would have hurt the patient.

That second object is new. The labelling industry was built on the first kind of worker — high volume, low skill, priced per task. The reinforcement-learning era buys the second kind, and the price per hour is not in the same universe.

The unit used to be the task. It is now the hour, and the hour is expensive. Mercor reached $2bn of annualized gross revenue in June 2026, up from a $760m run-rate at end-2025 [s]. Contractors keep 60 to 70 percent of that top line at average rates above $85 an hour, with over $2m of payouts a day [s].

Net that out and the business is roughly $600–800m annualized — but read the qualifier before using the number. The 60–70 percent share and the $85 rate are Sacra estimates with no independent corroborator; TechCrunch independently confirms only the scale of the payouts, not the split. Every netting exercise on this layer, including that one, rests on a single uncorroborated ratio.

The older economics ran the other way. Scale AI reported $870m of 2024 revenue against a $1.5bn annualized run-rate at end-2024 and an estimated $2bn in 2025 [e], priced per completed task at a markup. The corpus holds those per-task price points in a source note rather than as citable facts, so this page will not print them; the direction is what matters, and the direction is from cents to dollars-per-minute-of-expert-attention.

3.The players

Scale AI is the wounded incumbent. Meta invested $14.3bn for a 49 percent non-voting stake at a $29bn valuation in June 2025 [s], which is the single most consequential event in this layer's history and not for the reason the headline implies.

Surge AI is the quiet one, and the least verifiable. Sacra estimates $1.2bn of annualized revenue in 2024, bootstrapped and profitable since its 2021 launch [e]. No independent source for that figure exists — Surge is private and discloses nothing, and every restatement traces back to Sacra. In July 2025 it was reported in talks for its first outside capital, up to $1bn, at valuations reported between $15bn and $25bn [e]. Twelve months on, no close has been reported. Surge is not a $25bn company; it is a company that was reported to be in talks.

Mercor is the RL-era entrant. It raised a $350m Series C at a $10bn valuation in October 2025 [s] and quadrupled gross revenue in the eight months after. Sacra describes it as free-cash-flow profitable in early 2026, and names OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta as customers — which is also the problem.

4.Concentration & chokepoints

The buyer side is three or four companies. Every vendor in this layer sells the same product to the same frontier labs, and the labs are the only entities that need data at this scale. There is no long tail to fall back on.

The chokepoint, though, is not scale or skill. It is neutrality, and June 2025 demonstrated what happens when it breaks. Google — which spent about $150m with Scale in 2024 and had planned $200m for 2025 — moved to split from Scale after the Meta investment [s].

Now the correction, because this claim is widely told wrong and the corpus told it wrong too. "OpenAI departed Scale" is false, and was contradicted by the very CNBC report the claim was sourced to: OpenAI's CFO said the company would keep working with Scale as one of several data vendors. Google's move is reported intent, not a completed exit. Microsoft was reported to be pulling back; xAI was described as looking to leave. The dollar figures here are corroborated. The completeness of the exodus is not, and any page that lists four departures is inventing three of them.

What survives is the mechanism, and one hard number carries it. In July 2025 — one month after the Meta stake — Scale laid off 200 full-time employees, about 14 percent of staff, and ended roughly 500 contractor relationships [s]. That is the evidence for "neutrality was the moat," and it is better evidence than the departure list, because it is a measured consequence rather than a reported intention. A labelling vendor's asset is that it is not aligned with any lab; an investor can damage that asset simply by buying in, and the damage shows up in the payroll within a quarter.

5.Lead times & capacity

There is no turbine queue here. The two constraints are permitted text and credentialled humans.

Text is running out by enclosure, not exhaustion. Between April 2023 and April 2024, 5 percent or more of all C4 tokens and 28 percent or more of tokens from its most actively maintained critical sources became robots.txt-restricted, rising to 45 percent of C4 once terms of service are included [s] — a multi-thousand-domain audit across three major corpora, and the cleanest measurement of the enclosure that exists. Then Cloudflare, which says it serves about 20 percent of the web, made AI-crawler blocking the default for new domains on 1 July 2025 [s]. That is a company claim about its own footprint, and it is the first time the default flipped from open to closed.

The escape valve is synthetic. DeepSeek-R1's reinforcement-learning phase generated approximately 2 trillion tokens on its own [e], against the 14.8 trillion tokens of scraped text used to pretrain V3 [s] — one RL run self-generating a mid-teens percentage of an entire pretraining corpus .

forecast · Epoch AIThe indexed web holds roughly 500 trillion text tokens, and at 4x annual compute growth the text-only data constraint binds about five years from 2024 [e]. Multimodal and synthetic sources are the named escape valves; neither has a measured quality ceiling in this corpus.

6.2019 → today

2019. Bounding boxes for autonomous vehicles. Data is free to take, labour is piecework, and the winning vendor is whoever manages the largest low-cost workforce.

2022–23. RLHF makes human preference the product. Human judgment, not human labelling, becomes the input frontier labs cannot synthesise, and the labelling vendors become strategic suppliers.

2025–26. Three things break at once. Consent closes — 45 percent of C4 restricted [s], crawler blocking on by default. Labour repriced upward to expert hours above $85 [s]. And neutrality stops being free: $14.3bn buys 49 percent of the incumbent [s], and a month later that incumbent cuts 200 staff and around 500 contractors [s].

7.Commoditization pressure

The bottom of this layer already commoditized. Per-task annotation is a managed-labour business with no defensible position, which is why the incumbent's growth slowed while the market it serves grew.

The top is moving the other way. Expert hours are getting more expensive, not less, because the supply of physicians and quantitative analysts willing to grade model outputs is genuinely finite. That is the rarest thing in this stack: a price going up.

The real pressure is not competition, it is disintermediation. Two forces route around the vendors entirely — synthetic data, which substitutes generated tokens for purchased ones at the margin, and in-housing, of which Meta's 49 percent stake is the most expensive example anyone has attempted. Neither has produced a measured quality limit in this corpus, which is precisely why the layer's medium-term shape is unresolved.

8.Failure modes

The neutrality break. Demonstrated, once, at a cost of one large customer. Blast radius: the vendor, not the stack. Any lab can switch suppliers in a quarter.

Buyer concentration. Three or four buyers, each large enough to be a majority of a vendor's revenue and each capable of building the capability internally. A single in-housing decision removes a vendor's growth story.

Gross-versus-net. Marketplace revenue at 60–70 percent pass-through [s] is not software revenue, and valuations struck on the gross line assume a take rate the corpus cannot verify.

Consent and licensing. The corpus has no data-licensing deal figures at all — not one publisher contract, not one dollar amount. Enclosure is measured; the price of the permission that replaces it is not. That is a real hole in this page, and it is where a cost shock would appear first.

Contamination. If synthetic data degrades models trained on it, the escape valve closes. There is no measurement of this in the corpus either way.

9.Open questions

  • What is Surge actually worth? Reported talks at $15–25bn in July 2025 [e], no reported close twelve months later. Either the round was repriced or it was never as firm as reported; the corpus cannot say which.
  • Does the expert-hour price hold? $85+ an hour [s] could be a durable skill premium or a shortage premium that collapses as more credentialled people discover the work exists.
  • How much of Mercor's $2bn gross run-rate does Mercor keep? [s] The netting rests entirely on one Sacra-only ratio. A disclosed take rate would settle the layer's most important number.
  • Did the labs actually leave Scale? One intent, one continuing relationship, two reported pullbacks. The corpus cannot close this and the version circulating publicly is wrong — what it can show is the July 2025 cut of 200 staff and ~500 contractors [s], which is the consequence regardless of who stayed.
  • Is synthetic data a substitute or a supplement? 2 trillion tokens from one RL run [e] shows the volume is achievable. Nothing here shows what it costs in model quality.

◆The verdict

evidence-led verdict · updated 2026-07-25

Fairly priced — Real revenue at real margins, and the only layer in the stack where the labour got a raise. But the equity is priced on gross billings that the vendors mostly pass straight through.
