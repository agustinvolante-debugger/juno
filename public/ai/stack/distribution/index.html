Distribution & GTM

Marketplaces, SIs, incumbent bundling, agent commerce

The rake is not the moat. The accounting is.

AWS charges 1.5% on private offers of $10m or more and on every renewal [s], and in June 2026 it cut the fee on professional services private offers to 0.5% [s]. Meanwhile 100% of the pretax amount of an eligible partner purchase made through Microsoft Marketplace decrements the customer's Azure consumption commitment [s]. Only one of those changes a procurement decision, and it is not the one with the fee schedule.

1.What this layer is

Four rails carrying AI software to a buyer. Hyperscaler marketplaces move the transaction and, more importantly, the procurement paperwork. The system integrators sell the implementation labour. Incumbent bundling attaches AI to a renewal the customer was signing anyway — Copilot, Agentforce, Now Assist. And a fourth exists on paper only: agent commerce, two protocols with no disclosed volume.

The anchor to picture is not a product. It is a line item on a cloud bill the enterprise has already committed to spend.

Say the gap out loud, because it is large: this layer has no reseller economics. Microsoft states plainly that its Marketplace cannot specify or pay reseller margin, so partner margins are arranged outside Microsoft's systems, while its Cloud Solution Provider network runs to tens of thousands of organizations [s]. There is no rate to publish because Microsoft pays none through that rail. Google Cloud Marketplace's take rate could not be verified from a primary page at all, and this page does not assume parity [s].

AWS charges 3% on public software-as-a-service offers and 20% on public server offers including machine images and containers [s]. That near-sevenfold spread means a vendor's packaging decision — hosted service or customer-run container — changes its platform tax more than any negotiation could. Then the rake regresses toward zero exactly where the money is: private offers fall from 3% below $1m of total contract value to 2% between $1m and $10m and 1.5% at or above $10m, with all renewals at 1.5% [s]. Professional services private offers went from 2.5% to 0.5% in June 2026 [s] — an 80% cut on new offers, readable as a bid to pull integrator implementation work onto the AWS transaction rail. Microsoft charges a 3% standard store service fee with no listing fee, on an agency model across 141 locations and 17 transaction currencies [s].

A marketplace taking 1.5% of a large contract is not exercising App Store pricing power. It is running a payments-and-procurement rail near cost and buying something else with the forgone margin.

3.The players

Accenture is this layer's cautionary tale about metrics, not about demand. It recorded $5.9bn of generative-AI new bookings in fiscal 2025 [s], roughly 7.3% of $80.62bn of total new bookings in a year when total bookings fell 1% [e]. Read that as a terminal value, not a point in a series: Accenture disclosed no generative-AI bookings or revenue figure in any of its three fiscal-2026 quarterly releases, the terms appearing zero times in each [s], and third-quarter bookings were $19.32bn, down 2% [s]. The bear reading is that a company retires a growth metric when it stops flattering. The steelman is Accenture's own: generative AI stops being separable once AI sits inside most transformation programmes, and the mega-deal counts that replaced it may proxy the same demand better. Either way, nothing on this site may present the $5.9bn as current.

Capgemini is the only major integrator still publishing an AI share — more than 8% of group bookings in 2025 and more than 10% in the fourth quarter, on €24,356m of bookings [s], stated as a floor and above Accenture's terminal figure. Everyone else publishes positioning. Infosys reported fiscal 2026 revenue of $20,158m, up 3.1% in constant currency, with $14.9bn of large-deal contract value, and no AI-specific revenue, bookings or contract-value figure anywhere in the release [s] — while describing itself as an AI-first consulting firm. Cognizant reported trailing-twelve-month bookings of $29.6bn, up 11%, and headcount of 357,600, up 21,300, with no AI figure either [s].

The incumbents disclose more, in units of their own choosing. Microsoft said its AI business surpassed a $37bn annual revenue run rate, up 123%, in the quarter ended March 2026 [s] — a run rate whose composition is not broken out and whose product definition Microsoft has never published — against commercial remaining performance obligations of $625bn, up 110% [s], and Microsoft Cloud revenue of $51.5bn in the December quarter [s]. The loudest Copilot number is the least formal: Satya Nadella said Microsoft 365 Copilot had more than 20 million paid enterprise seats, up from 15 million a quarter earlier [s] — on the earnings call, absent from the press release, which is why the corpus holds it at medium confidence. Seats sold, not seats used.

At the $30 per user per month list price those seats imply roughly $7.2bn of annualised list value, about 19% of the disclosed $37bn [e]. That is this site's derivation and a ceiling, not a measurement: volume agreements are discounted, bundling shifts attribution between SKUs, and Microsoft has never confirmed Copilot sits inside the run rate as defined. It bounds the distance between the loud number and the quiet one.

Salesforce reported $1.2bn of Agentforce annual recurring revenue, up 205%, inside a combined Agentforce and Data 360 figure of nearly $3.4bn that includes $1.1bn of acquired Informatica Cloud ARR [s] — read the composition, not the headline. ServiceNow disclosed no absolute Now Assist contract value at all, reporting subscription revenue of $3,877m instead [s]. Adobe said AI-first ARR tripled and passed $500m against $6.62bn of quarterly revenue [s] — the smallest disclosed AI share of the three and the fastest-growing.

4.Concentration & chokepoints

The chokepoint is eligibility, and the platform writes the rules. Microsoft's benefit is genuinely powerful: 100% of the pretax purchase amount of an Azure-benefit-eligible partner offer bought through Marketplace decrements the customer's Azure Consumption Commitment [s]. For a CIO already carrying that commitment, buying through the marketplace is a zero-marginal-cash decision and buying direct is a budget request.

The conditions are the moat and they travel with the mechanism. The purchase must go through Marketplace inside the Azure portal on a subscription tied to the agreement, so credit-card purchases on the public marketplace do not count; Azure prepayment is ineligible; the licence must be used exclusively in Azure, so hybrid or on-premises deployment breaks eligibility; professional services are ineligible; reseller-routed deals close outside Marketplace and do not decrement the commitment at all; and to qualify in the first place an offer must attain Azure IP co-sell status [s]. The vendor's incentive is to become co-sell eligible; the platform's is to keep that tied to Azure-exclusive deployment. Note the two hyperscalers moving opposite ways on services: AWS cut its services fee to 0.5% to pull implementation work onto its rail [s], while Microsoft excludes services from drawdown entirely [s].

5.Lead times & capacity

The queue in this layer is procurement, and it is pre-paid: Microsoft's $625bn of commercial remaining performance obligations, up 110% [s], is the size of the committed pool drawdown mechanics act on.

The bypass is bottom-up adoption. Menlo Ventures found 27% of enterprise AI application spend arriving through product-led growth motions in 2025 against 7% in traditional software [s], and startups capturing 63% of enterprise AI application spend, up from 36% a year earlier [e]. Both come from one proprietary survey of 495 buyers, unreplicated, published by an investor with an incentive to emphasise exactly that shift. Flagged rather than discounted — it is the only measurement of the bypass the corpus has.

6.2019 → today

There is no pre-2024 anchor in the corpus for this layer, so the honest version is two snapshots and a warning.

2024. Marketplace software sales are a $30bn base [f] and AWS's simplified schedule takes effect in January at 3% for SaaS, 20% for server offers [s]. 2025. The AI metrics arrive: Accenture books $5.9bn [s], Capgemini passes 8% of bookings [s], two agent-payment protocols launch thirteen days apart in September [s][s].

2026. They start disappearing. Accenture drops the disclosure for three straight quarters [s], ServiceNow reports a 2030 target instead of a current figure [s], and AWS cuts its services fee by 80% [s]. The channel got cheaper and less legible in the same year.

7.Commoditization pressure

The rake is already commoditized. At 1.5% on the deals that matter [s] and 0.5% on services [s], marketplaces are procurement rails priced near cost, and the growth they expect is in volume rather than in take.

forecast · Omdia, October 2025 Omdia forecasts enterprise software sales through hyperscaler cloud marketplaces rising from $30bn in 2024 to $163bn by 2030, a 29.1% compound annual growth rate over 2025–2030 [f]. A named research shop's projection, not a measurement — and the corpus has no primary figure for current marketplace volume at all, because every available estimate traces to marketplace-tooling vendors' own content marketing.

The services half is where compression is live rather than forecast, and the executives are the ones saying so.

forecast · C. Vijayakumar, chief executive, HCLTech, April 2026 HCLTech's chief executive said AI deflation would reduce revenue by three to five percent in the coming year, in the same reporting round in which Tata Consultancy Services reported a 0.5% annual revenue decline [o]. Note what the claim is about: price, not volume.

The counterweight is headcount. Capgemini employed 423,400 people at the end of 2025, up 82,300 or 24%, in the year generative AI passed 8% of its bookings [s] — the WNS acquisition is part of that, so it is not a clean organic signal — and Cognizant added 21,300 [s]. Whatever AI is doing to this channel, it is not yet collapsing the labour model.

8.Failure modes

Measurement failure, already happening. When the layer's flagship metric is withdrawn [s] and the second-largest US-listed integrator never published one [s], AI demand through the services channel becomes unauditable from outside. The blast radius is analytical and it lands on everyone downstream of the number.

Eligibility risk. A vendor whose pipeline depends on committed-spend drawdown is exposed to conditions it does not control — co-sell status, Azure-exclusive deployment, the exclusion of services and of reseller-routed deals [s]. A rules change is a go-to-market change. And because reseller-routed and drawdown-eligible deals are mutually exclusive for the same contract while Microsoft publishes no marketplace reseller margin at all [s], vendors are choosing between two channels with published economics for one.

Agent commerce with no denominator. Stripe and OpenAI launched the Agentic Commerce Protocol in September 2025 with Etsy as first merchant, disclosing no transaction volume, no fee schedule and no governance body [s]; Google announced the Agent Payments Protocol thirteen days earlier with more than 60 partner organizations and no volume either [s]. Ten months on, no agent-commerce volume figure exists in any primary source. Against that silence the routing is already measurable: Adobe Analytics reported AI-referred traffic to US retail sites up 138% year over year in May 2026, converting 54% better than non-AI traffic [s] — Adobe's proprietary panel, and Adobe sells AI marketing tooling, so the incentive is real.

9.Open questions

  • What share of marketplace volume is drawdown-driven? The mechanism is documented in full [s] and its usage is disclosed nowhere, so the Omdia trajectory [f] cannot be attributed to a cause. Google Cloud Marketplace's own take rate is also unverifiable from any primary page, and the site will not assume it matches the 3% AWS and Microsoft both publish [s][s].
  • Is Accenture's silence a measurement problem or a demand problem? [s] Bookings fell 2% in the quarter AI consulting was supposedly compounding [s], which is suggestive and not decisive.
  • Do Copilot seats convert to revenue? 20 million paid seats [s] against a derived $7.2bn list-value ceiling [e] is the widest gap between a loud number and a quiet one in this layer.
  • Did agent commerce actually ship? Two protocols, more than 60 named partners, zero disclosed volume [s][s]. Reporting that one was already retired in favour of dedicated retailer apps came only from inadmissible sources, so the site records the question and no answer.

◆The verdict

evidence-led verdict · updated 2026-07-25

Fairly priced — The platform tax is already near zero: 1.5% on large private offers, 0.5% on services. What moves an enterprise purchase is committed-spend drawdown, not the rake. Priced fairly, measured badly.
