Middleware & Agent Infra

Orchestration, MCP, retrieval, evals, observability, sandboxes

Every bear case for this layer says the model will eat it. The evidence says something narrower and more useful: what gets absorbed is token-marshalling — routing, retrieval, sandboxing, prompt plumbing — and what survives is anything that makes a guarantee about state.

The sharpest datapoint is not a startup death. OpenAI announced Agent Builder, a visual canvas for multi-step agent workflows, in October 2025, and has scheduled it for shutdown on 30 November 2026 [s]. A frontier lab shipped a first-party competitor to the orchestration products in this layer and switched it off inside a year. There are two honest readings and this page carries both.

1.What this layer is

The plumbing between a model API and something that runs in production: orchestration, retrieval, sandboxing, evals and observability, plus the connector standard tying tools to models.

That last piece is the only part with a countable population, so it serves as the anchor. The Model Context Protocol had more than 10,000 active public servers and more than 97 million monthly SDK downloads as of December 2025 [s] — Anthropic's own count, corroborated on a property Anthropic controls, with client support in ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and VS Code. Adoption by direct competitors is the durable part. Then Anthropic gave the spec away: MCP became a founding project of the Agentic AI Foundation, a Linux Foundation directed fund co-founded with Block and OpenAI, in December 2025 [s]. Owning the connector standard is no longer available as a moat, which removes a risk and a differentiator at once.

Almost everything here meters a derivative of token throughput one layer down. Orchestration bills on agent steps: Temporal Cloud's entry plan is $100 a month including one million Actions, with marginal Actions from $50 down to $25 per million at volume [s]. Observability bills on traces under four different names — Arize AX Pro at $50 a month for 50,000 spans [s], Braintrust Pro at $249 for 50,000 scores [s], Langfuse at $8 per 100,000 units of overage graduating to $6 above 50 million [s], and Weights & Biases at $0.10 per megabyte of Weave ingestion above a 1.5 gigabyte allowance [s]. Retrieval bills on storage and reads: Pinecone at a $50 monthly minimum, $16–18 per million read units and $0.33 per gigabyte per month [s], with Chroma at an identical $0.33 headline [s]. Where the product is a user interface it bills on seats — LangSmith Plus at $39 per seat per month [s].

Two vendors price in units designed to defeat comparison: turbopuffer publishes monthly minimums of $16, $256 and $4,096, the top tier carrying a 35% usage premium, and no per-unit rate at all [s], while LlamaCloud sells credits at $1.25 per thousand [s]. Opacity is a pricing strategy in this sub-segment, and worth naming rather than modelling around.

Now the price they compete against. Gemini File Search charges $0.15 per million tokens to index once, and nothing for storage or query-time embedding generation [s]. Pinecone's entire meter is what Google gives away. Sandboxes are the same story: Anthropic grants 1,550 free container-hours per organization per month, then $0.05 per container-hour, and charges nothing when code execution runs alongside web search or web fetch — the most common agent pattern there is [s]; OpenAI charges $0.03 for a 20-minute 1 GB session rising to $1.92 at 64 GB [s]. Against that, E2B Pro is $150 a month plus usage [s], Daytona bills $0.0858 per vCPU-hour [s], and Modal charges a threefold markup over its own standard compute rate for the sandbox wrapper around the same silicon [e]. Cloudflare's move is bundling rather than price: 25 GiB-hours of container memory and 375 vCPU-minutes arrive inside a Workers plan the customer already pays for [s].

What the corpus does not have, and this page will not fabricate: no framework revenue. Not LangChain, not LlamaIndex, not CrewAI. Zero disclosed revenue figures for the sub-segment that gets the most attention.

3.The players

Orchestration. Temporal is the strongest survival case in the layer and it is not an AI company. It raised $146m at a $1.72bn post-money valuation in March 2025 [s], disclosing 184% net dollar retention, more than 2,500 Cloud customers and 4.4 times revenue growth over the preceding 18 months [s] — company-selected metrics in a funding announcement, unaudited. Note what they are claims about: durable execution is an operational guarantee, not a model capability. LangChain, by contrast, was valued at $1.25bn in an October 2025 Series B of $125m led by IVP [s], with no company-disclosed revenue figure in the corpus to set against it. CrewAI publishes no list price above a free tier capped at 50 workflow executions a month, and quotes its enterprise plan in 50 hours of development per month [s] — at the top tier the monetized unit is services time, not software.

Evals and observability. Braintrust raised $80m led by ICONIQ at a reported $800m valuation in February 2026, on $121m raised in total [s] — the valuation appears in press coverage rather than the company's own post, hence medium confidence. It discloses no ARR, customer count or volume metric anywhere, so no multiple can be computed, and this page says so instead of estimating one. Arize raised a $70m Series C with M12, Datadog and PagerDuty participating [s]: the two APM incumbents most exposed to AI observability bought an option rather than building. And the category's biggest name left — CoreWeave completed its acquisition of Weights & Biases, with roughly a million users, in May 2025 in a transaction reported at $1.7bn [e].

Retrieval. Pinecone's founder Edo Liberty moved from chief executive to chief scientist in September 2025, with Ash Ashutosh taking the CEO role [s]. The competitive set Ashutosh named is the substance — AWS, Google Cloud, Oracle and Databricks, platforms that added vector search as a feature — and he said acquisition is not on the table, which is the on-record answer to every claim that Pinecone is for sale. On revenue there is one number and it is weak: Latka estimates $26.6m of ARR for 2025, unchanged from 2024, against a $750m valuation that is the April 2023 mark [e]. Latka is a modelled, self-reported database; naming that is better than repeating the figure anonymously, which is what most commentary here does.

4.Concentration & chokepoints

The chokepoint is a price list, not a supply chain. Three vendors set the marginal cost of every primitive in this layer, and a sandbox session from them runs between three cents and free [s][s]. A standalone vendor's floor is somebody else's rounding error.

The second chokepoint is measurement. The 10,000-server figure is Anthropic's own count corroborated on Anthropic's own property [s], and third-party registry counts diverge by an order of magnitude — every source for them was an inadmissible aggregator. A layer nobody can count is a layer nobody can size, including the people raising against it.

5.Lead times & capacity

There is no queue here, and pretending otherwise would be the easiest dishonest paragraph on this site. Capacity is a line in a pricing table: E2B Pro allows up to 100 concurrent sandboxes and 24-hour sessions [s]; Cloudflare's included allowance is 375 vCPU-minutes a month [s]. What doubles when demand doubles is the bill — and because the meters are derivatives of step count and trace volume [s][s], that is why revenue in this layer can grow while its pricing power does not.

6.2019 → today

The corpus has no pre-2025 anchor here, so the three-snapshot treatment used elsewhere on this site does not apply. What it has instead is an absorption timeline compressed into about a year.

November 2025. Anthropic's code-execution-with-MCP example cut agent token consumption from 150,000 to 2,000, a 98.7% reduction [s]; three weeks later its Tool Search Tool cut token usage 85% and Programmatic Tool Calling took a complex research task from 43,588 to 27,297 tokens [s]. Both are company-published benchmarks, not independent replication. In between, Google launched File Search with free storage and free query-time embeddings [s].

December 2025 to now. MCP governance leaves Anthropic for the Agentic AI Foundation [s], Anthropic makes code execution free when bundled with web search [s], Google cuts grounded search from $35 to $14 per thousand queries in one model generation [s], and OpenAI schedules its own orchestrator for shutdown [s]. Every one of those events lowers what a third party can charge.

7.Commoditization pressure

Here is the counterintuitive part, and it runs against how this layer is usually argued. Evals and observability is the least durable sub-segment, not the most. The durability case is that as models improve the bottleneck moves to knowing whether your system works, which is customer-specific and therefore unabsorbable. Demand, granted. Pricing power, no: four vendors meter near-identical units within roughly one order of magnitude [s][s][s], one of them is open-source-core and therefore sets the ceiling for the rest [s], and the category's most valuable asset ended up as a differentiator on a GPU cloud's bill [e]. Note the direction of that last one: absorption from below, by the compute vendor, not from above by the model — a different failure mode than the one the bear case names.

Retrieval is deflating fastest, and inside the API rather than against it: grounded search went from $35 to $14 per thousand queries in one model generation [s]. No standalone vendor's cost base moves at that rate.

The exception has a shape, and it is not an AI-native one. Temporal's claim is about retries, timers and state that survives a crash [s]. A better model does not make a workflow idempotent. The durable businesses in this layer are the ones that would have existed without LLMs.

8.Failure modes

Absorption of the marshalling layer. Tool routing, tool registries and prompt plumbing were products; within three weeks in November 2025 two of them became features [s][s]. Blast radius is confined to vendors whose value-add sits between the model and its tools.

Bundling, which is quieter than absorption and works better. Sandboxing arrives inside a Workers plan already being paid for [s] and free alongside a search call [s]. Nobody has to win on product to win here.

Revenue coupled to upstream prices. Because the meters are derivatives of token and step volume [s][s], a price cut one layer down is a revenue cut in this one [s].

Valuation without a denominator. Braintrust at a reported $800m discloses no revenue metric [s]; LangChain at $1.25bn discloses none either [s]. When three companies are setting the layer's marginal price toward zero, an unpriceable private mark is the fragility rather than the funding.

9.Open questions

  • Is Agent Builder's shutdown a verdict on orchestration, or on model-locked orchestration? [s] The bear reading is that the lab concluded the model handles decomposition. The steelman is that a first-party tool tied to one model family is simply a worse product than a neutral one — and Temporal's retention is the evidence for it [s].
  • What is anyone's revenue? The corpus holds no disclosed ARR for a single independent vendor in this layer. Its one figure is a tier-3 model for Pinecone [e], and nothing should be load-bearing on it.
  • Does durable execution stay outside the model, and does open-source-core cap everything else? [s] The layer's one non-absorbable thesis rests on unaudited metrics from a single vendor, and Langfuse's published schedule already ceilings closed vendors on traces [s] — whether that dynamic reaches orchestration and retrieval is untested.
  • Who counts MCP? [s] One organization publishes the number, third-party counts diverge by an order of magnitude, and the body now governing the spec publishes none [s].
