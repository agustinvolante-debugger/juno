Memory & Interconnect

HBM, NVLink, InfiniBand vs Ethernet, optics

DRAM is the part of the stack everyone had filed under "commodity, cyclical, low margin."

In the first quarter of 2026, SK hynix reported a 72% operating margin [s]. That is the layer's headline and it should be uncomfortable. It is also where the discourse around this number goes wrong, so the page will be pedantic about it once, up front: the comparison everyone reaches for is against NVIDIA's operating line, and NVIDIA's operating margin is not in this corpus. Setting 72% against a gross margin would be inventing the number that makes the point.

1.What this layer is

Two things a rack cannot work without, both sold by the gigabyte-second and the bit-per-second rather than by the FLOP.

Memory is HBM stacked beside the accelerator die and DRAM in the host server. Google's Ironwood TPU carries 192 GB of HBM per chip at 7.37 TB/s [s] — that per-accelerator content is the demand driver for the whole HBM squeeze. Interconnect is the fabric: NVLink inside the rack, Ethernet or InfiniBand between racks, optics between rows. NVLink 6 moves 260 TB/s of all-to-all bandwidth inside one Vera Rubin NVL72 rack [s].

The physical anchor: a stack of DRAM dies bonded vertically, sitting a few millimeters from the compute die on the same package, because the distance the electron travels is the thing being bought.

SK hynix's Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 52,576.3bn, up 198% year over year and 60% quarter over quarter [s], at a 72% operating margin on operating profit of KRW 37,610.3bn [s]. For the prior full year, revenue was KRW 97,146.7bn at a 49% operating margin [s]. The margin went from very good to unprecedented in four quarters, and it was 58% as recently as the quarter before.

For scale on the other side of the package, NVIDIA's GAAP gross margin in the overlapping quarter was 74.9% [s]. That is a different line of the income statement and the site will not score the two against each other — the like-for-like figure does not exist in the corpus. What survives without any comparison at all is the level: 72% of revenue converted to operating profit, in memory.

Interconnect earns less per dollar of revenue but is growing just as fast. NVIDIA networking revenue was $14.8bn in the April 2026 quarter, up 199% [s] — larger, on its own, than AMD's entire data center segment at $5.775bn [s].

3.The players

SK hynix is the price-setter, and HBM is why: its HBM revenue more than doubled year over year in 2025 [s], though the absolute figure is not disclosed. It completed HBM4 mass production in September 2025 and moved to large-scale production in 2026 [s], and names custom HBM as the next differentiator — which is the tell: the "commodity" is being specified per customer.

NVIDIA is the interconnect incumbent, and it is now leading with Ethernet rather than InfiniBand. Spectrum-6, arriving in deployments in July 2026, is a 102.4 Tb/s Ethernet switch system, twice the previous generation's capacity, supporting pluggable and co-packaged optics [s].

forecast · Dell'Oro Group, July 2026 Dell'Oro forecasts the high-end router and aggregation switch market reaching $19bn by 2030, with cloud-provider spend growing at a 16% CAGR driven by AI infrastructure [f]. Dell'Oro is the author of that forecast, not a relay of someone else's — but it is still a forecast, and the corpus has no direct Ethernet-versus-InfiniBand share split to sit beside it.

The thinness is worth stating: this layer has the fewest usable facts of any deep-adjacent layer on the site. Samsung and Micron pricing, HBM share by vendor, and optics unit economics are gaps, not conclusions.

4.Concentration & chokepoints

Memory bandwidth is now the legal chokepoint as well as the physical one. The BIS rule effective 15 January 2026 caps licensable China and Macau advanced-computing exports at total DRAM bandwidth below 6,500 GB/s [s]. Bandwidth, not FLOPS, is the gate that separates an H200-class part from a Blackwell-class one — regulators went where the binding constraint actually is.

The demand side is concentrated by construction: HBM content per accelerator is fixed by the accelerator's design [s], so every incremental GPU or TPU ships with a pre-committed memory order. There is no substitution and no downgrade path.

5.Lead times & capacity

forecast · TrendForce, July 2026 Server DRAM contract prices are forecast to rise 13-18% quarter over quarter in Q3 2026 [f], with long-term agreements capping increases for some cloud buyers. Against that, total RDIMM bit supply is projected to grow 15-20% year over year in 2027 [f]. Both are TrendForce's own forecasts.

Put those two side by side and the layer's condition is legible. Prices are rising by mid-teens percentages per quarter while the supply answer is mid-teens percentages per year. That is what an underbuilt layer looks like: the response is real, scheduled, and an order of magnitude slower than the signal.

6.2019 → today

The corpus cannot write this section honestly and the page will not pretend otherwise. Its earliest memory anchor is full-year 2025 — a 49% operating margin [s] — and HBM4 reaching mass production in September 2025 [s]. The 2019 and 2022 DRAM troughs, which are the necessary comparison for judging whether 72% is a cycle or a regime [s], are not in the research. A Gate-3 pass on this layer should add them before anyone leans on the word "structural."

7.Commoditization pressure

This is the layer where the commoditization thesis is being tested in reverse. HBM began as a commodity product and is de-commoditizing: mass production of HBM4 [s], custom HBM as the stated next step, and margins that moved from 49% for 2025 to 72% in one quarter [s][s]. When a part gets specified per customer, it stops being a spot good.

The counterweight is the historical base rate rather than any current datapoint. DRAM has run this cycle repeatedly, and every prior peak margin was followed by a supply response — which is precisely what the 2027 bit-supply forecast describes [f], and why long-term agreements exist at all [f]. The honest reading is that HBM has partly escaped the cycle and server DRAM has not.

8.Failure modes

Memory as the binding input. Because HBM content per accelerator is fixed [s], a memory shortfall does not slow the stack proportionally — it caps accelerator shipments outright. Blast radius: every layer above, immediately, with no workaround at the design level.

The stockpile running out. SemiAnalysis estimated China procured 13 million HBM stacks, enough for 1.6 million Ascend 910C packages, with Samsung supplying 11.4 million of them [e] — purchases made before restrictions bit. That is a drawdown, not a domestic capability. What replaces it is the most consequential open number in this layer, and the corpus's only estimate for it sits behind a paywall and is therefore not on this site.

Price pass-through. Server DRAM rising 13-18% in a quarter [f] lands on the bill of materials of everyone building racks. Where long-term agreements cap it, the cost moves to whoever lacks one — smaller buyers first.

9.Open questions

  • Is 72% a cycle or a regime? [s] Without the 2019 and 2022 trough margins, the site cannot answer it, and neither can anyone quoting the number.
  • What does NVIDIA actually earn at the operating line? The comparison everyone wants to make against SK hynix needs a figure the corpus does not have; only the 74.9% gross margin is sourced [s].
  • Ethernet or InfiniBand, and in what proportion? NVIDIA is leading with a 102.4 Tb/s Ethernet switch [s], which is a signal about direction, not a share number.
  • How much of the 2027 supply response is HBM versus commodity DRAM? The 15-20% RDIMM bit-supply forecast [f] says nothing about HBM specifically, and the two markets are now pricing differently.
  • Can domestic Chinese HBM replace the stockpile? [e] The one available forecast is paywalled and single-source, so this page states the question and no number.
