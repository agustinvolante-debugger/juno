Fab & Tooling

ASML, TSMC, deposition/etch/metrology, advanced packaging

The narrowest pipe in the AI stack is not a chip. It is a machine, and there are about sixty-five of the good ones made per year [f].

Everything in the layers above is a claim on capacity that was allocated eighteen months ago. This is the layer where the queue actually lives — and over the last two years the queue has walked up-stack twice, from wafers to advanced packaging, and from packaging to the resin film the package is built on. The film is made by a company better known for seasoning.

1.What this layer is

Three businesses that the word "fab" hides.

The toolmakers sell the machines: ASML for lithography, Applied Materials and its peers for deposition, etch and metrology. The foundries — TSMC, Intel, Samsung, SMIC — buy those machines and sell wafer starts at a named process node. The packagers take finished dies and bond them, with memory, onto an organic substrate: TSMC's CoWoS line, plus the substrate and build-up film suppliers underneath it.

The physical anchor: a polished silicon disc the width of a dinner plate goes into a fab and comes out months later carrying a few dozen dies the size of a drink coaster. Each disc passes a lithography tool dozens of times. A single High-NA lithography tool costs roughly $380 million [e], and fewer than a dozen exist anywhere. The coaster is then glued to a slab of resin-coated laminate — the cheapest input in the chain and, right now, the one with the longest queue.

What is bought here is a wafer start at a node, and separately a slot on a packaging line. The unit is a 12-inch wafer. The price is not published.

TSMC's Q2 2026 revenue was $40.20bn, up 36.0% year over year [s], on 4.336 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers, up 16.6% [s]. Read those two numbers together and the layer's economics fall out: growth is carried by price, not volume. Gross margin was 67.7% [s], against 59.9% for full-year 2025, itself up from 56.1% in 2024 and 54.4% in 2023 [s]. Net profit was NT$706.56bn, up 77.4% [s].

Where the price goes: N2 wafers are expected to run above $30,000 each, against roughly $20,000 at N3 [e]. That is an estimate and must stay one — TSMC does not disclose wafer prices, and the figure has no primary behind it.

Where the cost goes: building a fab in the United States runs four to five times the Taiwan baseline. The figure is CFO Wendell Huang's, given to CNBC in July 2026, and the value here is the midpoint of his range [e].

And the margin that best explains where the chokepoint sits now is not a chipmaker's at all. Ajinomoto's Functional Materials segment, which makes the ABF build-up film every advanced package needs, reported a business profit margin above 50% for the fiscal year ended March 2026 [s]. One qualifier travels with it permanently: that is a segment margin, and the segment is "Electronic Materials and Others" — not an isolated margin on the film itself. Put the supply response next to it, because that is the whole argument: Ajinomoto's third ABF plant, in Kani City, Gifu, is planned to begin operations in 2032, with construction starting in 2028 [f]. A margin above 50% is not remarkable on its own. A margin above 50% with the next plant six years out is a chokepoint.

3.The players

TSMC is the layer, and its mix shows why. Nodes at 7nm and below were 77% of wafer revenue in Q2 2026 [s], with N3 at 30%, up from 25% the prior quarter [s], and N2 making its first-ever revenue contribution at 3% [s]. HPC reached 66% of revenue, from 61% in Q1, while smartphone fell to 22% [s]. The AI transition is visible in the platform mix quarter by quarter.

ASML is the hard ceiling. Q2 2026 net sales were €9.3bn at a 54.0% gross margin [s], of which EUV systems were €3.8bn of €6.6bn in net system sales, including one High-NA machine [s]. Full-year guidance was raised in July to €43–45bn, from €36–40bn after Q1 [f]. China fell to 29.1% of 2025 net sales, from 36.1% in 2024 [s].

Applied Materials is the read on tool demand outside lithography: fiscal Q2 2026 revenue $7.91bn [s].

Intel is less dead than consensus, and the corpus is specific about how much less. Q2 2026 revenue was $16.1bn, up 25%, which Intel called its strongest revenue growth in more than fifteen years [s]. Its first 18A products entered high-volume production at the start of 2026 [s] — the start, not the back half of 2025, which is the date that circulated. Intel also produced the first commercial logic product made with High-NA EUV, announced in Q2 2026, a milestone TSMC has deliberately not chased, holding Low-NA for N2 and A16 [s]. But Intel Foundry revenue was $5.77bn in the quarter [s] against TSMC's $40.20bn [s], and only $293m of that $5,765m came from external customers [s] — roughly 95% is Intel selling to Intel. That single ratio decides whether Intel Foundry is a merchant foundry yet, and the answer is not yet.

Samsung is reported to target foundry profitability by 2027 alongside a 20% share by revenue [f]. That is an aspiration relayed from unnamed industry sources, not a Samsung announcement, and the base matters: share by revenue, not by capacity.

SMIC is the trailing-edge counterweight — 2025 revenue of $9,327m at a 21.0% gross margin [s], running at 93.5% utilisation on more than a million 8-inch-equivalent wafers a month [s]. It sells everything it can make, at a margin thirty-nine points below TSMC's.

4.Concentration & chokepoints

Three concentrations stack on top of each other here, and only one of them is the famous one.

Geographic. Taiwan held 66% of global advanced-process foundry capacity — 16/14nm and below — against Korea at 11%, the US at 10% and China at 9% [s]. That is a 2024 measurement printed on a 2026 page, and it should be read as a baseline that US buildouts are actively eroding. Further up the node stack, Rhodium Group put Taiwan's share of logic below 10nm at 92% [e] — a 2022 estimate, and the direction has not changed, because every TSMC fab at 5nm and below except Arizona is still on the island.

Customer. TSMC's ten largest customers were approximately 78% of 2025 net revenue, the largest at 19% and the second at 17% [s]. The second-largest customer jumped from 12% while the largest fell from 22%. TSMC names neither.

Packaging. CoWoS capacity went from roughly 35,000 wafers per month at end-2024 [e] to 72,500–80,000 at end-2025 [e], with a target of 120,000–130,000 by end-2026 [f]. Against that, Morgan Stanley put 2026 global CoWoS demand near 1.0 million wafers, versus about 670,000 in 2025 and 370,000 in 2024 [e], and projected NVIDIA's 2026 allocation at roughly 595,000 wafers, about 60% of capacity [e].

One number that belongs in this section and cannot be used in it: the widely circulated claim that the top three CoWoS customers hold more than 85% of 2026 capacity [e]. Traced back, it is an unattributed industry estimate — the arithmetic of the author of a WeChat post republished in English, credited to no analyst, and no admissible corroborator exists. It is printed here so a reader knows the site looked. Nothing on this page rests on it.

Substrate. The tightest queue is the least glamorous input. Four Taiwanese and two Japanese substrate suppliers were seeking upfront customer commitments to secure capacity for Intel's EMIB packaging as of May 2026 [s], on the record from Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan at a J.P. Morgan conference. The direction of the squeeze is worth stating precisely, because it is easy to invert: the suppliers want commitments from Intel, and Intel in turn asks its own packaging customers to help fund the substrate prepayment. When a supplier can demand cash before capacity, the supplier is the chokepoint.

5.Lead times & capacity

If demand doubled tomorrow, the answer would be set in Veldhoven and in one packaging fab in Taiwan.

ASML plans to ship approximately 65 Low-NA EUV systems in 2026, with EUV net system sales growing above 45% year over year — the figure is CFO Roger Dassen's [f]. That is the true width of the pipe at the top of the stack.

forecast · ASML CFO Roger Dassen, July 2026 ASML plans a 30% capacity increase for both Low-NA EUV and immersion systems in 2027, and is only investigating a further 30% increase for 2028 [f]. The distinction is the whole forecast: 2027 is planned, 2028 is under investigation, and the metric is capacity — what ASML could build — not output. Any 2028 supply floor built on this is a floor built on an intention.

Packaging is the other queue, and it is already sold. CoWoS lead times ran 52–78 weeks as of Q1 2026, with capacity fully booked through end-2026 [e].

forecast · TrendForce, relaying unnamed institutional investors via Taiwan's Economic Daily News CoWoS capacity is reported to reach 120,000–140,000 wafers per month by end-2026, with the supply-demand gap narrowing from about 20% to about 10% [f]. Note the provenance: third-hand and unnamed, not TSMC, which discloses no CoWoS capacity at all. If it is right, packaging is still rationed at the end of the forecast period.

The money going into the queue is being raised as fast as the queue lengthens. TSMC guided 2026 capex to $52–56bn in April, after $40.9bn in 2025 [f], then raised it in July to $60–64bn [f]. Total announced US investment reached $265bn in July 2026 after a further $100bn Arizona commitment [s]. Annual capacity passed 17 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers in 2025, on shipments of 15.0 million [s].

forecast · Applied Materials — CEO Gary Dickerson and CFO Brice Hill, May 2026 Applied projects over 30% growth in its semiconductor equipment business in calendar 2026, raised from over 20% guided in February [f], and over 50% growth in packaging revenues in calendar 2026 [f]. Dickerson said "packaging revenues" — not packaging equipment revenue, which is a narrower line, and the distinction is the company's, not ours.

6.2019 → today

The corpus's earliest hard anchor in this layer is 2022, so the first snapshot is thinner than the others, and the page will not fill the gap with atmosphere.

2022–23. Taiwan makes 92% of logic below 10nm [e]. Advanced nodes at 7nm and below are 58% of TSMC wafer revenue in 2023 [s], at a 54.4% gross margin [s]. Advanced packaging is a line item, not a constraint.

2024. Advanced nodes reach 69% of wafer revenue [s] at a 56.1% gross margin [s]. CoWoS is around 35,000 wafers per month [e]. Taiwan holds 66% of advanced capacity [s], and China is 36.1% of ASML's sales [s].

2025–26. Advanced nodes are 74% of wafer revenue for 2025 [s], gross margin 59.9% for the year [s] and 67.7% in Q2 2026 [s]. CoWoS roughly doubles again to 72,500–80,000 wafers per month [e]. ASML's China share falls to 29.1% [s] as export controls bite from both directions. N2 books its first revenue [s]. And the binding constraint is now a resin film with a segment margin above 50% [s].

7.Commoditization pressure

The leading edge is moving away from commodity, not toward it, and the evidence is in the price. Revenue up 36% on units up 16.6% [s][s], gross margin up more than seven points in a year [s][s], and a new node priced fifty percent above the last [e]. A commodity does not do that. TSMC guided Q3 2026 revenue to $44.6–45.8bn [f].

The commoditization is happening one or two nodes back, and in China specifically. SMIC's 21.0% gross margin [s] on 93.5% utilisation [s] is what a sold-out commodity fab looks like: full, and barely funding the next node. Chinese-made equipment rose from 25% to 35% of China's domestic tool market between 2024 and 2025, from a pre-controls base of 10–15% [s]. Yet domestically produced chips were only about 30% of Chinese chip consumption in 2025, against a policy target of 70% [s] — massive over-delivery on tools and mature nodes, large under-delivery on the headline goal. At the advanced end, SemiAnalysis conservatively estimated SMIC's advanced-node capacity at 45,000 wafers per month by end-2025, of which at most 20,000 was needed for Huawei Ascend production [e] — one analyst's estimate, uncorroborated, and running DUV multi-patterning without EUV means yield rather than capacity is the binding term.

The analogue is the layer's own history: mature nodes commoditized once anyone could buy the tool. The leading edge has not, because one company sells the tool.

8.Failure modes

One island. 66% of advanced capacity [s] and 92% of sub-10nm logic [e] in one earthquake zone, feeding customers who are themselves concentrated — approximately 78% of TSMC revenue in ten names [s]. Blast radius: everything above this layer, immediately.

The substrate. The cheapest input has the longest lead and the highest margin: suppliers demanding prepayment for capacity [s] against a film business earning a segment margin above 50% [s] whose own next plant does not begin operations until 2032 [f]. A package that cannot be built stops a die that is already printed, and this is the one queue in the layer that no amount of 2027 capex shortens.

Tool politics. Export enforcement now reaches the tools directly. Applied Materials agreed in February 2026 to pay BIS $253 million to resolve an inquiry into China shipments [s], a settlement carrying a suspended denial order — breach would bar certain US exports outright. BIS separately revoked Validated End-User authorisations for three foreign-owned fabs in China, effective 31 December 2025 [s], removing blanket tool-import rights from the largest non-Chinese fabs inside the country.

Intel commits, customers do not. Intel committed in Q2 2026 to a 2028 high-volume ramp for 14A, with risk production in the second half of 2027 [f]. That is Intel committing to Intel. The only report of a named first external 14A customer is a Reuters wire citing Elon Musk, for chips destined for Tesla's Austin Terafab complex [e] — sourced to comments rather than to an announcement or a filing by either company, with every other outlet syndicating the same wire. Three months on, Intel's filings still describe external 14A demand as uncommitted. The caveat is the more important half: a second leading-edge supplier only relieves the chokepoint if someone signs.

9.Open questions

  • Does the 2028 EUV capacity arrive? ASML plans 30% for 2027 and is investigating 30% for 2028 [f]. Everyone modelling 2028 accelerator supply is implicitly answering this, mostly without saying so.
  • Who covers substrate between now and 2032? Ajinomoto's third plant starts operations in 2032 [f], which is after every accelerator roadmap the site has recorded. Either the incumbent lines debottleneck, a second film supplier qualifies, or packaging stays rationed for the rest of the decade — and the corpus has no capacity data on the alternatives.
  • Whose wafers fill the US fabs? $265bn announced [s] at four to five times Taiwan construction cost [e]. The site has no data on who has committed to those wafers at what price.
  • Is Taiwan still at 66%? The share is a 2024 measurement [s] and the buildouts since are exactly the variable. A 2026 number would change several arguments on this page.
  • Does Intel land external 14A demand before the 2028 ramp? [f] It is the cleanest falsifiable test of whether the leading edge stays a monopoly.

◆The verdict

evidence-led verdict · updated 2026-07-25

Underbuilt — Revenue is growing twice as fast as wafers shipped, and every physical queue — EUV tools, CoWoS slots, ABF substrate — is oversubscribed. That is scarcity rent, not overcapacity.

the counter · the companies' own schedules — ASML's 2027 capacity plan, TSMC's US announcements, Intel's Q2 2026 commitmentThe relief is already scheduled and it lands together: ASML plans +30% capacity for 2027, TSMC has announced $265bn of US fabs, Intel ramps 14A in 2028. Nobody has contracted the demand that far out.
