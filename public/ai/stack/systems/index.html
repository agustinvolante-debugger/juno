Systems & Integration

OEMs/ODMs, rack-scale, Supermicro, Foxconn, Quanta

Assembling the rack is not a business. Supplying it is.

In the first quarter of 2026 the companies that bolt AI racks together reported operating margins of 1.8 to 3.6 percent, while the thermal, power and connector suppliers whose parts sit inside those same racks reported 17.8 to 25.4 percent [s]. Same quarter, same demand, same physical object. What follows is mostly how that gap opened while revenue in this layer roughly doubled.

1.What this layer is

The unit of sale changed. A GB300 NVL72 is 72 Blackwell Ultra GPUs and 36 Grace CPUs in one fully liquid-cooled rack with 37 terabytes of fast memory [s]. Nobody buys a server any more; they buy the rack.

The physical anchor, from the generation NVIDIA has published numbers for: 120 kilowatts [s], 1.36 metric tonnes on the floor [s], more than two miles of copper in the NVLink spine [s], liquid coolant at two litres a second [s]. Choosing copper over optics in that spine saves 20 kilowatts, a sixth of the rack's power budget, in Jensen Huang's framing [s]. Every one of those specifications is a purchase order for somebody who is not the integrator.

Doing the work: the US OEMs (Dell, HPE, Supermicro), the Taiwanese ODMs (Hon Hai, Quanta, Wistron, Inventec, Wiwynn) and the western contract manufacturers (Flex, Celestica). NVIDIA says Vera Rubin NVL72 production is ramping across more than 350 factory sites in 30 countries [s] — a company claim, and the best available proxy for how distributed this network already is.

Nobody who sells a rack discloses its price. The one figure in the corpus is an estimate: Morgan Stanley put the Vera Rubin VR200 NVL72 at about $7.8m bought from an ODM, on the analyst's own assumption that NVIDIA resells procured content at a 70% gross margin [e]. The primary note is not accessible and a competing figure circulates unreconciled, so it is an order of magnitude, not a price. At Quanta's filed Q1 2026 margins, a $3m rack leaves roughly $143k of gross profit and $86k of operating profit [e] — this site's arithmetic over a company-level margin, not a rack-specific one.

One disclosure contaminates every margin on this page, including that one, and it belongs here rather than in a footnote. Hon Hai's CFO said consignment is now the main transaction model in its ASIC-server projects [s]. Under consignment the customer owns the silicon, so the most expensive content in the rack never touches the integrator's revenue or its cost of sales — identical physical work reports a higher gross margin on a much smaller base. No integrator discloses its buy-and-sell versus consignment mix, so every comparison below is measured against a variable nobody outside these companies can size.

3.The players

Supermicro is the cleanest experiment, because it is only this business and nothing averages the dilution away. Gross margin ran 18.0% on $7.12bn of revenue in FY2023 [s] and 11.1% on $21.97bn in FY2025, operating margin 10.7% to 5.7% [s][s]. Then 6.3% on $12.68bn of net sales in the December 2025 quarter: its lowest gross margin ever, in its largest quarter ever [s].

Dell recognised $1,873m of AI-optimized server revenue in FY2024 and $24,683m in FY2026 [s], then $16,132m in a single quarter, up 757% [s]. Infrastructure Solutions Group operating margin went the other way — 12.6%, 12.8%, 11.7% [s], then 10.5% on $29,009m of segment revenue in the May 2026 quarter [s] — and consolidated gross margin fell from 24.3% to 17.8% over three years, which Dell's filing attributes without hedging to AI-server mix [s]. The steelman is in the same document: Dell's stated reason the segment number held up is that its operating-expense rate declined faster than its gross-margin rate. That is a scale argument, not a pricing-power one, and it is fragile to volume falling. Dell also publishes the distinction this layer usually blurs — $24.4bn of AI orders booked against $16.1bn recognised in the same quarter [s].

forecast · Dell Technologies, Jeff Clarke, May 2026 Dell raised its fiscal 2027 AI server revenue expectation to $60bn [f] — company guidance from the vice chairman, not a filed result.

HPE gave the clearest read of the pass-through and then removed the ability to track it. Server segment gross margin fell from 29.4% in FY2023 to 20.9% in FY2025 [s] and segment operating margin from 12.7% to 7.6% while segment revenue grew 24% to $17,745m [s][s]. Revenue up, segment profit down, and HPE attributes the growth to higher average unit prices — it is reselling accelerator content. The segment no longer exists.

The ODMs say the same thing over a longer window. Hon Hai's gross margin was 6.15% in 2025 against 6.27% in 2018, while revenue rose from NT$5.29tn to NT$8.10tn [s]; operating margin improved from 2.57% to 3.20%, on leverage and in-sourcing rather than price [s]. Quanta's AI margin bump is a completed round trip — 4.78% gross margin in FY2019, a 7.85% peak in 2024, 4.78% again in Q1 2026 [s] — recorded in a half-year when its cumulative revenue rose 86.5% [s]. Wistron doubled and got worse: revenue up 108% to NT$2,187bn in 2025, gross margin down from 8.01% to 6.13% [s]. Selling direct helps and does not exempt: Wiwynn earned 6.72% against Quanta's 4.12%, on a fifth of the revenue [s].

Where the profit went. Asia Vital Components, which makes the cold plates liquid cooling created demand for, took its operating margin from 2.47% in 2018 to 19.73% in 2025 [s]. Delta, in power shelves and busbars, went from 7.66% to 15.13% [s] — diversified well beyond datacenter power with no AI segment broken out, so that attribution is inferential. Vertiv went from 4.9% in 2020 to 17.9% in 2025 [s]. Amphenol earned 25.4% in 2025 on $23.09bn of revenue [s], with IT datacom sales rising about $4,593.7m in that year alone [s] — roughly six times Quanta's operating margin [s][s] on parts inside the same rack. The two contract manufacturers whose margins expanded did it by changing what they sell: Celestica from 4.0% to 8.4% [s] on its own networking hardware, Flex from 1.7% to 4.9% [s].

4.Concentration & chokepoints

The concentration that bites is on the customer list. Four customers were 75.1% of Supermicro's accounts receivable at March 2026, the largest alone 32.2% [s].

Hon Hai is the largest single node: Cloud and Networking Products were 48% of its revenue in Q1 2026 [s], with AI servers above 50% of server revenue as a stated floor and no supporting table [s]. Its spokesperson James Wu also claimed around 40% share of "the AI market" [o] — no definition, no methodology, no corroboration, so this site records it as his opinion. Geography is moving slowly: Wistron opened a 324,000 square foot factory in Fort Worth in July 2026 under a roughly $700m commitment, building the first US GB300 compute baseboard [s], and Foxconn says it runs more than 40 facilities across 12 US states [s].

forecast · NVIDIA, July 2026 NVIDIA says it plans to produce up to $500bn of AI infrastructure in the United States with named partners [f]. No time horizon, no contractual basis, no breakdown — and the facilities actually named in the same announcement total a few hundred thousand square feet [s].

5.Lead times & capacity

The corpus has no admissible figure for integration lead time, burn-in hours or test capacity, and this page will not invent one. The filings offer something better: the binding constraint is working capital, not floor space. Supermicro's cash fell from $5.2bn in June 2025 to $1.3bn in March 2026, and the filing names the causes — inventory build, customers asking for longer payment terms as system costs rise, longer lead times on key components [s]. A company earning low single-digit operating margins cannot self-fund a doubling. Hon Hai's answer to the same arithmetic is consignment, which its CFO says cuts the working capital it carries [s].

forecast · Hon Hai, Michael Chiang, May 2026 Hon Hai guided 2026 AI rack shipments to more than double, with the condition stated explicitly and doing real work: only if supply-chain material availability can meet demand [f]. Capex was around NT$174bn in 2025, up about 27%, guided to more than 30% growth in 2026 [s].

6.2019 → today

2019 is boxes and airflow: Quanta's gross margin 4.78%, Asia Vital Components' operating margin 2.47% [s][s]. 2022–24 is the bump everyone remembers — Supermicro peaking at an 18.0% gross margin, Quanta at 7.85%, Wistron at 8.01% [s][s][s] — and rack-scale looks like a margin story. 2025–26 completes the round trip while revenue accelerates: Quanta back to 4.78% and Hon Hai's gross margin ending where it started [s][s], while Asia Vital Components reaches 19.73% and Amphenol 25.4% [s][s]. Nothing about the demand disappointed. The ratios belong to someone else now.

7.Commoditization pressure

This layer was never un-commoditized, so the useful question is inverted: can any of it escape? The largest player says yes and names the route. Hon Hai is expanding into liquid cooling, mechanical components, high-speed transmission, power modules and system validation to raise its self-manufacturing ratio [o]. Asia Vital Components and Delta are the proof it works [s][s]; Inventec is the proof it is not automatic, at a 1.48% operating margin in 2018 and 1.84% in 2025, straight through the whole cycle [s].

Absent that, the layer is a conduit and says so: Hon Hai's rotating CEO described many of its businesses as buy-and-sell models in which product prices are adjusted according to costs [o]. That protects absolute profit against input inflation and means the layer cannot absorb a cost shock — it transmits it into the buyer's capital budget one layer up.

8.Failure modes

Customer concentration at the integrator. Four customers at 75.1% of receivables [s], a 6.3% gross margin [s] and $1.3bn of cash [s] make one slipped large shipment a liquidity event rather than a bad quarter. Blast radius runs up into NVIDIA's revenue recognition and down into already-committed memory and PCB suppliers.

Falling margin is mix, not distress. Both Dell and Supermicro attribute the compression to AI mix in filed documents [s][s]. If AI revenue keeps growing, reported ratios keep falling: the layer looks worse the better it does.

Trade policy lands here first. Supermicro's tariff expense rose $89.4m, up 608% year over year, in the March 2026 quarter alone [s].

Auditability is deteriorating as the layer grows. HPE dissolved the Server segment [s], the ODMs report no AI-server segment, and the consignment mix stays undisclosed [s]. Several series on this page are now closed.

9.Open questions

  • What does a rack actually cost? The per-rack question rests on one second-hand analyst estimate with an unreconciled spread [e] and one derivation from a company-level margin [e]. No integrator or buyer discloses a rack price.
  • What is the buy-and-sell versus consignment mix? [s] Until someone discloses it, every margin comparison here carries an error bar nobody outside the companies can size — including the ones this page just made.
  • Does self-manufacturing scale? It worked at component scale [s]; whether an NT$8.10tn revenue base can move its whole mix that way [s] is unproven, and Inventec is the warning [s].
  • Does the US footprint change the margin or only the address? [s][s] Nobody discloses AI-rack output by geography, so tariff exposure that is already a P&L line [s] cannot be mapped onto it. The corpus also has no figure for how much rack content NVIDIA now specifies itself.

◆The verdict

evidence-led verdict · updated 2026-07-25

Fairly priced — Integrators earned 1.8-3.6% operating margins in Q1 2026 while the thermal, power and connector content inside the same racks earned 17.8-25.4%. Capacity is fairly priced; the profit pool moved.
