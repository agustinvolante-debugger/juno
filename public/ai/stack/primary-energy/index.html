Primary Energy

Gas turbines, nuclear & SMRs, solar+storage, fuel supply

The cheapest input in the AI stack is the gas. The scarcest thing is the machine that burns it.

That inversion is the whole layer. EIA has Henry Hub averaging $3.67 per MMBtu in 2026 and just under $3.50 in 2027 [f] — molecules are abundant, and getting cheaper. Meanwhile a heavy-duty gas turbine now carries an order book that runs past the end of the decade, and the queue for one is measured in years. Anyone arguing about whether AI has enough energy is arguing about the wrong noun.

1.What this layer is

Everything above this page is downstream of three physical objects.

The heavy-duty gas turbine: a jet engine the size of a locomotive, bolted to a concrete pad, spinning a generator. Three companies on earth build them at scale — GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries. The nuclear plant: mostly not new, mostly being un-retired or contracted out of the existing fleet, occasionally a small modular reactor that does not exist yet. And solar plus storage: the only new supply that can be sited, built and energised inside three years, which is why it keeps winning on volume while losing the narrative.

The demand these three serve is real and measurable. US data centers consumed roughly 176 TWh in 2023, about 4.4% of national electricity, up from 58 TWh in 2014 [s]. Where it goes from there is a forecast, and gets forecast treatment.

forecast · Lawrence Berkeley National LaboratoryUS data center electricity consumption of 325 to 580 TWh by 2028 — 6.7 to 12 percent of total US electricity [f]. The range is the finding: the low case roughly doubles 2023, the high case more than triples it.
forecast · BloombergNEFUS data center power demand of 106 GW by 2035, from approximately 40 GW in 2026 [f].

Turbine pricing has a present-day level for the first time. BNP Paribas' Moses Sutton put heavy-duty gas turbines at about $790 per kW in July 2026, HA-class combined-cycle machines at about $950 per kW and aeroderivatives at about $1,800 per kW [e]. Wood Mackenzie separately forecasts about $600 per kW by end-2027 [f]. These are not two points on one line. The mid-2026 estimate already exceeds the end-2027 forecast, so they cannot be the same measure — most plausibly a global average across turbine classes against US order-book pricing by class. The site will not plot them as a series and neither should anyone else.

Whole-plant cost is a different animal again, and must never be set against turbine-only dollars per kW. BloombergNEF put US gas plant construction at $2,157 per kW in 2025, up from under $1,500 per kW in 2023 — a 66% increase in two years, with construction timelines 23% longer [e].

The direction of pricing power is not ambiguous. GE Vernova guided first-half 2026 gas equipment orders to 10 to 20 points above Q4 2025 on a dollar-per-kW basis, and reported in July 2026 that it landed at the higher end of that range [e].

Nuclear prices at roughly twice wholesale, and the discovery range is now visible. Microsoft's Three Mile Island restart PPA is estimated at $98 per MWh by Morgan Stanley and $115 per MWh by Jefferies [e] — both estimates; the contract price is undisclosed. Meta's Clinton virtual PPA is estimated by Jefferies at $70 per MWh, about $20 per MWh above Illinois energy-plus-capacity compensation [e]. Constellation itself guided 80 to 88 dollars per MWh for future front-of-the-meter nuclear offtake [f]. A band is forming between roughly $70 and $115, and every number in it is an analyst estimate or a guide, not a printed contract.

3.The players

GE Vernova is the layer's clearest instrument. Gas equipment backlog plus slot reservations reached 116 GW at the end of Q2 2026, up from 100 GW a quarter earlier [s]. Total company backlog is $176bn [s], on $24.2bn of Q2 orders, up 88% organically [s].

The headline everyone repeats is that GE Vernova signed 20 GW of gas equipment contracts in Q2 2026 while shipping 3 GW [s]. Roughly seven times what it ships, and it is the most generous of three available ratios. In the same presentation GE Vernova reports its own unit metric: 12.1 GW of gas turbine orders against 3.3 GW of sales, 3.7 times, not seven [s]. The gap is composition: the 20 GW is firm orders plus a much larger block of non-binding slot reservation agreements, which the company reports separately from both orders and backlog [s]. On new firm orders alone, the quarter's signings came in below its 3.3 GW of shipments [s]. The scarcity is genuine. The multiple depends entirely on whether you count a reservation as a sale.

Data centers are the marginal driver, not the bulk of it. Data center load was 20% of GE Vernova's 100 GW under contract as of Q1 2026 [s] — and because GE Vernova says that 20% explicitly supports data centers, excluding indirect service, it is a floor. Downstream, its Electrification segment booked over $5bn of data-center-linked orders in the first half of 2026, more than double its full-year 2025 total [s]; gas power orders are not in that number.

Siemens Energy carries a €154bn backlog with gas turbine delivery waits of up to four years [s], and holds 60 GW of firm gas turbine orders plus 27 GW of reserved future capacity [s]. Data-center-related commitments were 24 GW of that 87 GW total, about 28% [s]. Its North American arm booked 14 GW in the first three quarters of fiscal 2025 with 65% destined for data centers [s] — the mix at the margin is far more AI-weighted than the stock.

Mitsubishi Heavy Industries planned as of September 2025 to double gas turbine manufacturing capacity over two years [f]. EPRI's Bobby Noble supplies the deflating arithmetic: one manufacturer doubling raises overall industry output by a small fraction of that, and is likely not enough to drastically reduce wait times.

Nuclear, on this layer, is four hyperscalers buying from the existing fleet and funding the next one. Microsoft signed a 20-year, 835 MW PPA with Constellation to restart Three Mile Island Unit 1 as the Crane Clean Energy Center [s], against roughly $1.6bn of recommissioning investment and a target pulled from 2028 to 2027 [s], helped by a $1bn DOE loan [s]. AWS expanded its Susquehanna PPA with Talen to 1,920 MW [s]. Meta signed 20-year agreements with Vistra for 2,100 MW from the existing Perry and Davis-Besse plants plus 433 MW of uprates in the early 2030s [s] and a 20-year, 1,092 MW virtual PPA for Clinton starting June 2027 [s].

4.Concentration & chokepoints

Three manufacturers, one order book, and a global capacity number that has not moved fast enough.

Global gas turbine orders reached 110 GW in 2025 against global manufacturing capacity of 60 to 70 GW per year [e]. That is the chokepoint stated in one line: the industry took roughly half again more orders than it can build in a year. GE Vernova had approximately 10 GW of combined 2029 and 2030 delivery capacity remaining after Q1 2026 [s] — GE Vernova's slots, not the industry's. It is roughly a third of the heavy-duty market; Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi slots are not in that figure, and presenting a single vendor's remaining slots as an industry ceiling overstates the scarcity.

What one buyer can absorb: the SB Energy Portsmouth data center project is sized at 9.2 GW and requires 24 to 30 heavy-duty gas turbines [s]. A single site can consume a meaningful share of a year of one vendor's output.

There is a second concentration on this layer that nobody prices, because it sits under the fabs rather than the racks. Taiwan imports about 97% of its energy and holds roughly two weeks of emergency supply without new imports [s]. In any blockade scenario the grid fails before the cleanrooms do.

5.Lead times & capacity

This is where the layer's two honest steelmen live, and both are named.

Waits for large gas turbines reached up to 84 months in 2025, from 30 to 36 months previously [e]. GE Vernova's own directional lead time on new heavy-duty orders was approximately 36 months in Q1 2026 [e] — a different scope from the industry maximum, and the gap between the two is the single most abused spread in this discourse.

Steelman one: the manufacturer says it is not the constraint. On that same call, GE Vernova CEO Scott Strazik said that in many of these projects "the gas turbines are really not the gating item," pointing instead at the EPC buildout, permitting and fuel availability over a three-year project cycle [e]. The chief executive of the company whose slot scarcity anchors the shortage narrative does not hold the shortage narrative.

Steelman two: supply is already responding. Wood Mackenzie's Ed Crooks noted in February 2026 that GE Vernova, Siemens and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries have all announced significant gas-equipment capacity expansions since the start of 2025 [o], and says turbine makers are increasingly not the primary bottleneck for large loads seeking power.

The capacity plans behind that are concrete, and they are forecasts.

forecast · GE Vernova (Scott Strazik)Gas turbine production output of 20 GW per year by Q3 2026, 24 GW by 2028 and 30 GW by 2030 [f] — with at least 125 GW of gas equipment under contract by end-2026 [f]. Note the shape of it: the 2030 output target sits below 2025's industry order intake.
forecast · Wood Mackenzie63 GW of gas turbine manufacturing capacity additions between 2026 and 2030 [f], with orders expected to peak in 2026 [e]. If both hold, the industry adds capacity into a falling order book — the overcapacity case, from the firm that documented the shortage.

Meanwhile the supply that does not queue for a turbine keeps growing fastest.

forecast · EIA (Short-Term Energy Outlook, July 2026)US solar capacity growing from 151 GW in 2025 to 183 GW in 2026 and 223 GW in 2027 [f]; natural gas generating capacity reaching 508 GW by end-2027 [f]; total generation rising from 4,502 to 4,636 billion kWh, roughly 3% growth [f].

Hold those two against each other. Total US generation grows roughly 3% to 2027 [f] while the low end of LBNL's range has data center consumption roughly doubling off its 2023 base by 2028 [f]. The system is not being asked for much more electricity in general. It is being asked for a great deal of electricity in a few places, on a schedule set by chip deliveries.

6.2019 → today

2019. The baseline year the whole price series is measured against. Gas is a mature, deflating business; turbine orders track utility retirements; nobody outside the industry can name the three manufacturers.

2022–23. Gas plant construction is still under $1,500 per kW [e]. Turbine waits are 30 to 36 months [e]. Nuclear plants are still closing — Palisades shuts in May 2022 [s].

2025–26. Orders hit 110 GW against 60 to 70 GW of annual capacity [e]. Waits reach 84 months [e]. Construction cost is $2,157 per kW [e]. Wood Mackenzie's forecast has turbine prices reaching approximately 195% above 2019 levels by end-2027 [f]. And the direction of the nuclear fleet reverses: the DOE closes a $1.52bn loan to restart the 800 MW Palisades plant, the first recommissioning of a retired US reactor [s].

The thing that changed is not the fuel. It is that a deflating capital-goods business discovered it was a bottleneck.

7.Commoditization pressure

This layer is de-commoditizing, and it is doing so in exactly the places where a commodity story would predict the opposite.

Gas itself is the commodity, and it behaves like one: EIA has power-sector consumption reaching 38.1 Bcf per day in 2027, up 4% from about 37.5 Bcf per day in 2026, with a record monthly 50.6 Bcf per day in July 2027 [f] — record volumes at falling prices. That is a commodity working correctly.

The turbine is going the other way. Prices at roughly 195% of 2019 levels on Wood Mackenzie's forecast [f], order pricing landing at the top of a 10-to-20-point guided increase [e], and slot reservation agreements — customers paying to hold a place in a queue — as a routine commercial instrument. Manufacturers are selling scarcity as a product.

Nuclear is pre-commodity: a price band being discovered deal by deal between roughly $70 and $115 per MWh [e], with the seller publishing a guide [f] because there is no liquid market to reference.

Solar plus storage is the one genuinely commoditized segment, and it is the reason the layer's scarcity has a ceiling. EIA has US solar going 151 → 183 → 223 GW across 2025 to 2027 [f]. If turbine slots stay sold out to 2030, that is where marginal load goes.

8.Failure modes

The turbine order book turns out to be a cycle top. Wood Mackenzie forecasts 63 GW of manufacturing capacity additions to 2030 [f] while expecting orders to peak in 2026 [e]. Every slot reservation signed at today's pricing is a bet that does not clear. Blast radius: three manufacturers' margins, and the credibility of every AI power forecast that assumed the shortage was structural.

Nuclear slips, in the way nuclear slips. The clearest datapoint is not a forecast but a plant. Palisades had still not returned to power operations as of July 2, 2026 — 800 MW, a $1.52bn federal loan, and Holtec reporting the last major restart projects closed out with operational-readiness work still underway [s][s]. Against that benchmark, read the SMR pipeline carefully. Google agreed to purchase up to 500 MW from Kairos Power reactors with the first targeted online in 2030 [s]. Amazon and Energy Northwest announced four X-energy SMRs with a 320 MW first phase expandable to 960 MW in the early 2030s [s]. Meta and Oklo announced support for development of up to 1,200 MW at Pike County, Ohio, first power possible as early as 2030 [s]; Meta and TerraPower announced funding for two Natrium units capable of up to 690 MW as early as 2032, plus rights to up to six further units producing 2.1 GW targeted for 2035 [s].

Read those two Meta agreements precisely, because the headlines do not. Both are "up to" ceilings on development and prepay frameworks, not contracted offtake. Oklo has no NRC-licensed operating unit. Nothing in either agreement obliges anyone to deliver a specific number of megawatt-hours on a specific date.

Demand-side flexibility becomes the release valve, or does not. Google had signed 1 GW of data center demand response with five utilities as of March 2026 [s], with Google's own caveat attached: there are limits to how flexible a given data center can be, and the capability will only be available at certain locations. If flexibility scales, the turbine shortage matters less than anyone thinks. If it does not, every GW of load needs a firm GW behind it.

9.Open questions

  • Which turbine price is the real one? BNP Paribas at ~$790/kW in mid-2026 [e] and Wood Mackenzie at ~$600/kW by end-2027 [f] cannot both describe the same object. The corpus does not have the scope definitions to reconcile them, and until it does, no chart on this site joins them.
  • Do orders peak in 2026? Wood Mackenzie says yes [e]; GE Vernova is guiding to at least 125 GW under contract by year-end [f]. This is the cleanest falsifiable test on the page and it resolves within twelve months.
  • Is the gating item the turbine or the project? Strazik says EPC, permitting and fuel [e]; Crooks says turbine makers are increasingly not the primary bottleneck [o]. If they are right, the constraint lives one layer up — in the interconnection queue — and this page is the wrong place to look for it.
  • What do the SMR frameworks actually oblige? Every announced agreement is an "up to" volume with a first-power date in 2030 or later. The corpus has no disclosed prepay amounts, no penalty terms, and no NRC licence for the lead vendor on Meta's Oklo deal [s].
  • How much of the nuclear price band is real? Every figure in the $70–115/MWh range is an analyst estimate or a seller's guide [e]. Not one contract price has been published.

◆The verdict

evidence-led verdict · updated 2026-07-25

Underbuilt — Fuel is cheap and getting cheaper. The machines that burn it are sold out past 2029, priced at the top of guidance, with waits reaching 84 months. That is a shortage, not a bubble.
