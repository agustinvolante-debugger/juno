layer 13 of 16 · deep dive

Applications

Horizontal assistants + vertical apps, seat vs outcome pricing

This is where the stack's dollar finally becomes revenue. It is also the layer the site knows least about.

Every layer below this one is measured in filings. Here, almost nothing is. The revenue figures for Harvey, Abridge, Decagon, Replit, Lovable and Clay are estimates published by one research shop — Sacra — and then restated by aggregators that add no measurement of their own. Restatement is not corroboration. So this page names the estimator every time, and where a company has published a figure of its own it wins; where a valuation multiple is printed, the page says which denominator it used.

1.What this layer is

Three businesses wearing one label.

Horizontal assistants sold to everyone. ChatGPT is the layer's only consumer-scale asset: more than 900 million weekly active users on OpenAI's own count as of February 2026, alongside more than 50 million consumer subscribers [s][s] and more than 9 million paying business users [s]. A separate 1-billion figure for monthly active app users is a Sensor Tower estimate relayed by Reuters, crossed in June 2026 — not an OpenAI disclosure [s].

Vertical apps sold to one profession. Harvey and Legora to lawyers, Abridge to clinicians, Glean to the enterprise's own documents, Clay to revenue teams.

Agents priced by the outcome. Sierra and Decagon in customer support, and Intercom's Fin, which publishes the cleanest price in the layer: $0.99 per outcome, billed only when it resolves an issue end to end or executes a configured procedure [s].

The physical anchor is a login and a meter. A lawyer's seat starting near $1,200 a month [e]; a resolved support ticket at $0.99 [s]. Behind both sits a token bill the vendor does not set.

Seat pricing still dominates, and it is priced like enterprise software rather than like a metered utility. Harvey's base offering starts at about $1,200 per lawyer per month on twelve-month commitments [e] — Sacra's wording is "starts at", so treat it as a floor, and note that Harvey publishes no pricing at all. Abridge sits an order of magnitude lower per user: roughly $2,500 per clinician per year [e], single-sourced, with the bottom-up comparators in the same note also unverified.

Outcome pricing is the genuine innovation, and it is small but real. Fin's $0.99 [s] is a live published price on Intercom's own page, subject to a monthly outcome minimum. It transfers model-cost risk from the buyer to the vendor, which is exactly why the margin question below matters more here than in any software layer before it.

Two margin datapoints exist for the whole layer, and both are weak. OpenAI's gross margin was approximately 33% for FY2025 [e] — an analyst estimate, because OpenAI publishes no audited margin. And Replit's gross margin fluctuated between positive 36% and negative 14% during 2025 [e], which Sacra attributes to the cost of running its coding agents.

That Replit range is the most load-bearing sentence on this page, so the site will state its fragility plainly: it is a range that crosses zero, which is why the corpus refuses to record a single number for it; it has no independent source at any date, because Replit is private and discloses nothing; and it is one unsourced sentence from one research shop. It is also the only public evidence anywhere that agentic cost of goods can invert an application's margin. Replit's own response — replacing flat checkpoint pricing with effort-based agent pricing — is consistent with the problem being real.

3.The players

Coding is the deepest pocket. Cursor reached $1bn annualized in November 2025 [s] and $3bn by May 2026 [s], then produced the layer's first mega-exit: an all-stock acquisition by SpaceX valuing Anysphere at $60bn, announced 16 June 2026 [s]. Lovable reached about $500m annualized in mid-2026 [e] with 146 full-time employees in February 2026 [e]. The revenue-per-employee figure in circulation for Lovable mixes a mid-2026 revenue with a February headcount; the site will not print it, because on matched dates it is materially lower. Replit reached about $525m annualized in April 2026 [e], single-sourced.

The labs are themselves applications. Anthropic disclosed a $14bn run rate on 12 February 2026 [s] and $47bn by May [s], with roughly 70–75% of revenue from pay-per-token API calls on Sacra's estimate [e]. Do not restate that as "80% from API": the independently corroborated ~80% figure is revenue from business customers, a different cut [s]. Claude Code is reported at $2.5bn annualized in February 2026 [e] — Sacra-only, and flagged in the corpus as unable to anchor a headline claim alone.

Legal is a two-horse capital race. Harvey served more than 100,000 lawyers across 1,300 organizations in over 60 countries as of March 2026 — its own disclosure [s]. Sacra's user count is materially higher, carries no source, and post-dates Harvey's own by two months, so the site uses the company's. Harvey raised at $11bn in March 2026 [s] on an estimated $300m ARR [e]; Legora raised $550m at $5.55bn [s]. Harvey's last well-sourced revenue figure is the $100m it reached in August 2025, three years after founding [s].

Support agents are the richest-priced. Sierra raised $950m at a $15.8bn post-money valuation in May 2026 [s]. There are two revenue denominators available and they disagree: Sacra estimates $200m [e], while Sierra itself said it had passed $150m in February 2026, with coverage of the May round still reporting ARR above $150m [s]. This page uses the company's figure, which is the lower revenue and therefore the less flattering multiple. Either way the growth is steep: an estimated $26m at the end of 2024 [e]. Decagon raised $250m at $4.5bn in January 2026 [s] against an estimated $35m annualized in October 2025 [e].

Enterprise search and GTM. Glean went from $100m ARR at fiscal year end January 2025 [s] to $300m in May 2026 [s]. Clay reached an estimated $150m [e]; Perplexity an estimated $500m in April 2026 [e], where the reported ~335% growth rate and the separate full-year-2025 figure on the same page are two different comparisons and must not be welded together.

4.Concentration & chokepoints

The obvious chokepoint is the model bill. OpenAI's inference costs reached an estimated $8.4bn in 2025 [e], and the lineage matters: the figure originates with Ed Zitron's leaked-document reporting and Sacra is plausibly downstream of it. That is one original plus one relay, not two measurements.

forecast · SacraOpenAI inference costs projected at $14.1bn for 2026, against $8.4bn in 2025 [e]. Independent reporting corroborates only the direction — that the day-to-day expense of running models quadrupled in 2025.

The less obvious chokepoint is measurement, and it is the reason for this page's verdict. One research shop supplies the denominator for most of the layer's valuation arithmetic: Claude Code's $2.5bn [e], Abridge's $100m [e], Sierra's $26m [e] and $200m [e], Decagon's $35m [e], Replit's $525m [e] and its margin range [e], Harvey's $300m [e] are all single-source, and several carry an explicit corpus flag that they may not anchor a verdict alone. There is no equivalent of a 10-Q anywhere in this layer.

Customer concentration runs the other way from the layers below: it is diffuse at the bottom and concentrating at the top. Anthropic reported more than 300,000 business customers as of October 2025 [s] — the count is Sacra-lineage, though the accompanying claim that business customers are ~80% of revenue is independently corroborated — and more than 1,000 customers spending over $1m a year by April 2026 [s]. Enterprise passed 40% of OpenAI revenue in 2026 on Sacra's estimate [e], which is the mix shift that makes this layer look like software rather than like a consumer subscription business.

5.Lead times & capacity

There is no permit queue here and no lead time on physical supply. The two real constraints are how fast a product gets inside a regulated profession, and what a token costs when it gets there.

The first constraint is slower than the funding rounds imply. Harvey took three years from founding to $100m ARR [s]. Glean took roughly sixteen months to triple from $100m [s] to $300m [s]. Cursor is the outlier that everyone extrapolates from, tripling from $1bn [s] to $3bn [s] in six months.

The second constraint does not reliably move in the vendor's favour. Inference prices for fixed benchmark performance fell at a median 50× per year in Epoch AI's regression [e], which is the tailwind every app-layer business plan assumes. But Anthropic has scheduled Claude Sonnet 5's output price to rise from $10 to $15 per million tokens on 1 September 2026 [s]. Per-capability deflation and per-product price increases are both true, and an application with fixed $0.99 outcome pricing eats the difference.

6.2019 → today

2019. This layer does not exist. AI is a feature inside software priced per seat, and no company buys a model.

2022–23. ChatGPT arrives and the horizontal assistant becomes a category. Pricing is a monthly consumer subscription bolted onto a research preview.

2025. Revenue at scale, and the shape becomes visible: coding first, then legal, then support. Cursor crosses $1bn annualized in November [s]; Harvey crosses $100m in August [s]; Glean crosses $100m at its January fiscal year end [s].

2026. Three things land at once. Outcome pricing appears as a published list price [s]. Valuations detach from measurable revenue — $15.8bn for Sierra [s], $11bn for Harvey [s]. And the layer produces a real exit at $60bn in stock [s], which is the first hard number in the layer's short history.

7.Commoditization pressure

The clearest commoditization evidence in the layer is a vendor cutting prices and growing anyway. Clay cut many native enrichment prices by 50 to 90% in March 2026 while restructuring to a dual-meter pricing system [s], and reached an estimated $150m ARR in May [e]. Two qualifiers travel with that: the dual-meter restructuring is confirmed on Clay's own live pricing page, but the 50–90% cut is Sacra-only, and 90 is the top of a range rather than a point. Sacra's quoted tier price also does not match what Clay's live page shows, which is a small discrepancy pointing at a stale reading.

What resists commoditization is not the model and not the interface. It is the depth of the workflow and the switching cost inside a regulated profession — Harvey's 100,000 lawyers across 1,300 organizations [s] are a distribution position, not a technology one. The uncomfortable corollary is that the moat being purchased in legal AI is capital: $11bn behind Harvey [s] and $5.55bn behind Legora [s] is a two-horse race financed to exhaust a market, which is a distribution strategy rather than a product one.

8.Failure modes

Margin inversion. Replit's 2025 range from +36% to −14% [e] is the mechanism: an agent that runs longer costs more, and a flat-priced product cannot pass that through. OpenAI's 33% [e] shows the same pressure at the top of the layer. Blast radius: every outcome-priced product, and every seat-priced product whose usage is unbounded.

A multiple built on an estimate. Decagon's $4.5bn against $35m annualized is roughly 128 times [s][e] — arithmetic over the two facts cited in this sentence, and the denominator is an estimate. Sierra's $15.8bn is roughly 105 times the company's own ">$150m" [s][s], and roughly 79 times Sacra's higher estimate [e]. A site whose whole claim is citation discipline should not quietly pick the denominator that makes the multiple look better, so this page prints the company's. Harvey's $11bn against an estimated $300m is roughly 37 times forward [s][e], on the same kind of unverified denominator.

Absorption by the model provider. Claude Code at a reported $2.5bn annualized [e] means Anthropic sells both the coding product and the tokens powering its largest competitor in that category. The application layer's suppliers are also its rivals, and they see its usage data.

Measurement failure. Abridge is the warning case: the widely repeated $316m Series E extension in April 2026 is not company-confirmed and comes from secondary-market data providers [e], and its last solid revenue figure is the estimated $100m from May 2025 [e]. A layer whose numbers arrive through this pipe will reprice suddenly when a real filing appears.

9.Open questions

  • What is the real gross margin of an agentic application? One company's range, from one unsourced sentence, is the entire public evidence base [e]. Every valuation in the layer implies an answer nobody has published.
  • Does outcome pricing survive a cost spike? Fin's $0.99 [s] is a fixed price against a variable cost. Anthropic's scheduled September 2026 increase [s] is the first live test.
  • Do the vertical apps hold their seats once the labs ship the same workflow? Harvey's 100,000 lawyers [s] are the strongest evidence for yes; Claude Code's growth inside coding [e] is the evidence for no.
  • Is the $60bn Cursor mark a price or an artefact of stock? It was all-stock [s], paid in the equity of a private buyer, and the corpus has no data on the exchange terms.
  • Where does enterprise revenue actually settle relative to consumer? OpenAI passing 40% enterprise [e] against 900 million weekly consumers [s] describes two businesses with different margins, and only the estimate exists for the split.

◆The verdict

evidence-led verdict · updated 2026-07-25

Overbuilt — The revenue is real and compounding. The prices are set against denominators one research shop estimates and nobody audits, and the layer's only public margin datapoint crosses zero.
