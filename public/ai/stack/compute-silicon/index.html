Compute Silicon

GPUs, TPUs, custom ASICs, accelerator challengers

The interesting question about this layer stopped being "can anyone catch NVIDIA" about a year ago. Custom silicon caught it, in the sense that matters — Broadcom's AI business is now guided at $16.0 billion a quarter [f] — and NVIDIA accelerated anyway.

That is the fact this page has to explain. Two things are both true: custom ASICs are taking real volume, and NVIDIA's data center line grew 92% year over year to $75.2 billion in a single quarter [s]. The resolution is upstream. Nobody in this layer is winning sockets off anybody else while advanced packaging is sold out.

1.What this layer is

The layer designs the thing that turns electricity into linear algebra, and increasingly does not sell it as a chip.

Merchant silicon is NVIDIA and AMD: a part with a price list, sold to anyone. Custom silicon is Broadcom and Marvell doing design and packaging for a buyer who owns the product — Google's TPU, Amazon's Trainium, Meta's and OpenAI's accelerators. Challengers are architecture bets: Groq's inference LPU, Cerebras's wafer-scale part.

The physical anchor: a die the size of a drink coaster, flanked by stacks of memory millimeters away, bolted onto a board, seventy-two of which now behave as one machine. NVLink 6 carries 260 TB/s of all-to-all fabric bandwidth inside a single Vera Rubin NVL72 rack [s]. Google's equivalent is a pod: 9,216 Ironwood TPUs delivering 42.5 exaflops [s], each chip at 4,614 TFLOPs peak [s]. The unit of sale is drifting from the chip to the rack, and that drift is the layer's actual strategy.

NVIDIA's total revenue was $81.6bn in Q1 FY2027, the quarter ended April 2026, up 85% [s], of which data center was $75.2bn, up 92% [s]. Data center is therefore about ninety-two percent of the company . GAAP gross margin was 74.9% [s], held near that level straight through the Blackwell-to-Rubin transition — the moment when a hardware company's margin usually dips.

The comparison that sizes the gap: AMD's Q1 2026 revenue was $10.253bn [s], its data center segment $5.775bn, up 57% [s], at a 55% non-GAAP gross margin — 53% GAAP [s]. A roughly twenty-point structural margin gap has now persisted through the entire AI cycle. And NVIDIA's networking line alone, at $14.8bn for the quarter, up 199% [s], is larger than AMD's whole data center segment [s].

3.The players

NVIDIA. Full fiscal 2026 revenue $215.9bn, up 65% [s], with data center at $193.7bn, up 68% [s].

AMD is the credible second merchant supplier and prices like one. Its route to differentiation is increasingly partnership rather than part — AMD and Cerebras announced a disaggregated inference system in July 2026 claiming up to 5x higher tokens per second per watt [e], which is a vendor claim on a vendor configuration and is logged as one.

Broadcom is the layer's real structural change. Q2 FY2026 total revenue $22.187bn, up 48% [s], following $19.311bn in Q1 [s]. AI semiconductor revenue inside that was $10.8bn, up 143% [s]. This is the best single datapoint anywhere on custom-ASIC share gains.

forecast · company guidance — NVIDIA (May 2026), AMD (May 2026), Broadcom (June 2026) NVIDIA guided Q2 FY2027 revenue to $91.0bn plus or minus 2% [f]. AMD guided Q2 2026 to approximately $11.2bn plus or minus $300m [f]. Broadcom guided Q3 FY2026 AI semiconductor revenue to $16.0bn, up 200% year over year [f]. Guidance is a forecast even when the forecaster is the company being forecast, and the three are what the whole additive-versus-substitutive argument below is tested against.

The captive designers. Google's Ironwood carries 192 GB of HBM per chip at 7.37 TB/s [s]. Anthropic announced access to up to 1,000,000 Google Cloud TPUs in October 2025, worth tens of billions of dollars [s], and separately stated in April 2026 that it already uses more than 1,000,000 AWS Trainium2 chips to train and serve Claude [s]. Note the difference between those two: one is a commitment, one is chips in production. The corpus keeps them apart and so should any chart.

The challengers thinned out. Groq raised $750m in September 2025 [s] and $650m in June 2026 for its inference cloud [s] — and in between signed away the architecture bet, which section 7 takes up. Cerebras announced plans for 200 MW of AI compute capacity in Europe by end-2027 [f].

4.Concentration & chokepoints

The chokepoint for this layer is not in this layer. It is the packaging line one level down.

Morgan Stanley projected NVIDIA's 2026 CoWoS allocation at roughly 595,000 wafers, about 60% of total capacity [e], against 2026 global CoWoS demand near 1.0 million wafers versus about 670,000 in 2025 [e]. Lead times were 52–78 weeks with capacity fully booked through end-2026 [e]. Whoever holds packaging slots ships accelerators; whoever does not, does not, however good the architecture.

The second chokepoint is regulatory, and it is memory bandwidth rather than compute. The BIS rule effective 15 January 2026 caps licensable China and Macau exports at total DRAM bandwidth below 6,500 GB/s [s], alongside a total-processing-performance ceiling of 21,000 [s]. The second gate, not the first, is what separates an H200-class part from a Blackwell-class one.

And the third is demand concentration. Anthropic committed in April 2026 to spend more than $100bn on AWS technologies over ten years [s], under an agreement covering up to 5 GW of new AWS capacity with about 1 GW of Trainium2 and Trainium3 online by end-2026 [f]. A layer whose growth is underwritten by a small number of labs inherits their financing risk.

5.Lead times & capacity

Here is the argument the numbers actually support: custom ASIC gains are additive, not substitutive — and the reason is that supply lags demand, so an ASIC wafer is a new wafer rather than a wafer taken from NVIDIA.

Test it against the two growth rates. NVIDIA's data center revenue grew 92% to $75.2bn in the April 2026 quarter [s], which means it added roughly $36bn of quarterly data center revenue year over year . Broadcom's entire AI semiconductor line, at the guided $16.0bn [f], is under half of that single increment. If ASICs were displacing merchant GPUs, NVIDIA's growth would be decelerating as Broadcom's accelerated. Instead NVIDIA guided the following quarter up to $91.0bn [f].

The mechanism sits in packaging. CoWoS demand is rising from about 670,000 wafers to about 1.0 million [e] against capacity that went from roughly 35,000 wafers a month at end-2024 [e] to 72,500–80,000 at end-2025 [e], with everything booked through end-2026 [e]. In a rationed market, a Google TPU order does not cancel an NVIDIA order — it consumes capacity that did not exist a year ago. The substitution question only becomes answerable when the queue clears.

6.2019 → today

FY2019. NVIDIA's full-year revenue was $11.72bn [s]. It is a graphics company with a promising accelerator business.

FY2022. Revenue $26.91bn [s], of which data center $10.61bn [s] — roughly two-fifths of the company . Gaming still matters.

FY2026 and after. Revenue $215.9bn [s], data center $193.7bn [s], and a single quarter now larger than the whole of FY2022 [s]. Meanwhile the China business went from material to zero: revenue from customers headquartered in China including Hong Kong fell to $4,550m in the April 2026 quarter, from $9,659m a year earlier [s] — and that measure is booked by customer headquarters, not shipping destination, so it is not a China-destination number.

7.Commoditization pressure

A 74.9% gross margin held through an architecture transition [s] is the single strongest piece of evidence against commoditization at the top of this layer. The pressure is real but it is arriving as vertical integration — buyers designing their own parts — rather than as price competition on merchant parts. Vertical integration compresses a supplier's volume, eventually. It does not compress its price while the queue is full.

What did commoditize is the challenger tier, and it consolidated rather than competed. Groq signed a non-exclusive licensing agreement with NVIDIA on 24 December 2025 worth $17bn in cash across three installments by end-2026 [s], with CEO Jonathan Ross and president Sunny Madra moving to NVIDIA and the CFO becoming CEO. The structure is independently corroborated, which matters — this was a single-source claim until it was not. Press reporting put the total value at about $20bn, of which $17bn was the cash [s]; the two numbers are different things and should never be used interchangeably. Read plainly, it is a soft exit of the independent-LPU thesis: the most credible architectural challenge to the GPU was resolved by a payment rather than by a benchmark.

Speaking of benchmarks, treat vendor throughput claims as claims. NVIDIA and CoreWeave stated in July 2026 that Vera Rubin NVL72 delivers 10x more throughput per megawatt than Grace Blackwell NVL72 [e] — one benchmark, one workload, both parties interested.

8.Failure modes

Policy, in one line item. NVIDIA took a $4.5bn charge in the April 2025 quarter for excess H20 inventory and purchase obligations after the US required licences for that class of chip [s]. That is the cost of a single export-control reclassification to a single vendor. It has since gone further: no data center Hopper products shipped to China in the April 2026 quarter, against $4.6bn a year earlier [s], and zero revenue had been generated under the February 2026 H200 licensing programme as of the May 2026 filing [s]. Supply was legalised in Washington while demand stayed banned in Beijing. Jensen Huang, an interested party, says NVIDIA's China accelerator share has fallen to zero [o].

Credit-financed demand. Silicon demand is now partly a private-credit product. If that channel reprices, the order book upstream reprices with it.

forecast · Broadcom, Apollo and Blackstone, June 2026 The platform announced in June 2026 targets more than 20 GW of global AI deployments through 2028 [f], on an initial tranche of $35bn led by Apollo [s]. The gigawatts are an announced plan, not deployed capacity; the tranche is committed capital. The two must not be added together or drawn as one bar.

Buyer concentration among labs. More than $100bn committed to AWS by one lab [s], up to a million TPUs from another vendor [s], multiple gigawatts of next-generation TPU capacity with Google and Broadcom from 2027 [f]. The customers are few, large, and mostly pre-revenue relative to what they have signed.

The queue clearing. The additive-not-substitutive argument in section 5 is conditional on scarcity. When packaging capacity catches demand, the same ASIC volumes stop being incremental and start being displacement. That is the mechanism by which this layer's margin, not its revenue, breaks first.

9.Open questions

  • What is NVIDIA's actual unit share, and of what? The corpus has revenue and margin, not share. Broadcom at $16.0bn guided [f] against NVIDIA at $75.2bn of quarterly data center revenue [s] is a revenue ratio, not a socket ratio, and the two differ because ASICs are cheaper per unit.
  • Does the additive story survive the packaging queue clearing? The falsifiable test is NVIDIA's data center growth rate in the quarter after CoWoS lead times fall below a year [e].
  • What are the captive parts actually costing their owners? Google and Amazon disclose specs, not cost per delivered FLOP. Without that, "TPUs are cheaper" is an assertion.
  • Does AMD ever close the margin gap? Twenty points of structural difference [s][s] has not narrowed in the strongest demand environment either company will ever see.
  • Was Groq the last independent architecture bet? [s] If a $17bn licensing cheque is the exit path, the next challenger's investors already know their base case.

◆The verdict

evidence-led verdict · updated 2026-07-25

Fairly priced — A 74.9% gross margin held through a product transition is scarcity rent set by packaging supply, not pricing power over customers. The risk here is not the economics — it is who is paying.
