Foundation Models

Frontier labs, open weights, training economics

Two lines define this layer, and they run in opposite directions.

The cost of a frontier training run grew 2.4x per year from 2016 through 2024 [s]. The price of a fixed level of capability fell about 10x per year from 2021 through 2024 [e]. Everything else on this page — the valuations, the burn schedules, the open-weight price shocks, one lab renting GPUs from a rival's parent company — follows from those two lines. You must spend more each year to stay at the frontier, and what you sell gets cheaper while you do it.

1.What this layer is

A foundation model is a file. The layer is the industrial process that produces one.

The physical anchor: DeepSeek-V3's full training run consumed 2.788 million H800 GPU-hours across 14.8 trillion tokens [s]. That is a warehouse of accelerators running for weeks to produce a single artifact of a few hundred gigabytes, which is then copied for free.

Where the money goes inside that run is not where most people assume. For the GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra generation, frontier development cost split 47–67 percent hardware, 29–49 percent R&D staff, and only 2–6 percent energy [e]. Compute and payroll are the business. Electricity, at this layer, is a rounding error — the power problem lives three layers down and arrives here as a capacity constraint, not a cost line.

The overtake is real. The margin is not what circulated. Anthropic's run-rate revenue crossed $47bn in May 2026 [s]. Sacra estimates OpenAI at approximately $33bn in the same month [e]. That is roughly 1.4x, not the 1.9x that comes from setting May against OpenAI's $25bn February figure [e]. And one carve-out travels with every OpenAI number here: Sacra states these annualized figures exclude Microsoft licensing revenue and large one-time deals, focusing on the core subscription and API business. Anthropic's $47bn carries no such exclusion. The two are not like-for-like, and the honest statement of the lead is "ahead on comparable-date estimates, by less than half again, on definitions that do not match."

Two more qualifiers on the $47bn itself. It is company-stated and unaudited. And it is a run-rate — Anthropic announced $30bn in April 2026 [s] and $47bn [s] seven weeks later, which annualizes a short window in which one large enterprise contract distorts the snapshot.

Margins are going the wrong way. OpenAI's gross margin was approximately 33 percent for FY2025 [e] — down from 40 percent the prior year, against an internal target of 46 percent [e]. The direction and the miss are the story, not the level: a company that planned for 46 and delivered 33 is losing the margin race against its own forecast, while growing revenue as fast as anyone ever has. The cost driver is inference: $8.4bn in 2025 [e], a figure that originates in leaked-document reporting and reaches the corpus through a single relay, not two independent measurements.

forecast · OpenAI internal projections, via The Information and SacraInference cost is projected at $14.1bn for 2026 [e]. Cash burn is projected at approximately $27bn in 2026 and $63bn in 2027, with cash-flow positive not expected until 2030 [f]. The forecast was revised sharply upward in a single pass — the revision is a better fragility signal than any single year in the series.

3.The players

Anthropic. $47bn run-rate [s], a $65bn Series H at a $965bn post-money valuation in May 2026 [s], and more than 500 companies each spending over $1m a year [s]. Revenue mix is the distinguishing feature: Sacra estimates 70–75 percent of it comes from pay-per-token API calls [e]. Never restate this as "80 percent from API" — the ~80 percent figure belongs to a different cut, revenue from more than 300,000 business customers [s], and the circulating claim looks like a corruption of it.

OpenAI. A $122bn round at an $852bn post-money valuation in March 2026 [s], corroborated by four independent organisations plus OpenAI itself. The fragility is inside the round rather than beside it. Amazon committed $50bn of it, of which $35bn is contingent on an initial public offering or on reaching artificial general intelligence [s]. Read that twice. Roughly a third of the largest private round ever raised is conditional on an event that has no agreed definition, no test, and no date — which means the headline valuation and the money actually available are two different numbers. Distribution is the asset — ChatGPT exceeded 1 billion monthly active app users in May 2026 and OpenAI reported more than 9 million paying business users in February 2026 [e]. The first of those is a Sensor Tower third-party estimate relayed by Reuters and CNBC, not an OpenAI disclosure; the second is best read as paying business seats.

xAI, now inside SpaceX. Revenue of $107m with a $1.46bn net loss for the quarter ended 30 September 2025 [s] — Bloomberg's read of investor documents, and every subsequent outlet traces to that one origin. A $20bn Series E at a $230bn valuation followed in January 2026 [s], then SpaceX acquired xAI in an all-stock deal on 2 February 2026 at a $250bn xAI standalone valuation [s]. Put the quarter and the valuation on the same line and the multiple speaks for itself.

DeepSeek and the open-weight frontier. V4 shipped on 24 April 2026 with V4-Pro output at $0.87 per million tokens [s]. Its V3.2 paper discloses no absolute training compute, no GPU count and no dollar cost [s] — every widely repeated DeepSeek training-cost figure comes from somewhere other than DeepSeek. Alibaba's Qwen anchors the largest model ecosystem on Hugging Face, with over 100,000 derivatives [s], carrying the source's own warning that trackers counting finetunes as distinct models inflate such totals.

4.Concentration & chokepoints

The chokepoint is compute access, and in 2026 it started crossing competitive lines.

Anthropic contracted with SpaceX — not xAI — for access to 220,000 GPUs and more than 300 MW of compute across Colossus 1 and 2, as of May 2026 [s]. Both corrections matter: the lessor is the acquiring parent, and the lease covers both facilities.

The mechanism is the interesting part, and it is a utilization number. The agreement was reported at $1.25bn per month through 2029, struck after Colossus 1 fell to about 11 percent GPU utilization when xAI moved training onto Colossus 2 [e]. Treat those terms as reported rather than filed — press attributes them to SpaceX's IPO filing, and the corpus types them as an estimate pending a check against the filing itself. But the shape is clear: an almost-idle cluster is worth more rented to a direct competitor than held empty. Rival-to-rival compute leasing is new, and it says something specific about this layer — at a certain scale, stranded accelerators are a worse problem than a strengthened rival.

Anthropic also disclosed agreements for up to 5 GW of new Amazon capacity plus 5 GW of next-generation TPU capacity with Google and Broadcom [s]. Read the "up to." That is a company claim about future capacity, not energized load, and the queue that decides whether it arrives is in the grid layer, not this one.

The concentration that follows is unusual: three or four entities can plausibly assemble ten-gigawatt compute programmes, and they are the same entities buying data, renting clouds, and designing accelerators. The chokepoint is not a component. It is balance-sheet access.

5.Lead times & capacity

Training runs are getting longer and more expensive on a schedule.

forecast · Epoch AIThe largest training runs are projected to cost more than $1bn by 2027 [f] on an amortized hardware-and-energy basis. Runs of 2e29 FLOP are likely feasible by 2030, with power as the first binding constraint [f] — orders of magnitude above the GPT-4 generation, and beyond what a single campus can energize, which is why the largest projected runs assume distribution across sites.

Two other queues are shortening. Reasoning is the new place compute goes: OpenAI scaled reasoning training compute 10x from o1 to o3 in four months [s], and Epoch expects reasoning training to reach the total-compute frontier not long after. And data binds eventually — roughly 500 trillion text tokens on the indexed web, with the text-only constraint arriving about five years from 2024 at 4x compute growth [e].

Meanwhile the cheap end proves how little compute a capability can need once the recipe is known. DeepSeek-R1's reinforcement-learning phase cost approximately $1m in hardware time, about 20 percent of its base model's pretraining cost [e]. That is the number that should worry anyone underwriting a $121bn compute programme [f].

6.2019 → today

2019. Training a language model is a research expense. The largest runs cost single-digit millions, data is free to scrape, and no one outside a lab has an opinion about GPU-hours.

2022–23. GPT-4 arrives and the layer becomes an industry. Hyperscaler capex quadrupled between GPT-4's March 2023 release and late 2025 [e], and leading labs began showing more revenue per employee than major public tech companies.

2025–26. Three regime changes at once. Reasoning becomes the compute sink, at 10x per model generation [s]. Open weights become a pricing force — DeepSeek-R1 launched at $2.20 per million output tokens against $60 for OpenAI o1 [s], the canonical margin-compression event. And revenue arrives in quantities nobody modelled: $47bn [s] and ~$33bn [e] run-rates at two companies that had almost no revenue three years earlier.

7.Commoditization pressure

Capability commoditizes; the frontier does not. The mechanism is a ladder, not a price cut.

Per-capability deflation runs at roughly 10x a year [e], measured by tracking the cheapest model that still clears a fixed benchmark. But the premium tier barely moved. o1 sold output at $60 per million tokens in January 2025 [s]; today's flagships sit at $25 [s] and $30 [s] per million output tokens, with a new and more expensive tier stacked above them. Labs are not defending a price point. They are adding rungs at the top and letting the old ones collapse.

The open-weight gap is the number to watch, because it sets how fast the collapse propagates. The best open-weights model scored 51 against 61 for the leading closed model on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index in July 2026 [s], on an index where the majority of evaluated models are now open weights. Chinese models trail leading US releases by 7 months on average since 2023, with a range of 4 to 14 [e], and most of the leading Chinese models are open-weight while US frontier models are closed. That combination — a months-long lag, released openly — is what turns a capability into a commodity on a predictable clock.

8.Failure modes

The scissors close. Training costs compound at 2.4x a year [s] while per-capability prices fall 10x [e]. A lab must grow revenue faster than its own input costs, forever, on 33 percent gross margins [e]. Blast radius: everything downstream that assumed cheap tokens.

The burn schedule slips. This is the layer's counterparty risk, and it lands on the cloud layer first: OpenAI is the largest single name in several other companies' backlogs.

forecast · OpenAI internal projections, via The Information$121bn of compute spend and an $85bn loss in 2028 [f], with cash-flow positive not expected before 2030 [f]. The originating reporting is paywalled and the accessible corroboration is secondary.

A strategy divergence resolves badly. Two companies selling into the same demand curve appear to disagree by a factor of four on the cost of staying at the frontier. One of them is wrong, and the answer is worth hundreds of billions.

forecast · secondary reporting on Anthropic's projections — single source, directional onlyAnthropic's training costs are projected to peak near $30bn through 2028, roughly 4x below OpenAI's projected $121bn 2028 compute spend [f]. Single secondary source. Treat the ratio as a direction, not a measurement.

An open-weight price shock. R1 cut the price of o1-class reasoning by more than an order of magnitude [s] and its RL phase cost about $1m in hardware time [e]. The next such event does not require a new frontier — only a good recipe and cheap GPU-hours.

Rival dependency. Renting 220,000 GPUs from a competitor's parent [s] at a reported $1.25bn a month through 2029 [e] is efficient until it is renegotiated — and the counterparty is the one company with a structural interest in the tenant's capacity being scarce.

Conditional capital. $35bn of a $50bn anchor commitment turns on an IPO or on reaching AGI [s]. A funding round is only as good as its non-contingent tranche, and this one's contingency has no test attached.

9.Open questions

  • Does the overtake survive like-for-like accounting? $47bn [s] against ~$33bn [e] is roughly 1.4x on definitions that do not match, because the OpenAI figure excludes Microsoft licensing and one-time deals. No public dataset reconciles them, and until one does the ranking is an estimate, not a fact.
  • What does a seven-week run-rate mean? $30bn in April [s] to $47bn in May [s] is both endpoints company-stated and separately reported, so there is no source conflict — but annualizing a short window in which one large contract lands is not the same as a year of revenue.
  • Which binds first, power or data? Epoch names power as the first constraint on 2e29 FLOP runs [f] and separately dates the text-data wall about five years from 2024 [e]. Those two forecasts have not been reconciled against each other.
  • Is the 10-point open-weight gap stable? [s] It is one reading on one index in one month. The entire commoditization timeline hangs on whether that gap is widening or closing.
  • What do frontier runs actually cost? The best-documented run in the corpus is DeepSeek's, and DeepSeek's own V3.2 paper discloses no training cost at all [s]. Every US frontier figure is an estimate from the outside.
