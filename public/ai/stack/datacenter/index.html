layer 03 of 16 · field guide

Datacenter Physical

Shells, land, water, cooling, DC REITs, construction

The scarcity on this layer is borrowed. Land is abundant, concrete is available, and the cost of building a shell is rising more slowly than it was two years ago. What is missing arrives from the two layers below: a connection date.

The clearest single number in the corpus makes the point. Of the more than 280 data center industry experts surveyed, 48% named power availability as the most prominent obstacle to delivering projects on schedule [s]. Not labour, not equipment, not capital.

1.What this layer is

A grey box, a substation, a chiller plant, and a lease.

Microsoft's Fairwater site in Mount Pleasant, Wisconsin is the physical anchor: three buildings, 1.2 million square feet under roof on a 315-acre site, built with 46.6 miles of foundation piles and 26.5 million pounds of structural steel [s]. Those are company figures, and the performance claims attached to them are marketing. But the mass is real, and it is the least interesting part of the project — the hard part was the megawatts.

The layer splits in two commercially. Wholesale and hyperscale landlords sell megawatts on long leases. Retail colocation sells racks and cross-connects. The AI cycle accrues almost entirely to the first.

Construction is priced per watt, not per square foot, which tells you what is being sold. Tokyo was the most expensive market in 2025 at $15.2 per watt, followed by Singapore at $14.5 and Zurich at $14.2 [s]. Cost per watt for a traditional air-cooled build rose 5.5% in 2025 — against 9.0% the prior year [s]. Construction inflation is decelerating. That is not what a supply-constrained input looks like.

Nor is the cooling transition expensive in the way the discourse implies. US liquid-cooled AI facilities cost 7 to 10% more to build than air-cooled facilities of equivalent IT capacity [e] — a consultancy midpoint on an unpublished method, and a single-digit premium either way.

The returns tell the real story. Digital Realty had 1,402 MW under construction at 100% share as of June 2026, 54% pre-leased, with $20.2bn of expected total investment at an 11.5% average expected stabilized yield [s]. Renewal leases signed in Q2 2026 re-priced 25.4% higher on a cash basis [s]. You do not get double-digit development yields and double-digit renewal spreads in the same quarter in a market with spare capacity.

3.The players

Digital Realty is the megawatt seller. Q2 2026 revenue of $1.9bn, up 29% year over year [s] — a figure flattered by net promote income from a development joint venture and an insurance settlement, both of which the company discloses separately. Its backlog of signed-but-not-commenced leases reached a record $1.9bn of annualized GAAP base rent [s], and in July 2026 it signed two hyperscale leases alone worth $410m of annualized base rent — more than its entire Q2 bookings [s].

Equinix is the control group, and it is the strongest counterargument on this page. Q1 2026 revenue of $2.444bn grew 10%, with full-year guidance of $10.144–10.244bn [s] and 2026 capex guided to about $4.1bn [s]. Ten percent against Digital Realty's twenty-nine. Equinix says roughly 60% of its largest Q1 2026 deals were AI-related [s] — its own characterisation, with "largest deals" and "AI-related" undefined, so treat it as a claim rather than a measurement.

Vertiv is where the layer's demand shows up first, because power and cooling equipment is ordered before the shell is finished. FY2025 net sales of $10.2bn against $8.01bn in 2024 [s], and a backlog that went from $7.2bn to $15.0bn in twelve months [s] — roughly one and a half times annual revenue, with its own 10-K stating that orders are being placed in advance of its ability to fulfil them. Q1 2026 net sales were $2,650m, up 30%, at a 20.8% adjusted operating margin [s] — margin expanding inclusive of tariff impacts, which is pricing power in plain sight.

4.Concentration & chokepoints

The chokepoint is not land. Digital Realty alone held land supporting 6,560 MW plus shells supporting 550 MW of future development capacity at 100% share [s], against 3.1 GW of operating IT capacity and about 8.5 GW buildable [s]. One landlord is sitting on more than twice its operating fleet in undeveloped potential.

The chokepoint is the interconnection date behind each of those megawatts — the layer below — and, increasingly, permission. Data Center Watch counted 75 US projects worth about $130bn blocked or delayed in Q1 2026, which it describes as the largest single-quarter concentration in its own dataset [e]. That is an advocacy-adjacent tracker counting with an unpublished method, and the superlative is its own — but the direction is corroborated by the count of active opposition groups, which more than doubled in the same window.

5.Lead times & capacity

The equipment order book is the leading indicator, and it is already showing what happens when demand doubles: Vertiv's backlog doubled in a year [s] and its 10-K says customers are ordering ahead of what it can build. A shell can be poured in eighteen months. A transformer, a switchgear set and a chiller plant cannot be conjured, and a substation energisation date is not for sale.

Density is the other capacity question, and here the roadmap and the observation must be kept apart.

This is a projection about what manufacturers are planning, not an observation of racks in service — the corpus previously recorded it as present tense and the fact-check retyped it.

The announcement is fact; the claimed efficiency and copper savings are NVIDIA's own. Its engineering analysis puts a 1 MW rack on legacy 54 V distribution at up to 200 kg of copper busbar [e] — a vendor estimate, and the reason the re-architecture exists.

6.2019 → today

2019. The unit of sale is the square foot and the kilowatt is an afterthought. Cooling is air. Power usage effectiveness is the efficiency metric anyone cares about, and Google is already winning it.

2022–24. AI racks arrive but are still air-cooled, rated at about 41 kW [e] — Uptime Institute research whose exact article the corpus has not pinned down, so treat it as the best available anchor rather than a hard one. Build cost inflation runs at 9.0% a year [s]. Immersion cooling looks like the future.

2026. Rack power draw during AI training swings by more than 100% within milliseconds, with GB200 NVL72 racks observed climbing from roughly 60–70 kW to more than 150 kW against a 132 kW specification [s]. The spikes recur every second or two. The honest before-and-after is 41 kW to 150 kW [e][s] — roughly a factor of four in two hardware generations, not the ninefold jump the corpus briefly recorded by mistaking a fluctuation for a level. Google reports a fleet-wide PUE of 1.09 against a surveyed industry average of 1.54 [s] — company-reported, and the industry comparison is a survey Google cites rather than an independent audit.

And immersion cooling lost. LiquidStack, the immersion pioneer, announced a 300 MW direct-to-chip CDU order in January 2026 [s] — a company claim with the customer undisclosed — and Trane Technologies completed its acquisition of the company two months later. The technology pivot and the exit happened in the same quarter.

7.Commoditization pressure

Shells are commoditizing. Megawatts are not.

The evidence for the first half is the cost curve: escalation halving from 9.0% to 5.5% [s], a liquid-cooling premium in single digits [e], and land banks measured in gigawatts [s]. The evidence for the second is the rent: 25.4% renewal spreads [s] and 11.5% stabilized development yields [s].

The historical analogue is warehousing after e-commerce. The building became a commodity; the location with a highway interchange did not. Here the interchange is a substation.

The strongest attributed argument that the density transition is slower than it looks: Uptime Institute's Jacqueline Davis argued in April 2026 that direct liquid cooling will remain confined to high-density AI and HPC applications and will not reach mainstream mission-critical adoption within three years [o], citing redundant CDU and manifold engineering, reduced ride-through time for generator start, and unresolved ownership of DLC components between facilities and IT teams.

8.Failure modes

Power quality, not power quantity. Racks that swing more than 100% within milliseconds and overshoot their specification [s] are a facility engineering problem before they are a grid problem — and they are already a grid problem one layer down. Blast radius: the electrical design of every facility built to a pre-2025 spec.

Permission. $130bn of projects blocked or delayed in a single quarter [e] is the failure mode that no amount of capital fixes, and it lands on the layer that has to site the building.

Shells without demand. The counter-example exists, offshore: data centre utilisation in China's subsidised western AI hubs was reported at 20 to 30%, in regions offering power-bill cuts of up to 50% [e]. That figure has no published counting method anywhere in its source chain — no definition of utilisation, no sample, no facility count — so it is a caveat, not a finding, and this page will not build an argument on it.

Stranded density. If Davis is right and liquid cooling stays niche [o], the retrofit market that the equipment backlogs assume does not arrive on schedule.

9.Open questions

  • Why is Equinix growing at 10% and Digital Realty at 29%? [s][s] If the answer is purely product mix, this layer is only short of hyperscale megawatts. If retail colocation is also being crowded out of power, the shortage is wider than the rent data shows.
  • How firm is the 41 kW baseline? It now anchors every before/after density comparison on this site [e], and it rests on Uptime Institute research whose exact article the corpus has not located. The figure is almost certainly right; the citation is not yet publication-grade.
  • Does 200 kW happen, and when? It is a roadmap projection [f] and NVIDIA's 800 V answer does not ship until 2027 [s]. Everything built before then is built to a lower ceiling.
  • How much of the pre-leasing is the same demand counted twice? Digital Realty's 54% pre-leased construction [s] and Vertiv's $15.0bn backlog [s] may describe the same buildings from two sides. The corpus cannot net them.
  • Is the opposition count a trend or a spike? One quarter of an advocacy tracker's own data [e] is not a series.

◆The verdict

Underbuilt — Digital Realty banks land for 6,560 MW it cannot energise while re-pricing renewals up 25.4%. The scarce input here is not space or steel — it is a megawatt with a connection date.
